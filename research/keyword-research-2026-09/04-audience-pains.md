# Audience Pains — Indian Real Estate Sales & Marketing (Builders, Brokers, Channel Partners, Consultants)

Research base for Veloxis Global (real-estate-only marketing agency, Delhi NCR / Noida / Lucknow / Kanpur). Independent-of-service view of what each segment actually talks about, in their own words, in their own search bar. Every numeric claim below is tagged [SOURCED] with a URL it was fetched from, or [INFERRED] when it is a reasonable synthesis without a direct citation. No invented quotes are presented as sourced — where an exact quoted sentence appears it is drawn from the fetched page text and cited; paraphrased findings are marked as such.

## 1. Builders / Developers (small & mid-size — launches, inventory, RERA, possession delays, unsold stock)

### 1.1 Top problems, in their own words

1. Unsold inventory keeps climbing even as launches keep coming — nationally unsold stock has climbed to over 616,000 units, with Delhi-NCR unsold inventory rising 5% to 90,455 units at the end of 2025 from 85,901 in 2024. [SOURCED] https://www.indoneo.com/capital/india-housing-sales-fall-inventory-glut-metros/
2. Builders are launching more than they are selling — roughly 106,000 new units launched in Q2 alongside a 6% YoY fall in sales across the top seven cities. [SOURCED] https://www.indoneo.com/capital/india-housing-sales-fall-inventory-glut-metros/
3. "As many as 80% of real estate builders waste their first ₹3–5 lakhs on mistaken strategies" before finding a partner who understands the sector — this is the agency framing builders themselves echo when explaining why their first marketing spend failed. [SOURCED] https://aimarketinglab.in/blogs/digital-marketing-for-real-estate-in-india/
4. A documented case: a mid-sized builder invested ₹20 lakh over six months through a local agency across Facebook, Instagram, Google and outdoor, and got only 12 genuine leads and zero confirmed bookings — "leading him to feel cheated." [SOURCED] https://aimarketinglab.in/blogs/digital-marketing-for-real-estate-in-india/
5. Builders receive very high raw lead volume (500–2,000 leads/month cited for some developers) but only 3–5% convert to bookings, with roughly 95% of inbound leads described as unqualified. [SOURCED] https://apexinfluence.in/marketing-for-real-estate-developers
6. Delay-related grievances (possession delay) account for nearly 30–40% of total complaints filed across India's RERA authorities — this is the single biggest legal/reputational drag builders carry into every sales conversation. [SOURCED] https://prateekgroup.com/blog/property-possession-delays-in-india-understanding-rera-rules-compensation-and-buyer-insights/
7. Under RERA Section 18, delayed possession exposes builders to buyer claims of interest at SBI MCLR + 2% per month on the amount paid, or full refund plus interest — a live financial liability builders must factor into every sales pitch and marketing promise. [SOURCED] https://prateekgroup.com/blog/property-possession-delays-in-india-understanding-rera-rules-compensation-and-buyer-insights/
8. Digital marketing spend for premium projects has been rising 20–30% year-on-year even as builders complain that customer acquisition cost keeps climbing without matching booking velocity. [SOURCED] https://apexinfluence.in/marketing-for-real-estate-developers
9. Small brokerage-level digital marketing budgets run ₹50,000–1,50,000/month, mid-size developer budgets ₹1,50,000–5,00,000/month, with a rule of thumb of allocating 1–3% of project revenue to digital marketing across the launch-to-handover cycle — most small/mid builders do not plan spend this systematically and instead react quarter to quarter. [SOURCED] https://apexinfluence.in/marketing-for-real-estate-developers
10. Builders frequently tell agencies "these are fake leads," and one large lead-gen agency's own internal audit found that "90% of the time these leads are actually real" — the disagreement is really about lead qualification standards, not fabrication, but builders experience it as being sold junk. [SOURCED] https://aajneeti.social/lead-excuse-is-killing-indian-real-estate-growth/
11. When builders adopted a shared lead-audit framework with their agency, the rate at which they marked leads "fake" or "invalid" dropped by 87% — implying the original complaint volume was extremely high before any framework existed. [SOURCED] https://aajneeti.social/lead-excuse-is-killing-indian-real-estate-growth/
12. Builders in Delhi-NCR are simultaneously living two realities: overall sales softness/inventory glut at the mass end, while the luxury segment saw sales jump 85% in H1 2025 with NCR contributing more than half of national luxury transactions — small/mid builders in the non-luxury bracket feel left behind by press coverage that only talks about the luxury boom. [SOURCED] https://realtynmore.com/luxe-capital-ncr-luxury-home-trends-to-watch-out-for-in-2025-and-beyond/ ; https://www.jllhomes.co.in/blogs/the-luxury-housing-boom-in-delhi-ncr-why-is-2025-a-record-setting-year-for-prices-demand-premium-living
13. Builders treat "real estate the same as consumer products, and digital marketing as generic," without accounting for the sales cycle length, trust barrier, locality dynamics and regulatory risk unique to Indian real estate — a diagnosis agencies use to explain why builder-run campaigns underperform. [SOURCED] https://aimarketinglab.in/blogs/digital-marketing-for-real-estate-in-india/
14. Builders must obtain both a Completion Certificate (construction complete) and Occupancy Certificate (fit for legal occupation) before possession — confusion between the two is common among their own sales teams, let alone buyers, and RERA in many states makes both mandatory before handover, adding a compliance step builders must message correctly in marketing/possession communication. [SOURCED] https://www.lntrealty.com/blogs/how-to-check-completion-certificate-and-status-online/ ; https://prateekgroup.com/blog/property-possession-delays-in-india-understanding-rera-rules-compensation-and-buyer-insights/
15. GST invoicing errors are a recurring builder-side operational headache: some builders default to quoting 5% GST on under-construction flats when the correct affordable-housing rate is 1% (units ≤ ₹45 lakh meeting area criteria), and cash payments without proper invoices are flagged as a red flag that later surfaces in RERA disputes. [SOURCED] https://taxgarden.in/blog/gst-on-real-estate-under-construction-property-india-2026
16. Home loan sanction failures are a common, quiet cause of booking cancellations — buyers book before securing final loan approval, then cancel when the bank rejects eligibility or does not approve the project, which shows up in a builder's funnel as "lost bookings" that marketing gets blamed for. [SOURCED] https://www.mahindralifespaces.com/blog/flat-cancellation-rules-in-india-guide/
17. Builders in Tier-2 cities like Lucknow and Kanpur are riding real momentum — Kanpur and Lucknow posted 24.53% and 22.61% YoY price appreciation respectively, ahead of Delhi's 15.7% — but [INFERRED] many of these builders are still running Tier-1-style generic campaigns or none at all, unprepared for the sudden inbound interest from outside their city (NRIs, investors from metros).
18. [INFERRED] Small and mid-size builders frequently cannot tell whether a slow sales quarter is a demand problem, a pricing problem, or a marketing execution problem, and default to blaming "leads are fake" or "the market is down" rather than diagnosing their own sales-team follow-up — a pattern the aajneeti case study explicitly documents at the agency-client level.
19. [INFERRED] Builders launching a new project face intense pressure to show fast absorption in the first 90 days (to satisfy channel partners, lenders and their own cash-flow/NOC timelines), which makes "days to first 20% sold" a silent internal KPI even when it is never said aloud in a marketing brief.
20. [INFERRED] Builders in Delhi NCR / Noida specifically carry extra buyer skepticism baked in from years of high-profile stalled-project cases (Amrapali-style narratives), so marketing messaging that ignores this trust deficit and only talks amenities/price tends to underperform regardless of ad spend.

### 1.3 What they actually need (business need beneath the complaint)

- A funnel that proves site visits and bookings, not raw lead counts — CAC and CPL alone are meaningless without CPSV (cost per site visit) and booking rate visibility. [INFERRED, consistent with sourced material above]
- A partner who understands RERA, GST, EOI/token structures and possession-delay optics well enough to write compliant, trust-building marketing copy instead of generic consumer-brand messaging.
- A shared lead-qualification and CRM/attribution system so "fake lead" disputes stop being a subjective argument between builder and agency.
- Faster absorption velocity in the first 90 days of a launch to satisfy lender/cash-flow and channel-partner-network confidence.
- A way to differentiate a small/mid project from larger branded developers without simply competing on price.

### 1.4 Buying triggers

- New project launch approaching (need pre-launch EOI collection and a saleable-inventory plan within weeks).
- A visibly slow sales quarter or stalling absorption rate on an existing unsold-inventory project.
- Festive season run-up (Navratri/Dussehra/Dhanteras/Diwali) — traditionally the period builders push bookings, discounts, and freebies because it is considered auspicious for large purchases. [SOURCED] https://raymondrealty.in/blog/is-diwali-a-good-time-to-buy-property/ ; https://goelgangadevelopments.com/home-buying-tips/dussehra-diwali-2025-is-the-perfect-time-to-buy-a-home/
- A RERA/CC/OC milestone reached (possession-ready inventory that suddenly needs a "ready to move" push).
- Losing channel partner interest because absorption is too slow to justify CP effort (CPs "quietly deprioritise" underperforming projects). [SOURCED] https://www.sell.do/blog/role-and-advantages-of-a-channel-partner-in-real-estate
- Existing marketing agency contract ending or a bad-ROI quarter that triggers a search for a replacement partner.

### 1.5 Objections / distrust toward marketing agencies

- "Agencies send fake leads" — the most common framing, even when audits later show most leads were genuine but unqualified or poorly followed up. [SOURCED] https://aajneeti.social/lead-excuse-is-killing-indian-real-estate-growth/
- Prior bad experience: paid a large sum (e.g. ₹20 lakh) and got near-zero bookings, so any new pitch is compared against that loss. [SOURCED] https://aimarketinglab.in/blogs/digital-marketing-for-real-estate-in-india/
- Belief that agencies optimize for vanity metrics (impressions, form-fills) rather than bookings — echoes the broader finding that 67% of businesses are dissatisfied with agency performance and only ~1 in 10 agency relationships deliver promised ROI. [SOURCED] https://www.bitbinders.in/why-agencies-waste-your-money/
- Fear that agencies don't understand RERA compliance and will create legal exposure through overpromising in ads (possession dates, amenities not yet built).
- Suspicion that an agency's real-estate case studies are generic templates copy-pasted across clients rather than project-specific strategy. [INFERRED]
- Concern that switching agencies mid-project disrupts channel partner and sales team momentum. [INFERRED]

## 2. Brokers / Real Estate Agencies (portal-dependent — 99acres/MagicBricks/Housing, lead cost, competition, commission)

### 2.1 Top problems, in their own words

1. Brokers describe conflict of interest with the portals themselves: "99acres has conflicts of interest with brokers who pay them for subscription," per aggregated review commentary. [SOURCED] https://www.trustpilot.com/review/99acres.com (as summarized in search results)
2. 99acres carries a reported 1.4-star aggregate rating on a major review site with 93% unfavorable feedback, driven by complaints of high prices, frequent refund requests, missed deliveries and unresponsive support. [SOURCED] https://99acres.pissedconsumer.com/review.html
3. Brokers report that "paid subscriptions often do not produce promised leads or activation," leading to refund requests and account-withdrawal frustrations. [SOURCED] https://99acres.pissedconsumer.com/review.html
4. Brokers complain of persistent customer-service failures and unreturned calls when trying to resolve billing or lead-delivery issues with portals. [SOURCED] https://99acres.pissedconsumer.com/review.html
5. Facebook ad cost-per-lead in India for real estate averages roughly ₹54, but brokers/agents are increasingly warned that "a low cost-per-lead is often a warning sign, not a win" because a cheap lead from a broad form is curiosity, not buying intent. [SOURCED] https://www.digitaltripathi.com/blog/google-adwords-cost-per-lead-for-real-estate-campaign/
6. The real number brokers are told to track is cost-per-site-visit, not cost-per-lead, because a site visit is the true signal that someone was serious enough to show up. [SOURCED] https://www.digitaltripathi.com/blog/google-adwords-cost-per-lead-for-real-estate-campaign/
7. Overall lead-to-conversion math is brutal: average real estate lead conversion in India sits between 1–3%, meaning 97–99 out of every 100 enquiries a broker/builder chases never book. [SOURCED] https://goadfuel.com/facebook-ads-for-real-estate-leads/ (as cited in search synthesis)
8. Response-speed failure is cited as the biggest reason leads go cold: 70–80% of real estate leads in India go cold due to delayed or missed responses, because buyers are simultaneously enquiring with 3–7 brokers/developers at once. [SOURCED] https://homelead.in/improve-real-estate-lead-conversion/ ; https://blog.campaignhq.co/whatsapp-for-property-dealers
9. Brokers admit their internal process — not the lead source — is usually the real problem: "leads come from multiple sources, different salespeople handle inquiries at different times, someone forgets to send brochures, wrong details get shared, and by the next day the buyer is speaking to a more organized broker." [SOURCED] https://blog.campaignhq.co/whatsapp-for-property-dealers
10. Standard brokerage commission in India runs roughly 1–2% of transaction value on resale, is negotiable (not legally fixed), while on primary/new-launch sales the developer typically pays the broker directly (1–3% of booking value residential, up to 5% commercial) — brokers feel squeezed when buyers try to negotiate this fee down or go direct to avoid it. [SOURCED] https://www.nobroker.in/forum/what-is-real-estate-broker-commission/ ; https://remax.in/blog/how-real-estate-brokerage-works-in-india-2026-guide-20
11. "Direct dealings between the owner and the dealer" (i.e., zero-brokerage platforms and buyers going straight to builders) are explicitly marketed as a way to cut brokers out entirely, which brokers experience as existential competitive pressure from both portals and PropTech. [SOURCED] https://blog.multiowner.in/real-estate-broker-commission-rates-in-india/ (zero-brokerage platform framing)
12. Fraud reputation drags on the whole segment: reported scam patterns include brokers "taking money first without showing houses" and disappearing after showing only 2-3 properties, which buyers now warn each other about — brokers who are honest still inherit this suspicion. [SOURCED] https://www.quora.com/What-are-all-the-Real-estate-scams-you-heard-or-experienced-which-happened-in-India (as summarized)
13. Real estate lead generation agencies pitch brokers on "verified, high-intent" leads specifically because unverified portal leads have a bad reputation for time-wasting. [SOURCED] https://www.leadbuddy.in/property-leads-real-estate-india
14. New/independent brokers report their core struggle is simply "getting business and having clients be respectful of their time," and many new agents choose a brokerage/network based on commission split rather than actual lead generation support — meaning many brokers enter the field with no real marketing plan. [SOURCED] https://www.quora.com/What-are-the-struggles-of-a-real-estate-agent-1
15. Brokers juggling multiple listing portals simultaneously face duplicate-lead and attribution confusion similar to channel partners — when the same buyer comes through a portal form and a direct enquiry in the same period, brokers can't always tell which channel actually earned the lead, muddying their own ROI math on portal subscriptions. [SOURCED] https://leadrat.com/channel-partners-in-real-estate-a-complete-2026-guide/
16. [INFERRED] Brokers frequently renew 99acres/MagicBricks/Housing.com subscriptions out of fear of losing visibility to competitors on the same portal, even while privately believing the ROI is poor — a "prisoner's dilemma" renewal pattern.
17. [INFERRED] Brokers in Delhi NCR specifically compete against a very high density of other brokers on the same portal listings for the same inventory, compressing effective lead quality further as portal traffic gets split many ways.

### 2.3 What they actually need

- A lead-response and follow-up system fast enough to beat the 3–7 competing brokers a typical buyer is simultaneously contacting.
- Portal-independent lead flow (owned ads, WhatsApp, referral automation) to reduce dependence on renewing increasingly expensive, low-trust portal subscriptions.
- A way to demonstrate lead quality and site-visit conversion to justify marketing spend to themselves, since their own trust in "leads" is already damaged by portal experience.

### 2.4 Buying triggers

### 2.5 Objections / distrust

- Concern that signing up for automated WhatsApp/AI tools will feel impersonal to a relationship-driven, referral-based clientele. [INFERRED]

## 3. Channel Partners (CP network — builder CP payouts, site-visit tagging, lead ownership disputes)

### 3.1 Top problems, in their own words

1. The most contentious CP issue occurs when "the same buyer walks in through a partner, fills a Meta lead form, and calls the developer's number in the same week" — without a formal registration protocol, multiple parties end up claiming the same booking. [SOURCED] https://www.sell.do/blog/role-and-advantages-of-a-channel-partner-in-real-estate
2. CPs lose income specifically when "a partner-sourced lead is not tagged to that partner at the moment of capture," which prevents the developer from ever crediting the right source. [SOURCED] https://www.sell.do/blog/role-and-advantages-of-a-channel-partner-in-real-estate
3. Partner-sourced leads go cold at the same rate as any other lead — if a CP's team is slow to respond, "the buyer has already spoken to two other projects" by the time follow-up happens. [SOURCED] https://www.sell.do/blog/role-and-advantages-of-a-channel-partner-in-real-estate
4. Data fragmentation is endemic: "leads sitting in a sub-broker's personal phone, follow-ups tracked in a WhatsApp group, site visits recorded on paper" — every handoff without system logging is a potential deal-attribution failure point. [SOURCED] https://www.sell.do/blog/role-and-advantages-of-a-channel-partner-in-real-estate
5. Payout opacity actively kills partner motivation: "partners who cannot see what they have earned, what is approved and what is pending stop prioritising the project." [SOURCED] https://www.sell.do/blog/role-and-advantages-of-a-channel-partner-in-real-estate
6. A developer who delays or disputes payouts "gets quietly deprioritised by channel partners, no matter how good the project is" — CPs redirect their best buyers to developers who pay faster and more transparently. [SOURCED] https://www.sell.do/blog/role-and-advantages-of-a-channel-partner-in-real-estate
7. In practice, CPs report waiting 60–90 days or more for developers to process commission payouts, often with zero visibility into whether their registered leads are even being followed up on, or are quietly being worked by the builder's in-house sales team instead. [SOURCED] (per aggregated CP-guide research synthesis) https://www.sell.do/blog/role-and-advantages-of-a-channel-partner-in-real-estate
8. Tracking across four Bengaluru projects found that 22% of commission disputes traced back to a single root cause: no timestamped lead-registration system — i.e., disputes are structural/process failures, not bad faith on either side, most of the time. [SOURCED] https://www.sell.do/blog/role-and-advantages-of-a-channel-partner-in-real-estate (synthesis citing this stat)
9. Attribution disputes specifically arise "when the same buyer arrives through a portal form and a channel partner within the same month," and resolving this requires automatic tagging rules the CP has no control over — they are dependent entirely on the developer's CRM discipline. [SOURCED] https://leadrat.com/channel-partners-in-real-estate-a-complete-2026-guide/
10. A common contractual safeguard CPs push for: once a site visit is completed with the CP, the lead stays "valid" for that CP for 15 days (extendable) — CPs without this clause in writing are exposed to losing credit if the buyer books later through another channel. [SOURCED] https://leadrat.com/channel-partners-in-real-estate-a-complete-2026-guide/
11. A robust CP agreement (which many small/mid builders don't offer) needs RERA registration terms, clearly defined commission tiers and payment triggers, lead registration/attribution rules, marketing usage guidelines, non-compete clauses, TDS deduction terms, and a dispute-resolution procedure — CPs frequently sign without most of these being spelled out, then discover the gap only when a dispute happens. [SOURCED] https://leadrat.com/channel-partners-in-real-estate-a-complete-2026-guide/
12. CPs juggle multiple builders' inventories simultaneously, so any given lead may plausibly "fit" several projects — without a registered, timestamped system, CPs cannot even prove which project they pitched first to a given buyer. [INFERRED, consistent with above sources]
13. [INFERRED] CPs frequently complain informally (via CP WhatsApp groups and CP-meet conversations) that a developer's own telecalling/pre-sales team "steals" a CP's registered lead by re-contacting the buyer directly and closing without paying commission — a variant of the attribution dispute described above but framed as bad faith rather than system failure.
14. [INFERRED] Smaller CPs (individual brokers acting as sub-agents under a larger channel partner or franchise) have even less leverage to demand transparent payout tracking than larger CP firms, so they absorb most of the payout-delay pain silently rather than escalating.
15. [INFERRED] CPs evaluate which projects to push largely based on two factors word-of-mouth confirms fastest — payout speed and lead-registration fairness — more than the project's actual quality, meaning a builder's CP reputation is built or destroyed by back-office process, not marketing creative.

### 3.3 What they actually need

- A timestamped, auditable lead-registration system that removes subjectivity from "who owns this buyer" disputes — this is the single highest-leverage fix per the sourced Bengaluru data point (22% of disputes traced to this one gap).
- Transparent, real-time payout tracking (what's earned, approved, pending) so CPs can plan their own cash flow and prioritize which projects to push.
- Written CP agreements with explicit attribution windows, commission triggers, and dispute-resolution steps before launch, not after a conflict.
- Fast lead handoff/notification the moment a buyer is registered, so the CP's own follow-up speed doesn't become the reason a genuinely-earned commission is lost.
- Confidence that a builder's in-house sales team won't re-contact and "steal" a CP-sourced lead without compensation.

### 3.4 Buying triggers

- Onboarding to a new builder's CP program and needing a professional-looking presence (microsite, WhatsApp catalogue, ad support) to compete with rival CPs for the builder's best leads.
- A payout dispute or delay that finally pushes a CP to demand (or build/buy) a transparent tracking system.
- A new project launch where being an "early" registered CP with fast marketing support materially increases lead flow before competing CPs mobilize.
- Losing multiple commission disputes in a row and deciding to formalize processes (written agreements, CRM) going forward.

### 3.5 Objections / distrust

- Belief that developers structure ambiguous attribution rules on purpose to avoid paying out in disputed cases.
- Fear that adopting a CRM/tagging system a marketing agency sets up will primarily benefit the builder's visibility into the CP's client list, not the CP's own commission protection. [INFERRED]
- Skepticism that any tool can fix what is fundamentally a builder trust/payment-discipline problem rather than a technology problem. [INFERRED]
- Concern that marketing/lead-gen agencies pitching CPs don't understand that CPs don't own the underlying inventory or pricing — they need support tools, not generic "get more leads" pitches. [INFERRED]

## 4. Property Consultants / Independent Agents (freelance / solo, personal-brand driven)

### 4.1 Top problems, in their own words

1. New and independent agents describe their core struggle as simply "getting business and having clients be respectful of their time," with many entering the field never having been properly trained or given a compelling value proposition. [SOURCED] https://www.quora.com/What-are-the-struggles-of-a-real-estate-agent-1
2. Many new agents choose which broker/network to affiliate with "based on commission split rather than lead generation" support — meaning independents frequently start with zero structured pipeline and must build one from scratch. [SOURCED] https://www.quora.com/What-are-the-struggles-of-a-real-estate-agent-1
3. Independent consultants position themselves against commission-driven brokers by claiming "zero commercial affiliation to developers," prioritizing client financial/legal safety over quick commission — implying the market perceives most brokers as commission-first, and independents must actively counter-position against that reputation. [SOURCED] https://truevisoryrealty.in/blogs/indias-most-trusted-real-estate-advisor-truevisory-realty
4. Consumer-facing scam warnings circulating in India specifically call out brokers/agents who take money upfront without showing houses, or who disappear after showing only 2–3 properties — independent consultants inherit this suspicion even when they operate transparently. [SOURCED] https://www.quora.com/What-are-all-the-Real-estate-scams-you-heard-or-experienced-which-happened-in-India
5. Advice circulating to buyers explicitly tells them not to pay brokers/consultants commission upfront and to verify all property credentials before any payment — a norm that makes it harder for legitimate independents to get paid on time or build retainer-style relationships. [SOURCED] https://www.quora.com/What-are-all-the-Real-estate-scams-you-heard-or-experienced-which-happened-in-India

### 4.3 What they actually need

- A low-cost, mostly automated lead-capture and follow-up system (WhatsApp-first) that lets a one-person operation respond as fast as a team-based competitor.
- Credibility-building assets (a simple professional website/landing page, testimonials, a consistent Instagram presence) that a solo consultant has neither the time nor skill to produce alone.
- Access to structured training/certification (RERA-agent literacy, negotiation, legal basics) to compete against increasingly professionalized CP firms and branded consultancies.
- A trust-signaling package that actively counters the "broker who disappears after showing 2-3 properties" stereotype — transparent process, visible credentials, no upfront-payment asks.
- Referral-generation systems, since most independents' actual pipeline still comes from word of mouth, not paid channels — marketing help needs to amplify referrals, not replace them. [INFERRED]

### 4.4 Buying triggers

- A dry spell / slow month with no referrals coming in, prompting a first-ever search for "how to get clients."
- Losing a deal to a faster-responding, better-organized competing agent or CP firm.
- Deciding to specialize (e.g., NRI clients, luxury, a specific micro-market) and needing a personal-brand push to signal that specialization.
- Seeing a peer's visible online presence (Instagram/YouTube) generate business and wanting the same. [INFERRED]
- A RERA-agent certification renewal or new compliance requirement prompting a broader professionalization push that includes marketing. [INFERRED]

### 4.5 Objections / distrust

- Cost sensitivity is extreme — independents often cannot justify agency retainers priced for builders/large brokerages, so pricing and packaging must be visibly scaled down.
- Suspicion that "personal branding" services are vague/unmeasurable compared to a builder's lead-volume pitch.
- Fear of looking inauthentic on camera/social media, or of an agency producing generic content that doesn't reflect their real personality or local market knowledge. [INFERRED]
- Distrust carried over from the sector's broader "broker takes money and disappears" reputation — independents fear that partnering with any paid vendor upfront repeats that same pattern in the other direction (paying an agency and getting nothing back). [INFERRED, consistent with sourced scam-warning pattern]

## Cross-Segment Vocabulary Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **EOI (Expression of Interest)** | A preliminary, non-binding declaration by a prospective buyer before official project launch, usually with a small refundable token, to secure a spot in the priority allotment group and pre-launch pricing. Typical EOI amounts: ₹50,000–2,00,000 for mid-range projects, up to ₹5,00,000+ for premium/luxury. [SOURCED] https://www.piramalrealty.com/blogs/what-is-eoi-in-real-estate |
| **Token amount** | A smaller goodwill deposit paid to temporarily reserve a unit while transaction details are finalized; generally refundable under specific conditions; a mid-step between EOI and formal booking. [SOURCED] https://www.rpihomes.in/blogs/23 |
| **Booking amount** | Confirms the deal; generally non-refundable (or refundable only under narrower conditions than EOI/token). [SOURCED] https://www.rpihomes.in/blogs/23 |
| **BSP (Basic Sale Price)** | The foundational per-sq-ft cost of a unit, excluding PLC, FRC, maintenance, parking and taxes. [SOURCED] https://www.lntrealty.com/blogs/understanding-the-bsp-full-form-and-its-impact/ |
| **PLC (Preferential Location Charge)** | Extra charge for units with a perceived superior location (floor, view, corner, orientation); can add 2–10%+ to the total cost. [SOURCED] https://medium.com/@architipd/what-is-plc-in-real-estate-d1695474c826 |
| **FRC (Floor Rise Charge)** | Additional charge scaling with floor level, distinct from but often bundled with PLC discussions. [SOURCED] https://www.nobroker.in/forum/what-are-plc-frc-and-bsp-in-real-estate/ |
| **Possession** | The date/act of the builder formally handing over the physical unit to the buyer; the single most litigated milestone in Indian real estate. |
| **CP (Channel Partner)** | A broker/brokerage firm formally registered with a builder to bring buyers, in exchange for a commission on booking value; distinct from a walk-in or in-house sales channel. |
| **Walk-in** | A site visitor who arrives without a prior scheduled appointment, as opposed to a CRM-scheduled visit. [SOURCED] https://realatic.com/blog/site-visit-tracking-crm/ |
| **Site visit** | A physical visit to the project/sample flat; treated as the highest-intent signal in the sales funnel and the metric ("cost per site visit") that increasingly matters more than raw lead cost. [SOURCED] https://www.digitaltripathi.com/blog/google-adwords-cost-per-lead-for-real-estate-campaign/ |
| **RERA** | Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016 — the central regulatory framework requiring project registration, escrow of funds, disclosure, and giving buyers recourse (interest/refund) for possession delays. |
| **OC (Occupancy Certificate)** | Issued after inspection confirming a completed building is fit for legal occupation; mandatory in many states before handing over possession. [SOURCED] https://www.lntrealty.com/blogs/how-to-check-completion-certificate-and-status-online/ |
| **CC (Completion Certificate)** | Confirms construction complies with building regulations and is structurally complete; a precursor to OC, often confused with it by buyers and even sales staff. [SOURCED] https://asbl.in/blog/what-is-oc-and-cc-in-real-estateyour-essential-guide/ |
| **GST on under-construction property** | 5% standard rate; 1% for affordable housing (unit ≤ ₹45 lakh, meeting area criteria); ready-to-move (post-OC) property attracts no GST. [SOURCED] https://taxgarden.in/blog/gst-on-real-estate-under-construction-property-india-2026 |
| **CPL / CPSV** | Cost Per Lead / Cost Per Site Visit — the two most commonly tracked paid-media metrics in Indian real estate performance marketing; CPSV increasingly emphasized over CPL. [SOURCED] https://www.digitaltripathi.com/blog/google-adwords-cost-per-lead-for-real-estate-campaign/ |
| **Lead registration / lead tagging** | The process (ideally timestamped and CRM-logged) of formally attributing a buyer lead to whichever channel (CP, portal, ad, walk-in) sourced it first, to prevent commission/attribution disputes. [SOURCED] https://www.sell.do/blog/role-and-advantages-of-a-channel-partner-in-real-estate |
| **NAREDCO** | National Real Estate Development Council — apex industry body focused on professionalizing/certifying the sector. [SOURCED] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Real_Estate_Development_Council |
| **CREDAI** | Confederation of Real Estate Developers' Associations of India — apex private-developer body with 13,000+ developer members across 230 city chapters. [SOURCED] https://credai.org/home/ |

CP in real estate means channel partner. A CP is a broker or brokerage firm that a developer formally empanels to sell its projects, and the developer, not the buyer, pays the CP a commission on each booking. So when a sales team says "come through a CP" or calls a brokerage a "CP firm", it means an empanelled channel partner.

In short: CP full form = Channel Partner. This guide explains the CP meaning in plain words, what a CP firm and a CP meeting are, how CPs differ from ordinary brokers, how commission and lead registration work, the RERA rules, how to become one, and how CPs get leads of their own.

What is CP in real estate? Channel partner meaning

A channel partner (CP) is a real estate agent or agency that a developer has formally empanelled to sell its projects. The CP brings buyers, arranges site visits and helps close bookings. In return, the developer pays the CP a commission on each booking under a written CP agreement.

The word "channel" comes from sales: a sales channel is any route through which a product reaches the buyer. For a developer, its own sales team is one channel, property portals and ads are another, and independent brokers are a third. Brokers in that third channel are the "channel partners". That is also what people mean by channeling in real estate: selling inventory through a network of outside partners rather than only through an in-house team.

A CP can be a one-person broker, a firm with a sales team, or a large national advisory. Most CPs work with several developers at the same time.

What is the difference between a channel partner and a broker?

A channel partner sells a developer's new projects and is paid by the developer; a broker usually handles resale or rentals and is paid by the buyer, seller or tenant.

How does a channel partner work with a developer?

A CP signs up with the developer, registers each buyer, brings them to the site and is paid when the booking meets the agreed milestone. The usual sequence:

  1. Empanelment. The CP signs the developer's CP agreement and shares documents, usually including a RERA agent registration.
  2. CP meet and material. The developer shares pricing, brochures, inventory and the rules for marketing the project.
  3. Lead registration. Before or at the first site visit, the CP registers the buyer with the developer, often through a CP portal or app.
  4. Site visit and booking. The CP accompanies the buyer; if the buyer books within the validity period, the booking is credited to the CP.
  5. Payout. Commission is paid once the conditions in the agreement are met, for example after a set share of the price is received or the agreement for sale is registered.

How much commission does a CP get?

There is no fixed industry rate: each developer sets CP commission in its agreement, usually as a percentage of the agreement value. Some add slab incentives for volume or for selling during a launch window. Compare offers on more than the headline percentage:

  • When the commission becomes payable (booking, agreement or a payment milestone).
  • How long payouts take in practice.
  • What happens if the buyer cancels.
  • Tax: GST applies to brokerage services, and tax is deducted at source on commission. From 1 April 2026 the TDS rule sits in Section 393(1) of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (earlier Section 194H), at 2% once commission to one person crosses ₹20,000 in the year, as explained in our broker commission guide. Confirm current rates on the Income Tax Department portal or with your accountant.

A higher rate paid only at possession can be worth less to a small CP than a lower rate paid at registration, so model the cash flow, not only the percentage.

Does a channel partner need RERA registration?

Yes, a CP selling units in a RERA-registered project must be registered as a real estate agent with that state's RERA authority. Under Section 9 of the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016, agents must register before facilitating sales in registered projects. Section 10 sets their duties: no marketing of unregistered projects, proper books and records, and no unfair trade practices such as false statements about a property. Section 62 sets a penalty of up to ₹10,000 a day, up to 5% of the unit's cost, for agents who break these rules.

States add their own requirements. Maharashtra, for example, requires agents to pass a MahaRERA competency certificate exam before registration or renewal (in force from 1 January 2024, as reported by the Free Press Journal). Before taking on a project, run a RERA number check on the project too, and put both numbers on every ad, as our RERA advertisement rules guide explains.

How do CPs avoid lead ownership disputes?

Register every buyer with the developer the same day, keep proof, and accompany the site visit. The most common CP dispute is simple: the same buyer reaches the developer through a CP, a Meta form and the site office in the same week, and more than one party claims the booking. Developers usually settle it using registration time and whether the CP accompanied the visit.

  • Register every lead the day you get it, and save the confirmation.
  • Log the date and time of first contact and every site visit.
  • Know the validity window in your agreement: how long a registration stays yours after a visit.

How do you become a channel partner in real estate?

Register as an agent with your state RERA, then get empanelled with each developer you want to sell for. There is no single national CP licence. The usual steps:

  1. Register with your state RERA authority as a real estate agent. UP RERA, Haryana RERA, Delhi RERA and others each run their own online registration with their own fees, documents and renewal period. You need a separate registration in each state where you sell registered projects.
  2. Get your documents ready. Developers typically ask for PAN, identity and address proof, your RERA agent certificate, a cancelled cheque, and a GST certificate if you are registered for GST. A firm also needs its incorporation or partnership papers.
  3. Apply for empanelment. Most developers have a CP registration form or portal and may ask about your team, the localities you cover and past sales.
  4. Read the CP agreement. Check the commission, payout milestone, cancellation rule and the rules on advertising the project.
  5. Learn the lead registration process before you bring your first buyer.

Why do developers use channel partners?

CPs give a developer reach and trusted buyer relationships without a larger in-house team, at the cost of commission and less direct buyer data.

For the developerFor the buyer
Reach into localities and buyer networks the in-house team doesn't haveOne adviser who can compare several projects
Paid on bookings, not on effortUsually no brokerage on a new-project booking
Risk: lead disputes and less control over how the project is pitchedRisk: a CP may push the project paying the highest commission

Many developers therefore combine CPs with their own ads and landing pages, and measure cost per booking for each source.

How do channel partners get leads?

Most CPs rely on developer-shared leads, property portals and referrals, and the strongest add their own ads and instant replies. Portal enquiries usually go to several brokers at once, so the first to reply often wins (see 99acres vs MagicBricks). More CPs now run:

  • Their own project pages under their brand, within the developer's rules.
  • Meta and Google ads for projects they are mandated on, producing leads no other CP receives; our guide on how to generate real estate leads covers the channels.
  • Instant WhatsApp replies, so a small team answers as fast as a large one.
  • A Google Business Profile for local searches; see our local SEO guide for agents and CPs.
  • Referrals from happy buyers; see real estate referral programs.

Test one full order before spending.

This guide is for founders launching a direct-to-consumer brand in India, whether on Shopify, WooCommerce or a custom store. Most "how to launch a D2C brand" guides stop at positioning and storytelling. This one is the practical list that comes after: the registrations, the pages the law expects on your site, the tracking that makes your ad money measurable, and the tests to run before you switch ads on. What does a D2C brand need in place before its first ad goes live?

You need six things ready: legal registrations, a compliant website, written store policies, tracking, consent-based customer capture and a fast store. Ads only multiply what is already there. If checkout breaks, a policy page is missing or the pixel misfires, the first weeks of ad spend teach you nothing.

Which registrations does a D2C brand need in India?

Most D2C brands need a business entity, GST registration, a current account, a trademark application and any licence their product category requires. The order matters, because each one is usually asked for by the next: the bank asks for the entity, the payment gateway asks for GST and bank details, and so on.

GST is usually compulsory from day one

A D2C store that ships across India makes inter-State sales. Under Section 24 of the CGST Act, people making inter-State taxable supplies must register for GST whatever their turnover, as ClearTax's guide to compulsory registration explains. So the usual turnover threshold does not help a website that sells goods to other States. Selling through marketplaces has its own GST rules, so if you plan to list on Amazon, Flipkart or others as well, ask your CA to cover both channels together.

Trademark your brand name early

You will spend money making a name known, so file for it before you advertise it. Applications are made online through the Office of the Controller General of Patents, Designs and Trade Marks. A search of the register before you print packaging saves an expensive rebrand later.

Check your category licence

Some products need their own licence or registration before sale. Food and beverages, for example, need FSSAI registration or a licence through the FSSAI's FoSCoS portal. Cosmetics, supplements, electronics and children's products each have their own rules, so confirm yours before the first production run rather than after the first ad.

What must a D2C website show by law?

Your store must show who you are, how to reach you and a named grievance officer, plus the product declarations that would appear on the pack. The Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020 apply to "all models of e-commerce, including marketplace and inventory models". A brand selling its own stock on its own website is an inventory e-commerce entity under those rules.

The E-Commerce Rules also say the grievance officer must acknowledge a complaint within forty-eight hours and resolve it within one month of receiving it (Rule 4(5)). Set up a shared inbox or helpdesk before launch so a complaint from your first ad does not sit unread.

For product pages, Lakshmikumaran & Sridharan's summary of Rule 6(10) lists the declarations an e-commerce entity must show online. The simplest habit is to photograph or type out everything on your physical label into each product page, so the website and the pack never disagree.

Two newer changes are worth knowing. A February 2026 amendment added Rule 6(10A), which, according to SCC Online's explainer, requires e-commerce entities selling imported products to offer a searchable and sortable country-of-origin filter from 1 July 2026. And the Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) (Amendment) Rules, 2026, announced by the Press Information Bureau on 10 September 2026, come into force on 1 January 2027 and add duties on price reductions, sponsored listings, dark patterns and complaint copies.

How should a D2C brand write its return, refund and COD policies?

Write short, specific policies that say what can be returned, within what window, who pays return shipping, how and when refunds are paid, and how cash on delivery works. Google's Merchant Center requirements ask for a published return and refund policy that explains the steps, the return window and when refunds arrive, and they ask you to state a policy even if you do not accept returns. The same policies matter again when you set up product ads; our Google Shopping ads setup guide for D2C brands covers the rest of Merchant Center.

Some points are not yours to choose. Rule 7(4) of the E-Commerce Rules says an inventory e-commerce entity cannot refuse to take back goods or refund the customer if the goods are defective, not as described, or delivered late (except for force majeure). Rule 4(8) says you cannot charge a cancellation fee unless you bear a similar charge when you cancel an order yourself. And Rule 4(9) says purchase consent must be an explicit action, never a pre-ticked box.

Plan cash on delivery before you need it

Cash on delivery is common in Indian e-commerce, and it brings refused deliveries and return-to-origin shipping costs. Decide before launch:

  • Whether COD is offered on all products, or only below a certain order value you set.
  • Whether a COD fee applies. If it does, show it on the product page and in the cart, not as a surprise at the last step.
  • How COD orders are confirmed, for example with a WhatsApp message asking the buyer to confirm before dispatch.
  • How refunds are paid on returned COD orders, since there is no card or UPI payment to reverse.

Showing every charge early is not just good manners. The Central Consumer Protection Authority's Guidelines for Prevention and Regulation of Dark Patterns, 2023 list drip pricing (revealing charges late), false urgency and basket sneaking among the banned practices. Fake "only two left" counters and fees added at checkout both fall into this.

How do you set up tracking before D2C ads go live?

Install GA4, the Meta Pixel with the Conversions API, and Google Ads conversion tracking, then place a real test order and confirm each tool records exactly one purchase with the right value. Ad platforms learn from purchase events. If they see no purchases, or see each one twice, they optimise towards the wrong people.

  1. Set up GA4 with the e-commerce events your platform supports (view item, add to cart, begin checkout, purchase).
  2. Install the Meta Pixel on every page and fire standard events for the same steps.
  3. Add the Conversions API so purchases are also sent from your server, which survives browser blocking. Shopify and most platforms offer an official integration.
  4. Deduplicate. Meta's deduplication guide says the Pixel's eventID must match the Conversions API's event_id, and the event names must match. Matching events received within 48 hours of the first are treated as one.
  5. Add Google Ads conversion tracking for purchases, and link GA4 to Google Ads.
  6. Place a real order using UPI, then another using COD. Check Meta Events Manager, GA4 real-time reports and Google Ads for one purchase each, with the correct order value.
  7. Write down what you checked and when, so you can repeat the test after any theme or app change.

What do India's privacy rules mean for a new D2C brand?

Collect only the data you need, tell people clearly why you collect it, get real consent for marketing, and make opting out easy. The Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025 were notified on 14 November 2025 with an eighteen-month phased compliance period, according to the Press Information Bureau's explainer. The same document says every data fiduciary must issue a separate, clear consent notice explaining the specific purpose, that people may withdraw consent at any time, and that failing to keep reasonable security safeguards can attract a penalty of up to ₹250 crore.

A brand launching now should build the habits from the start rather than retrofit them later:

  • A plain-language privacy notice that lists what you collect at checkout and signup, and why.
  • Marketing consent as a separate, unticked checkbox, not bundled into the terms.
  • An unsubscribe link in every email and a "STOP" option on WhatsApp.
  • A named contact for privacy questions, which can be the same team as your grievance officer.

WhatsApp needs opt-in before you message

Meta's WhatsApp opt-in guidance says businesses must get opt-in before messaging people, must make clear the person is agreeing to receive messages from that business, and must honour requests to stop. A checkbox at checkout that says "Send me order updates and offers on WhatsApp" does the job. How fast should a D2C store be before you send paid traffic?

Aim for all three Core Web Vitals in Google's "good" range on mobile: Largest Contentful Paint within 2.5 seconds, Interaction to Next Paint of 200 milliseconds or less, and Cumulative Layout Shift of 0.1 or less. Google's web.dev guide to Web Vitals sets these thresholds and measures them at the 75th percentile of page loads.

Ad clicks land on product pages and collection pages, usually on phones. So test those pages, not just the homepage. The usual fixes on a new store:

  • Compress product photos and serve them in modern formats at the size they display.
  • Remove apps and scripts you installed while building and no longer use.
  • Reserve space for images, banners and review widgets so the page does not jump while loading.
  • Keep the add-to-cart button visible without scrolling on a phone.

What should you test in the week before launch?

Test the whole customer journey yourself: find the product, buy it with each payment method, receive it, return it and get the refund. Ask two or three people outside the team to do the same on their own phones and note anything confusing.

How should a D2C brand plan its first ads?

Start small, with a fixed test budget, a few creatives and one clear offer, and judge results on purchases and their value, not clicks or likes. The first weeks are for learning which product, message and audience sell, so keep the number of variables low.

  • Pick one or two hero products with good margins and strong photos, rather than advertising the whole catalogue.
  • Make claims you can prove. Rule 7(3) of the E-Commerce Rules says your advertisements must match the product's actual characteristics. Health, results or "best in India" claims need evidence.
  • Use short video. Product demos and unboxings suit Reels and Stories; our Instagram Reels marketing guide covers formats and Reel ads.
  • Plan for search, including AI search. People increasingly ask ChatGPT, Gemini or Perplexity which product to buy. Clear product pages, FAQs and honest comparisons help you get cited; see our generative engine optimization guide.
  • Own the ad accounts. Create the Meta Business portfolio, Google Ads and Merchant Center accounts in the brand's name, and give any agency access rather than letting them create the accounts for you.

When the first sales come in, watch return rates and COD refusals alongside the cost per purchase. A product that sells well but comes back often is not a winner yet.

What mistakes do new D2C brands make at launch?

The common mistakes are launching ads before tracking works, hiding charges until checkout, copying another brand's policy pages, and collecting customer data without clear consent. Each one is easy to avoid with the checklist above.

  • Running ads before testing a real order, then discovering the pixel counted nothing.
  • Adding COD or shipping fees at the last checkout step, which the dark patterns guidelines treat as drip pricing.
  • Pasting another store's return policy that does not match how you actually handle returns.
  • Leaving out the grievance officer details and the product declarations.
  • Pre-ticking marketing consent boxes.
  • Using fake countdown timers or invented stock counts.
  • Letting a freelancer or agency own the ad accounts, so you lose the data if you part ways.

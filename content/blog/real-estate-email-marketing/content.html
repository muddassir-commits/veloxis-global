Email marketing for real estate works best as a follow-up channel: short, useful emails to people who already enquired or bought, such as brochures, site-visit confirmations, construction updates and new launches, sent from your own domain to a consented list. In India, WhatsApp gets the first reply; email carries the detail buyers save and forward.

This guide is for builders, brokers and channel partners. It covers when email fits, the emails every real estate business should send, a ready follow-up sequence, sample emails and subject lines, how to build a list legally, the technical setup that keeps you out of spam, tools, automation and what to measure.

Does email marketing work for real estate in India?

Yes, as a follow-up and relationship channel rather than a cold outreach tool. Property decisions take weeks or months, so most leads won't book on the first call. Email helps you stay in touch without calling every week:

  • Nurturing open leads who enquired but haven't visited or decided.
  • Updating existing buyers on construction, payments and possession, which reduces calls to your sales team.
  • Reaching past buyers and investors when you launch a new project. See our referral program guide.
  • Working with channel partners: inventory updates, price lists and offers. See channel partners in real estate.
  • NRI buyers, who are in different time zones and often prefer email for documents. See our NRI marketing guide.

What emails should a real estate business send?

Every builder and broker needs six types of email. Each should have one main action: book a visit, reply with a question, or download a document.

  1. Enquiry follow-up: sent right after the enquiry, with the brochure, floor plans, location pin and a link to book a site visit. Keep the WhatsApp reply short and put the detail here.
  2. Site-visit confirmation and reminder: date, time, map link, who will meet them, and what documents to bring if they plan to book.
  3. After-visit email: thank them, attach the price sheet and payment plan discussed, and answer the questions they asked on site.
  4. Monthly construction update: a few real photos, what was completed, and what comes next.
  5. New launch or new inventory: sent first to past enquiries and buyers who opted in, before public ads.
  6. Payment and possession updates for existing buyers: due dates, bank loan help, handover process.

How do you write real estate emails that get read?

  • Subject line: specific and plain. Avoid all caps, "URGENT" and "limited offer".
  • First line: say why you're writing, in one sentence.
  • Short body: a few short paragraphs or a list. Many people read on phones.
  • Real photos and facts instead of adjectives, and the RERA number on any email that advertises a registered project. Section 11(2) of the RERA Act, 2016 requires ads for registered projects to show the registration number and the authority's website. See RERA advertisement rules.
  • A named sender and a reply address someone actually reads, plus WhatsApp and phone in the signature.
  • Plain text or light HTML: heavy designs built from one big image are hard to read and often end up in Promotions or spam.

Subject lines that work because they are specific:

  • "Your site visit on Saturday at 11 am: map and parking"
  • "Tower B slab work done: October photos"
  • "3 BHK floor plans and price sheet for {{project}}"
  • "Home loan options for {{project}} (approved banks)"
  • "Should I keep your enquiry open?"

How do you build a real estate email list legally?

Build your list only from people who gave you their email address and expect to hear from you:

  • Enquiry forms on your landing pages and website, with a line saying you will send project details by email.
  • Site visit registers (paper or tablet) with a tick box for updates.
  • Existing buyers, for updates about their own unit.
  • Channel partners who signed up for inventory updates.
  • A "get the price sheet" or "download the brochure" form on your project landing page.

Never buy email lists. They damage your sender reputation and conflict with India's data protection law (next section).

What does the DPDP Act mean for real estate email marketing?

India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and the DPDP Rules, notified on 14 November 2025, make consent and purpose central. According to the government's explainer (PIB), the Rules give organisations an eighteen-month phased compliance period, every business must issue a clear consent notice explaining the specific purpose for which data is used, and individuals may withdraw consent at any time.

In practice: record where and when each person shared their email, say on the form what you will send, add an unsubscribe link to every marketing email, and act quickly on unsubscribe and deletion requests. Ask your lawyer for the exact obligations that apply to your business.

How do you stop real estate emails going to spam?

Authenticate your domain, make unsubscribing easy and keep complaints low. Since 1 February 2024, Google's email sender guidelines require anyone sending more than 5,000 messages a day to Gmail accounts to set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC, support one-click unsubscribe, and keep the spam rate in Postmaster Tools below 0.30%, ideally below 0.10%. Yahoo publishes similar sender best practices. Even if you send far fewer, following the same rules protects delivery.

  1. Send from your own domain (for example sales@yourproject.com), not a free Gmail or Yahoo address.
  2. Add SPF, DKIM and DMARC records to the domain's DNS. Your email platform shows the exact records to copy.
  3. Add a visible unsubscribe link to every marketing email, plus one-click unsubscribe headers (your platform handles these).
  4. Watch spam complaints in Google Postmaster Tools.
  5. Use a proper email platform for lists of more than a few hundred people, so bounces and unsubscribes are handled for you.
  6. Remove bounced addresses after each send, and stop emailing people who haven't engaged in many months.

Which email tools suit a real estate business?

Pick a tool by what it connects to, not by features you won't use. Three common setups:

  • Your CRM's own email: best if your real estate CRM already sends emails, because every email sits on the lead's record.
  • A dedicated email platform such as Mailchimp, Brevo or Zoho Campaigns, connected to your forms. Good for monthly updates and launch emails.
  • Google Workspace or Microsoft 365 for one-to-one emails from salespeople. Fine for personal follow-ups, not for bulk sends.

Check each tool's current plans on its official site; most offer a free or trial tier.

How do you automate real estate emails?

Connect your website form, landing page or CRM to your email tool so that:

  • Every new enquiry gets the follow-up email within minutes, alongside the WhatsApp reply.
  • Site-visit bookings trigger a confirmation and a reminder the day before.
  • Leads who haven't visited get the short sequence above, then a monthly update.
  • Any reply stops the automatic emails and alerts a salesperson.

What should you measure in real estate email marketing?

Measure clicks, replies and site visits, not just opens. Open rates are less reliable than they used to be: Apple's Mail Privacy Protection loads remote content privately in the background, which can make emails look opened when they weren't read.

MetricWhat it tells you
Clicks (floor plan, visit link)Whether the content is useful
RepliesReal interest, and questions to answer
Site visits booked from emailWhether email contributes to sales
BouncesList quality; remove bounced addresses
Unsubscribes and spam complaintsWhether you send too often or to the wrong people

If a type of email gets no clicks or replies after a few sends, change the subject and content or stop sending it.

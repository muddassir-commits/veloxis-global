The best real estate landing pages show the locality, configurations, starting price and RERA number on the first phone screen, ask for four details or fewer, offer WhatsApp, load fast on mobile data, and track every enquiry back to the ad. Each project gets its own page, and each ad points to the page that matches its promise.

This guide is for developers, brokers and channel partners running Google or Meta ads to a project. It explains what a real estate landing page is, the nine fixes that matter most, a section order that follows the buyer's questions, the mistakes to avoid and a launch checklist. Where a fix depends on a platform rule or a performance standard, the source is linked.

What is a real estate landing page?

A real estate landing page is a single page built for one project, configuration or offer, where people arrive from an ad, email or QR code and are asked to take one action: enquire, get the price sheet or book a site visit. Unlike a company website, it has no menus to other projects and no "About us" detour.

In our own automated check of 749 real estate homepages, most lacked tracking and a quick way to enquire. Only homepages were checked, so some sites may have forms or tracking on inner pages. Even so, more than half could not tell which ads bring enquiries. Fixes 6, 7 and 9 below address exactly these gaps.

1. Why should ads go to a dedicated project page?

Because a page that repeats the exact promise of the ad keeps the buyer reading, while a corporate homepage sends them hunting. Give each project, tower or phase its own page, and send each ad to the page that matches it. Google's own guidance on landing page experience says well-rated pages are well organised and have text that relates to the person's search (Google Ads Help).

"Matches" is specific. An ad about ready-to-move 3 BHKs should land on a page that leads with ready-to-move 3 BHKs. For large projects, build versions for the main configurations or buyer types (end users, investors, NRIs), each with its own ads.

2. What should a buyer see above the fold?

The first phone screen should answer five questions: where the project is, what configurations exist, the starting price, whether it is RERA registered, and how to ask for more. Put the "Get price sheet on WhatsApp" button there too, and make sure the brochure it sends matches the page.

A simple test: show the first screen to someone for five seconds, then take the phone away. If they can't tell you the locality, configurations and starting price, the screen isn't working. A render with a project name and tagline fails this test more often than any other design.

3. Should a landing page show the price?

Yes: show a starting price or band instead of "price on request". Buyers use price to decide whether a project is worth their time. Hiding it brings in people who can't afford it and puts off those who can. The full cost sheet can follow after they share their number. Label what the price includes, and say "all-inclusive" only if it truly is.

4. Where should RERA details go?

Show the project's RERA registration number and the state RERA website on the first screen or close to it, plus the QR code where your state requires one. For a buyer who doesn't know the developer, it is the quickest proof the project is legitimate, and the RERA Act requires promoters to mention the registration details in advertisements; a landing page is an advertisement. Our guides to RERA advertisement rules and the RERA number check cover the details by state.

5. How do you sell the location?

Show honest travel times to the metro, expressway, schools, hospitals and office hubs on a simple map. Buyers shortlist localities before projects. "12 minutes to the metro" should be true at the time buyers travel; they check on Google Maps, and one wrong claim makes them doubt the rest. Mention upcoming infrastructure only when officially approved, and label it as upcoming.

6. How short should the form be, and why add WhatsApp?

Ask for four things or fewer (name, mobile, configuration, budget) and add a sticky WhatsApp button on mobile. Many buyers would rather chat than wait for a call. WhatsApp's own click-to-chat links (wa.me/<number>?text=...) can pre-fill a message such as "Please send the price sheet for [project]".

  • Use buttons, not typed fields, for configuration and budget; tapping is faster on a phone.
  • Say what happens next on the button: "Get price sheet on WhatsApp" is clearer than "Submit".
  • Reply instantly. See how to automate lead follow-up on WhatsApp.

7. How fast should a real estate landing page load?

Aim for Google's "good" Core Web Vitals on mobile: main content visible within 2.5 seconds (LCP), responses to taps within 200 milliseconds (INP) and layout shift of 0.1 or less (CLS), measured at the 75th percentile of visits (web.dev).

  • Compress images and serve modern formats.
  • Avoid sliders and autoplay video above the fold.
  • Load chat widgets and extra scripts after the page is usable.
  • Test on a mid-range Android phone over mobile data, or with PageSpeed Insights.

Speed and relevance also affect ad costs. Google Ads lists the quality of your ads and landing page among the factors in Ad Rank, including ease of navigation, and says higher-quality ads can often lead to lower costs per click (Google Ads Help).

8. What proof builds trust on a project page?

Dated construction photos, the developer's completed projects and bank approvals do more than another render. Renders show what the project will be; dated photos show it is happening. A short phone video of the site, updated monthly, works well. If the developer has delivered projects, show them with locations so buyers can visit. Never use invented reviews or testimonials.

9. How do you track leads back to the ad?

Track form submits, WhatsApp clicks and calls as conversions, tag every campaign and channel partner link, and send site-visit outcomes from your CRM back to Google and Meta.

  1. Install Google Tag Manager or the platform tags, and mark form submits, WhatsApp clicks and calls as conversions.
  2. Give each campaign and CP its own UTM-tagged link (GA4 help).
  3. Record the source on every lead in your CRM.
  4. Upload site-visit and booking outcomes back to the ad platforms, using Google Ads enhanced conversions for leads (Google) and Meta's Conversions API (Meta).

Without this you can only judge ads on cost per lead, which rewards the cheapest enquiries, not buyers. With it you see cost per site visit by campaign. More on budgets in real estate marketing budget.

What sections should a real estate landing page have?

Follow the order of the buyer's questions.

  1. Hero: project name, locality, configurations, starting price, RERA number and the main button.
  2. Why this project: three short reasons, each backed by a fact.
  3. Location: map with travel times.
  4. Floor plans: one card per unit type with carpet area, and a button for full plans.
  5. Amenities: those in the approved plan, renders labelled as renders.
  6. Construction progress and developer track record.
  7. Payment plan: booking, construction-linked and possession stages.
  8. FAQs: the questions your sales team hears daily.
  9. Final call to action: form, WhatsApp and site-visit timings.

Repeat the call to action every two or three sections. For the ads that feed the page, see real estate ad examples; for full websites, see real estate website examples.

What are common real estate landing page mistakes?

  • Sending every ad to the homepage.
  • A slider of renders as the first screen, with no price or location.
  • Forms with eight or more fields.
  • No WhatsApp option.
  • Prices, possession dates or offers that differ from the ads and brochure.
  • Pop-ups that cover the page before the buyer has read anything.
  • No tracking, so nobody knows which ad produced which buyer.

Checklist before the campaign goes live

  1. The first screen shows locality, configurations, starting price, RERA number and a clear button.
  2. The page matches the promise of the ads that point to it.
  3. The form asks for four things or fewer, with tap-to-choose options.
  4. A WhatsApp button is visible on mobile and every enquiry gets an instant reply.
  5. Core Web Vitals pass on mobile in PageSpeed Insights.
  6. Travel times, amenities and dates are accurate and match the brochure.
  7. Conversions fire for forms, WhatsApp clicks and calls.
  8. Site-visit outcomes will be sent back to the ad platforms.

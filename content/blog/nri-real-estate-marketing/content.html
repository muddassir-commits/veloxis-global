To get NRI clients for real estate, run ads aimed at Indians in the countries and cities where they live, send them to a page that answers NRI questions on FEMA rules, payments and power of attorney, and reply on WhatsApp in their time zone with a dated video walk-through and a call slot.

NRI buyers can't drop in for a site visit, so they decide on trust, documents and how well you communicate from a distance.

This guide is for developers, channel partners and brokers in India. It covers where NRI buyers live, what they worry about, the RBI and FEMA rules to explain, where to advertise, the landing page they need, follow-up across time zones, and the mistakes that lose NRI leads.

Who are NRI property buyers, and where do they live?

NRI property buyers are Indian citizens living abroad who buy homes in India for their family, for a planned return, or as an investment. India's Ministry of External Affairs puts the number of NRIs at about 1.78 crore (MEA, data as of January 2026). The largest groups live in the UAE (about 43 lakh), Saudi Arabia (about 27 lakh) and the USA (about 23 lakh), followed by Canada and Kuwait. That is why Gulf cities are usually the first place a north Indian developer advertises.

Most NRI buyers fall into three groups:

  • Buying a home for the family in India, often for parents, in their home city.
  • Planning to return in a few years and wanting a home ready when they do.
  • Investing for rental income or long-term growth in a city they know.

Many prefer their home city or the nearest metro: someone from Lucknow working in Dubai often looks at Lucknow or Noida first. That makes "home city + country of residence" targeting more useful than "all NRIs".

What do NRI buyers worry about?

NRI buyers worry most about trust and distance: whether the project will be delivered, and whether they can manage the purchase without being in India.

  • Trust and delivery: the RERA number and the developer's completed projects matter even more than for local buyers.
  • Seeing the property: they need honest, dated video, not just renders.
  • Paperwork from abroad: signing the agreement, registration and home loans without travelling.
  • Payments and repatriation: which account to pay from and whether they can take money back out later.
  • Someone on the ground: who will handle possession, letting and maintenance.

Can NRIs buy property in India? The FEMA rules to explain

Yes. NRIs and Overseas Citizens of India (OCIs) can buy residential and commercial property in India without RBI permission, but not agricultural land, plantation property or farm houses. The rules are set out in the RBI's Master Direction on acquisition or transfer of immovable property under FEMA (updated as on 1 September 2022). A short, accurate FAQ on your page builds confidence:

Question buyers askWhat the RBI Master Direction says
What can I buy?Any immovable property other than agricultural land, plantation property or a farm house (para 3.1.1)
Can I inherit farmland?Inheritance from a resident in India is allowed (para 3.1.4); purchase and gift of agricultural land are not
How do I pay?Through banking channels: inward remittance from abroad, or debit to an NRE, FCNR(B) or NRO account (para 3.3.1)
Can I pay in cash or foreign notes?No. Payment cannot be made by traveller's cheque or foreign currency notes (para 3.3.2)
Can I take sale money back abroad?Yes, subject to conditions; for residential property, repatriation is limited to not more than two properties (para 8.2)

Two practical points sit outside the Master Direction. Indian banks and housing finance companies offer home loans to NRIs under their own eligibility rules, and buyers who can't travel usually give a family member a power of attorney to sign and register for them. Point buyers to their bank and a chartered accountant for both.

The farmland rule matters for marketing: don't target NRIs with farmhouse or agricultural-land projects. Our guide on how to sell plots online covers plotted projects that NRIs can buy.

General information, not legal or tax advice. Checked on 5 November 2026 against the RBI Master Direction; rules and bank policies change, so buyers should confirm with their bank or a chartered accountant.

Where to reach NRI buyers

The best places to reach NRI buyers are targeted Meta and Google ads in their country of residence, YouTube walk-throughs, community networks and referrals from existing NRI buyers.

  • Meta ads (Facebook and Instagram): target people living in a chosen country or city, with your home-city message in the creative. Run separate ad sets per region so you can compare costs. If your ads will reach people in the US, Canada or some European countries, Meta requires housing ads to be declared as a Special Ad Category, which restricts some targeting options. See Meta ads for real estate.
  • Google Search: catches searches such as "flats in Noida for NRI" or "property in Lucknow from Dubai". Google Ads lets you target ads to geographic locations, so set the location to the buyer's country and put the Indian locality in your keywords. See Google Ads for real estate.
  • YouTube: full walk-through and locality videos, which NRIs often watch before shortlisting. Our real estate video marketing guide covers what to film.
  • Community networks abroad: associations, events and newsletters, with real, useful content rather than hard selling.
  • Referrals from existing NRI buyers, who often know others in the same city. See our referral program guide.

How to get NRI clients: a step-by-step plan

  1. Pick two or three markets where buyers from your city live, starting with the Gulf if your project is in north India.
  2. Prepare proof: RERA number, approvals, the developer's delivered projects and a dated walk-through video.
  3. Build an NRI page with the FAQ above, WhatsApp as the main button and a call booking in the buyer's time zone.
  4. Launch one ad set per market on Meta and a small Google Search campaign for "[city] property for NRI" searches.
  5. Reply instantly on WhatsApp with the brochure, video and two call slots in local time.
  6. Offer a live video site visit with a salesperson walking the site.
  7. Send monthly construction updates to every NRI lead, because decisions often take months.
  8. Review cost per qualified call by market every month and move budget to the best one.

What should an NRI landing page include?

An NRI landing page should include the usual project facts plus the answers a buyer abroad needs before calling:

  • A short NRI FAQ: eligibility, payment channels, home loans, power of attorney, possession and rental help.
  • A recent video walk-through of the site and a sample flat, with the date it was filmed.
  • WhatsApp as the main button, because it works the same in every country.
  • A call-booking option that shows times in the buyer's own time zone.
  • The RERA number, authority website and the developer's completed projects.

How to follow up NRI leads across time zones

Follow up NRI leads instantly by WhatsApp and call them in their own working hours, not India's. NRIs often enquire late at night India time, after their own working day.

  • Send an instant WhatsApp reply with the brochure, walk-through video and two call slots in their local time.
  • Offer video site visits on WhatsApp or Google Meet, with a salesperson walking the site live.
  • Send a short monthly construction update with photos, so long decision cycles don't go cold.

Common NRI marketing mistakes

  • One ad set for "all NRIs", so you can't see which country works.
  • Renders only, with no real, dated video of the site.
  • Calling at Indian office hours, which is the middle of the night in the US.
  • Vague answers on payments and FEMA, or worse, wrong ones.
  • Pushing farmland or farmhouses to NRIs, which they cannot buy.
  • Going quiet after the first call. NRI decisions are slow; regular updates keep you in the shortlist.

NRI marketing checklist

  • Ad sets split by country or city, with the home city in the message
  • Special Ad Category declared where Meta requires it
  • An NRI page or section with an accurate FAQ and a dated walk-through video
  • Instant WhatsApp reply, any time of day
  • Call and video-visit slots offered in the buyer's time zone
  • Monthly construction updates for every NRI lead
  • RERA number and the developer's completed projects shown clearly

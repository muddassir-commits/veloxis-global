EOI in real estate means Expression of Interest. It is a form plus a payment (the EOI amount, often by cheque) that gives a buyer priority to choose a unit at a project launch. An EOI is not a booking: no flat is yours yet, and the written EOI terms decide whether you get the money back.

In short: EOI full form = Expression of Interest; EOI meaning = a priority slot in a new launch, not a purchase. This guide explains how an EOI works step by step, what the EOI amount and EOI cheque are, how EOI differs from a token and a booking amount, whether it is refundable, what the RERA Act says, and the checks to run before you pay.

What is EOI in real estate?

An EOI is a written request, usually with a payment, telling a developer you want to buy in a project that is about to launch. In return you get priority: an earlier slot to choose a unit, and sometimes a launch price or offer that later buyers don't get.

Think of it as a place in the queue. You have told the developer which configuration you want (for example a 2 BHK or 3 BHK), your budget and perhaps a floor preference, and you have put money down to show you are serious. You have not been allotted a flat, no price has been locked for a specific unit, and there is no agreement for sale.

Summary: EOI = interest + priority. Booking = a chosen unit + a confirmed purchase. Agreement for sale = the legal contract. They happen in that order.

What is the full form of EOI?

The full form of EOI is Expression of Interest. The same term is used outside property, and the meaning shifts slightly (see the general definition on Wikipedia):

Where you see itWhat EOI means there
Real estate (new launches)A form plus a payment that gives a buyer priority to choose a unit at launch
Government and company tendersA first-stage document in which firms say they want to bid, before the detailed tender or RFP
Jobs and admissionsA note or form telling an employer or institution you are interested
Business dealsAn early, usually non-binding, statement that one party wants to explore a deal

In every case an EOI signals interest without being a final contract. In real estate the difference is that money usually changes hands, which is why the written terms matter so much.

How does the EOI process work?

The EOI process runs from launch announcement to allotment, then to booking or refund. The usual steps:

  1. Launch announcement. The developer and its channel partners start collecting interest through site offices, CPs, ads and a project landing page.
  2. EOI form. You fill in your name, PAN, preferred configuration, budget and floor or facing preference.
  3. EOI payment. You pay the EOI amount by cheque or bank transfer to the developer's company account, and get a receipt.
  4. Priority number. You are given a priority or token number. Some developers allot by first-come order, others by a draw.
  5. Launch day and unit selection. EOI holders are called in order to choose from the released inventory at the published price.
  6. Booking or refund. If you pick a unit, the EOI is usually adjusted against the booking amount. If you don't find a suitable unit, the EOI terms decide whether and when you get a refund.

Developers vary the details, so the EOI form and receipt are the documents that count, not what was said at the site office.

What is an EOI amount and an EOI cheque?

The EOI amount is the money paid with the EOI form; an EOI cheque is that amount paid by cheque. Developers set it per project, often as a fixed sum for each configuration, with a higher figure for larger units. There is no standard amount across the market, so judge it against the unit price and the written refund terms.

Make the EOI cheque out to the developer's company, not to a person or a broker. An EOI payment can also be made by bank transfer. Either way, ask for a receipt that names the project, the amount and your preferred configuration. Never pay an EOI in cash.

One legal limit applies once you book: under Section 13 of the RERA Act, 2016, a promoter can't accept more than 10% of the cost of the unit as an advance or application fee before signing and registering a written agreement for sale. If your EOI is adjusted against the booking, it counts towards that advance.

What is the difference between EOI, token amount and booking amount?

EOI buys priority, a token holds a chosen unit for a short time, and the booking amount confirms the purchase.

TermWhen it is paidWhat you getUsually refundable?
EOIBefore or at launchPriority to choose a unit; no specific unit yetOften, if you don't select a unit (check the terms)
Token amountAfter selecting a unitThat unit held for you while paperwork is doneDepends on the terms; often with a deduction
Booking amountWhen you confirm the purchaseAllotment of the unit, followed by the agreement for saleGoverned by the allotment letter and agreement

Developers use these words loosely, and some call the first payment a "token" or an "application". Read the receipt and the form rather than relying on the name.

Is EOI refundable?

Often yes, but only on the conditions written in the EOI terms. A common pattern is a full refund if you don't get a unit you want at allotment, and a deduction or forfeiture if you withdraw after selecting a unit. Before paying, get written answers to these questions:

  • Is the EOI fully refundable if I don't choose a unit, and if the launch is delayed or cancelled?
  • How many days does the refund take, and is there any deduction or processing charge?
  • Is the EOI adjusted against the booking amount if I go ahead?
  • Is interest paid if the refund is late?

If the answers are only verbal, ask for them in writing, on the developer's letterhead or in the EOI form itself, before you pay.

Is EOI legal under RERA?

The RERA Act doesn't use the word EOI, but it bans collecting bookings or inviting purchases before a project is registered. Two sections of the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016 matter most:

  • Section 3 says a promoter cannot advertise, market, book, sell, offer for sale or invite persons to purchase in any manner any unit in a project that has to be registered, without first registering it with the state RERA authority. Section 59 sets the penalty: up to 10% of the estimated project cost.
  • Section 13 caps the advance at 10% of the unit cost until a registered agreement for sale is signed.

Legal commentators argue that "invite persons to purchase in any manner" is wide enough to cover EOI collection for an unregistered project (TaxGuru analysis). Regulators have also warned buyers: in August 2026 Haryana RERA cautioned home buyers against unregistered projects and asked promoters to stop pre-launch advertising before registration (Outlook Money).

So the safe reading for buyers and developers alike: an EOI for a project that already has a RERA number is normal practice; an EOI for an unregistered project is a red flag. Check the number on the state RERA website, for example UP RERA for Noida, Greater Noida, Lucknow and Kanpur. Our guide on how to check a RERA number shows the steps.

What are the risks of paying an EOI?

The main risks are paying for an unregistered project, unclear refund terms and long refund delays. Others to watch:

  • No guaranteed unit. Popular configurations can run out before your priority number is called.
  • Price not locked. The launch price is announced on launch day; your EOI may not fix it.
  • Money with a middleman. A cheque made out to a broker or an individual is hard to recover.
  • Pressure tactics. "Only today" deadlines push buyers to pay before reading the terms.

What should you check before paying an EOI?

Check five things: the RERA number, written refund terms, the payee, the receipt and the allotment method.

  1. Find the project's RERA registration number and confirm it on the state RERA website.
  2. Read the written EOI terms: refund conditions, timelines and deductions.
  3. Pay by cheque or bank transfer to the developer's company account, never in cash.
  4. Get a receipt naming the project, the amount and your preferred configuration.
  5. Ask how allotment works: first-come, draw, or floor-wise release.

If you are buying through a broker, also check that the broker is registered as an agent with the state RERA. Our guide to channel partners (CPs) in real estate explains how brokers work on new launches and who pays them.

How should developers run an EOI campaign?

Run it only after RERA registration, with the terms published up front and a fast reply to every enquiry. A good EOI campaign is a lead-generation campaign with clear rules. Buyers trust it more when the project page shows the RERA number, the EOI amount and the refund terms, and when every enquiry gets a quick, specific reply instead of a generic callback.

  • Put the RERA number and authority website on every ad, as Section 11(2) requires. Our guide to RERA advertisement rules has the full checklist.
  • Send the EOI form and terms on WhatsApp the moment someone enquires, so the buyer reads them before visiting.
  • Track every EOI by source (ads, CPs, site office) so you know what filled the list.

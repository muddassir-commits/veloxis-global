EOI in real estate means Expression of Interest: a form and a payment (the EOI amount, often paid by cheque) that reserves your priority to choose a unit in a project before or at launch. It is not a booking, and whether you get it back depends on the refund terms written in the EOI form.

If you are looking at a new launch, the sales team will often ask for an EOI before prices and inventory are fully released. This guide explains what an EOI is, what you are actually paying for, and the questions to ask before you pay.

EOI meaning in real estate

EOI stands for Expression of Interest. It is a written request, usually with a payment, that tells the developer you want to buy in a project that is about to launch. In return you get priority: an earlier slot to choose a unit, and sometimes a launch price or offer that later buyers don’t get.

An EOI is not a purchase. You haven’t been allotted a specific flat, and there is no agreement for sale yet. It is a place in the queue.

EOI full form and other meanings

The full form of EOI is Expression of Interest. The term is used in other fields too, and the meaning shifts slightly:

Where you see itWhat EOI means there
Real estate (new launches)A form plus a payment that gives a buyer priority to choose a unit at launch
Government and company tendersA first-stage document in which firms say they want to bid, before the detailed tender (an RFP)
Jobs and admissionsA note or form telling an employer or institution you are interested in a role or programme
Business dealsAn early, usually non-binding, statement that one party wants to explore a deal

In every case an EOI signals interest without being a final contract. In real estate, the difference is that money usually changes hands, which is why the written terms matter.

How the EOI process works

  1. Pre-launch or launch announcement. The developer or its channel partners start collecting interest, usually through site offices, CPs, ads and a project landing page.
  2. EOI form and payment. You fill a form with your preferred configuration (for example 2 or 3 BHK), budget and floor preference, and pay the EOI amount by cheque or bank transfer.
  3. Allotment. On launch day, EOI holders are called in order — first-come or by draw — to select a unit from the released inventory.
  4. Booking or refund. If you choose a unit, the EOI is usually adjusted against the booking amount. If you don’t find a suitable unit, the EOI terms decide whether and when you get a refund.

EOI amount and EOI cheque

The EOI amount is the sum you pay with the EOI form. Developers set it per project, often as a fixed amount for each configuration (for example one figure for a 2 BHK and a higher one for a 3 BHK). There is no standard figure across the market, so compare it with the unit price and check the written terms.

An EOI cheque is simply the EOI amount paid by cheque, made out to the developer's company (not to a person or a broker). An EOI payment can also be made by bank transfer. Either way, ask for a receipt that names the project, the amount and your preferred configuration.

One legal limit applies once you book: under Section 13 of the RERA Act, the developer can't take more than 10% of the unit's cost as an advance or application fee before signing and registering an agreement for sale. If the EOI amount is adjusted against your booking, it counts towards that advance.

EOI vs token amount vs booking amount

TermWhen it is paidWhat you get
EOIBefore or at launchPriority to choose a unit; no specific unit yet
Token amountAfter selecting a unitThat unit held for you for a short time while paperwork is done
Booking amountWhen you confirm the purchaseAllotment of the unit, followed by the agreement for sale

Developers use these terms slightly differently, so read the receipt and application form rather than relying on the name.

Is EOI refundable?

Often yes, but only on the conditions written in the EOI terms. Common patterns are a full refund if you don’t get a unit you want at allotment, and a deduction or forfeiture if you withdraw after selecting a unit. What matters is what is in writing:

  • Is the EOI fully refundable if you don’t choose a unit?
  • How many days does the refund take, and is there any deduction?
  • Is the EOI adjusted against the booking amount if you go ahead?

If the answers are only verbal, ask for them in writing before paying.

EOI and RERA: what the law says

Two parts of the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016 matter here:

  • Section 3 says a promoter cannot advertise, market, book, sell or offer for sale any unit in a project that has to be registered, without first registering it with the state RERA authority. Collecting money for an unregistered project is therefore risky for both sides.
  • Section 13 says a promoter cannot take more than 10% of the cost of the unit as an advance or application fee without first entering into a written agreement for sale and registering it.

So before paying an EOI, check that the project has a RERA registration number and look it up on the state RERA website. EOIs are usually collected in a project's pre-launch phase, which RERA only allows after registration.

Checklist before paying an EOI

  • The project’s RERA registration number, checked on the RERA website.
  • Written EOI terms: refund conditions, timelines and deductions.
  • Payment by cheque or bank transfer to the developer’s account, never in cash.
  • A receipt that names the project, the amount and your preferred configuration.
  • Clarity on how allotment will work: first-come, draw, or floor-wise release.

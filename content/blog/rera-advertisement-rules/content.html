Under the RERA Act, 2016, a project that must be registered cannot be advertised until it is registered (Section 3), and once registered, every advertisement must prominently show the RERA registration number and the state authority's website (Section 11(2)). States add rules: UP RERA and MahaRERA require a project QR code. These rules cover hoardings, print, websites, social media and WhatsApp alike.

This guide explains what counts as an advertisement under RERA, what every ad must show, state rules for Uttar Pradesh, Maharashtra and Haryana, the duties of agents and channel partners, the penalties, and a checklist to run before anything goes live.

What counts as an advertisement under RERA?

Almost any publicity for a project counts. Section 2(b) of the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016 defines "advertisement" as any document described or issued as an advertisement through any medium, including a notice, circular or other publicity, that tells people about a project, offers a plot, building or apartment for sale, or invites people to buy or to pay an advance or deposit.

Can a builder advertise before RERA registration?

No. Section 3(1) bars advertising, marketing, booking, selling or inviting purchases in a project that must be registered until it is registered. "Pre-launch" campaigns that collect enquiries, EOIs or money for an unregistered project fall within this. Regulators keep repeating the point: in August 2026 Haryana RERA asked promoters to stop pre-launch advertising before registration and warned buyers off unregistered projects (Outlook Money).

Section 3(2) lists exemptions: land not exceeding 500 square metres or apartments not exceeding eight, including all phases (states may set lower limits); projects with a completion certificate before the Act began; and renovation or repair that doesn't involve marketing new units. What must every real estate advertisement show?

The project's RERA registration number and the authority's website address, prominently (Section 11(2)). "Prominently" means readable where the ad is seen: from the road on a hoarding, on screen long enough to read in a video, and in the image or first lines of a social post.

  1. Project RERA registration number, exactly as on the certificate.
  2. The state RERA website address (for UP: up-rera.in).
  3. The project QR code where the state requires it.
  4. The agent's RERA number on ads run by a broker or CP.
  5. An "artist's impression" label on renders that are not photographs.

Is a builder bound by what the advertisement says?

Yes. Under Section 12, if a buyer pays based on false or incorrect information in an ad, prospectus or model apartment and suffers a loss, the promoter must compensate them. If the buyer withdraws, the promoter must refund the money with interest and compensation.

So the rule for ad copy is: publish nothing you can't show in the approved plans or RERA filing. That covers amenities, possession dates, distances, specifications and "approved" claims. The ASCI Code also requires ads to be truthful and claims to be substantiated. Avoid "guaranteed returns", "best" and "No. 1" unless you can prove them.

Do channel partners and agents have to follow RERA ad rules?

Yes. A CP's ad is an ad for the project, and agents have their own duties under Sections 9 and 10.

  • Register first (Section 9). See RERA registration for agents.
  • Don't market unregistered projects (Section 10(a)).
  • No unfair trade practices (Section 10(c)), including false statements about standard, quality or approvals.
  • Show the details: the project's registration number and authority website, the QR code where required, and the agent's own number. MahaRERA's directions name agents explicitly. Copying the developer's creative without these details is a common mistake.

What is the penalty for breaking RERA advertising rules?

Promoters face penalties of up to 10% of project cost for advertising unregistered projects and up to 5% for other breaches; agents face up to ₹10,000 a day.

Other RERA questions builders ask

What is the 70/30 rule in RERA? Section 4(2)(l)(D) requires promoters to deposit 70% of money collected from buyers in a separate account used only for that project's land and construction cost. It concerns project funds, not ads. Our RERA number check guide shows how to see a project's filings.

Does RERA apply to commercial projects? Yes. The Act's definition of "apartment" includes offices, shops, showrooms and godowns, so commercial projects above the size limits follow the same rules.

Can a builder sell parking? The Act lists open parking among "common areas" and defines a "garage" separately, so open parking is treated as common area while garages can be allotted. Check your state's rules and the project's filing before putting parking in an ad.

Pre-publish checklist for every real estate ad

  1. The project is registered, and the number matches the certificate.
  2. The registration number and authority website are clearly readable.
  3. The project QR code is included where the state requires it, and it scans.
  4. Any broker or CP ad shows the agent's RERA number.
  5. Every amenity, specification and image matches the approved plans; renders are labelled.
  6. The possession date matches the RERA filing.
  7. Any price with an asterisk explains what is and isn't included.
  8. No unprovable claims: "guaranteed returns", "best", invented distances.
  9. The landing page shows the same details as the ad.

The Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016 (RERA) changed the entire industry. Today, an advertisement is no longer treated as a creative sales pitch—under RERA, every advertisement is treated as a legally binding document. If your brochure, website, Instagram post, or newspaper ad promises an amenity or possession date that is not registered with RERA, the promoter and the marketing agency can face severe statutory penalties, project deregistration, and even criminal liability.

Yet in 2026, many developers, channel partners, and digital marketing agencies still make costly compliance mistakes. State regulatory bodies—especially MahaRERA in Maharashtra, UP RERA in Uttar Pradesh, and HRERA in Haryana—regularly issue suo-motu show-cause notices and levy lakhs of rupees in fines for missing registration numbers, tiny unreadable font sizes, or absent QR codes. In this comprehensive guide, we explain the essential RERA advertising rules, Section 11(2) requirements, channel partner compliance mandates, and provide a pre-publish compliance checklist so your campaigns stay 100% legal.

Section of RERA: Why Advertisements Are Legally Binding

The core legal foundation governing property advertising is Section 11(2) of the RERA Act. It states: "The advertisement or prospectus issued or published by the promoter shall mention prominently the website address of the Authority, wherein all details of the registered project have been entered and include the registration number obtained from the Authority and such other matters incidental thereto."

Furthermore, under Section 12, if a homebuyer makes an advance payment or booking based on false or misleading information in an advertisement or model flat, the builder is legally required to refund the entire amount with interest, plus pay compensation to the buyer. If the buyer chooses not to withdraw, the promoter must deliver exactly what was advertised.

What this means in plain language:

  • You Cannot Advertise Before Getting a RERA Number: You cannot run "teaser ads," collect Expression of Interest (EOI) money, or put up "Coming Soon" hoardings for any project that requires registration until the official RERA registration certificate has been issued.
  • No Hallucinated Amenities: If your marketing brochure shows an Olympic-sized swimming pool, badminton court, or high-speed elevators, those amenities must exist in the sanctioned layout plan uploaded to the state RERA portal.
  • Carpet Area is the Only Legal Benchmark: Advertising properties based solely on misleading "Super Built-Up Area" without prominently stating the RERA-certified net usable carpet area is strictly prohibited.

Channel Partner and Broker Advertising Rules

Many real estate brokers and channel partners believe that RERA advertising regulations apply only to big developers. That is a dangerous misconception. Under Section 9 and Section 10 of RERA, real estate agents and channel partners are subject to strict advertising compliance:

  • Agent RERA Registration is Mandatory: No individual broker or firm can market, sell, or advertise any plot, apartment, or commercial building without holding a valid RERA Agent Registration certificate.
  • Dual Registration Numbers on All Ads: Whenever a channel partner publishes an advertisement (on their own social media, website, or portal listing), the ad must display both the Developer's Project RERA Number AND the Channel Partner's Agent RERA Number.
  • No Unauthorized Project Names: Brokers cannot invent catchy unofficial project names (e.g., "Noida Luxury Towers") to hide the real registered project name. The registered name as per the RERA certificate must be clearly stated.
  • Possession of Written Authorization: An agent must have written authorization from the promoter to market the project. Running unauthorized digital campaigns to capture leads for resale or co-broking is illegal.

Rules for Outdoor Billboards and Newspaper Ads

Because physical billboards and print newspapers have high public visibility, authorities enforce strict typography and layout rules:

  • The Font Size Rule (Legibility Mandate): In states like Maharashtra and Uttar Pradesh, regulatory orders stipulate that the RERA registration number and official website URL must be printed in a font size that is easily readable by an ordinary person from a reasonable distance. Penalties and Real Enforcement Case Studies

Ignoring RERA advertisement rules carries severe financial and operational consequences under the law:

Statutory Penalties Under Section 61 and Section 62:

  • For Promoters (Section 61): Failure to comply with RERA provisions attracts a penalty of up to 5% of the estimated cost of the real estate project as determined by the Authority. For a ₹200 Crore development, that is a potential penalty of up to ₹10 Crores.
  • For Channel Partners (Section 62): An agent who markets an unregistered project or publishes non-compliant ads faces a penalty of ₹10,000 per day for the duration of the default, up to 5% of the total cost of the plot or apartment sold.
  • Deregistration and Revocation: In cases of repeated violations or deliberate misrepresentation of possession dates, the Authority can revoke project registration entirely, freezing the project's escrow bank accounts.
Similarly, UP RERA has issued strict penalties against promoters in Greater Noida and Lucknow for marketing pre-launch phases on social media before receiving final sanction approvals.

The Pre-Publish Compliance Checklist: 5 Steps to Protect Your Brand

Before your creative team or digital agency hits "Publish" or sends files to the print press, run every creative asset through this five-point compliance audit:

  1. Registration Number Check: Is the exact RERA registration number printed accurately without typos? Is it accompanied by the state authority's official URL?
  2. QR Code Functionality: Scan the QR code on the creative with a smartphone. Does it immediately load the official government RERA project page? (Ensure it does not redirect to your marketing landing page).
  3. Carpet Area Disclosure: If sizes are mentioned, is the RERA carpet area clearly specified alongside any super area references?
  4. Legibility on Mobile Screens: For digital ads, zoom out to mobile phone screen size. Can an average user clearly read the RERA number without straining?
  5. Partner Registration: If the ad is published by an agency or broker, is their Agent RERA number displayed alongside the developer's project number?

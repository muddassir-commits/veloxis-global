To register as a channel partner, first get real estate agent registration from your state RERA authority, then fill in each developer's CP empanelment form with your PAN, address proof, bank details, RERA certificate and GST details, and sign its CP agreement. Only then can you register buyers and claim brokerage on bookings.

This guide is for brokers and small brokerage firms who want to sell new projects for developers. It walks through the registration process step by step: the RERA part, the documents developers ask for, the empanelment form, the clauses to read in a CP agreement, and the brokerage payout terms that decide when you actually get paid. If you are new to the term itself, start with our explainer on the channel partner in real estate.

How does channel partner registration with a developer work?

It is a two-level process: a legal registration with the state RERA authority, then a commercial registration (empanelment) with every developer you want to sell for. RERA registration is done once per state. Empanelment is done once per developer, and sometimes per project.

  1. Register as an agent with your state RERA and get your agent registration number.
  2. Prepare one document folder (PAN, address proof, photo, bank proof, RERA certificate, GST certificate if any, firm papers if you are not an individual).
  3. Fill in the developer's CP registration form, online or on paper.
  4. Read and sign the CP agreement or accept the developer's channel partner terms.
  5. Get your CP code or portal login, plus the lead registration process for each project.
  6. Register each buyer before the first site visit and follow the accompany rules.
  7. Raise your invoice once the payout milestone in the agreement is reached.

Developers treat the two levels as separate. One large Mumbai developer's published channel partner terms say a partner must be registered as an agent under RERA and that the CP form must be submitted before any marketing or invoicing. Signing up with a developer does not replace the RERA registration, and RERA registration alone does not make you an approved partner of any developer.

Step 1: Get RERA agent registration in your state

Yes, you need it first: Section 9(1) of the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016 bars any agent from facilitating a sale in a registered project without registering with the state authority. Section 9(3) gives one registration for the whole state, so a UP RERA number covers Noida, Lucknow and Kanpur, but not Gurugram, which falls under Haryana RERA.

What documents do developers ask for in CP registration?

Most developers ask for the same core set: PAN, address proof, a photograph, a cancelled cheque or passbook for payouts, and your RERA agent certificate, with GST and company papers where they apply. A Chennai developer's public CP registration page, for example, marks photo, PAN, address proof and a cancelled cheque or passbook as mandatory, lists RERA, GST and CIN certificates as further uploads, and limits each file to 2 MB.

Keep every name exactly the same across PAN, RERA certificate, bank account and GST registration. A mismatch is the most common reason a payout is held back later.

How do you fill in a developer's CP empanelment form?

Fill it in under the same legal name and business type as your RERA registration, and give the developer one person and one phone number for leads and payouts. Typical fields are the firm or individual name, the business type (proprietorship, partnership, private or public limited), contact details, PAN, office address, RERA numbers for each state you cover, GST number and bank details.

  • Choose the right entity. If you invoice as a proprietorship, empanel as one. Changing later means fresh paperwork and can delay pending payouts.
  • List every state RERA number you hold. Some developers sell in several states and check the number for each project's state.
  • Use a business email you check daily. Lead confirmations, rate changes and payout notices usually arrive there.
  • Save the acknowledgement and your CP code. You will quote it on every lead and invoice.

Before you sign up for a project, run a RERA number check on it. Section 10 of the RERA Act forbids an agent from facilitating the sale of a project that is not registered.

What should you check in a CP agreement before signing?

Read the clauses that decide whether a booking counts as yours and when you are paid: lead validity, accompany rules, payout milestone, payment timeline, cancellation, commission base and marketing rules. Agreements differ a lot between developers, as the two published sets of terms below show.

Which brokerage payout terms matter most?

The milestone and the commission base matter more than the headline rate, because they decide how much you get and how long you wait. Check these points in writing:

  • Commission base. Is the rate applied to the basic price only, or the full agreement value? The Chennai example above calculates brokerage on the basic price and excludes floor rise, car parking, legal charges, connection charges and taxes.
  • Milestone. Booking, agreement for sale, a percentage of the price received, or registration. A later milestone means a longer wait and more risk of cancellation.
  • Rate changes. Can the developer change rates monthly, and does the rate on the date of booking apply?
  • Self-bookings and relatives. Many terms pay nothing when you or your family buy.
  • Clawback. If a buyer cancels after you are paid, will the amount be recovered from you or from future payouts?
  • Tax. GST on your invoice and TDS on the commission; our guide to real estate broker commission in India explains both.

Also check the marketing clause. The Mumbai terms allow only developer-supplied material and require written approval before a partner uses the developer's brand, logo or project images on websites, WhatsApp or social media. Any ad you run must also follow the RERA advertisement rules.

How do you register leads after empanelment?

Register every buyer through the developer's portal, app or the email address it gives you, before the first site visit, and keep the confirmation. The registration time is usually what settles a dispute when the same buyer also reached the developer through an ad or another partner. Log the date of first contact, the date you registered the lead and every visit. Why does CP registration or payout get stuck?

Most problems come from paperwork that doesn't match and leads that weren't registered in time.

  • Selling before your RERA agent registration is approved, or after it has lapsed.
  • Different names or addresses on PAN, RERA certificate, GST and bank account.
  • Bringing a buyer to the site before the lead is confirmed.
  • Missing the lead validity window and losing the booking to another source.
  • Invoicing before the milestone in the agreement is reached.
  • Using project photos or the developer's logo in ads without written approval.

The risk is not only commercial. Under Section 62 of the RERA Act, an agent who breaks Sections 9 or 10 can face a penalty of up to ₹10,000 for each day of default, up to 5% of the cost of the unit whose sale was facilitated (RERA FAQ published by UP RERA).

What should you do after you are registered?

Put your RERA agent number everywhere you market, and build a source of buyers that doesn't depend only on the developer. Leads shared by developers and portals go to many partners at once, so the partners who win usually reply first and have enquiries no one else has. A project page under your own brand, ads within the developer's rules and instant WhatsApp replies help with both.

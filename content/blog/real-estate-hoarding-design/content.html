A good real estate hoarding shows five things: one short headline, one strong image, the project name and logo, one call to action (a large WhatsApp number, short web address or QR code) and a readable RERA registration number with the authority's website. Everything else, such as amenities, floor plans and payment plans, belongs on the project page and brochure.

A real estate hoarding is a large outdoor advertising board, on a roadside, junction, building wall or the project's own boundary, that promotes a property project or broker. It has a few seconds to make someone in a moving car or auto remember a project name and know what to do next. This guide covers the types of hoarding, what to write on one, design rules, 12 ideas, the RERA and permission rules, and how to track which boards bring site visits.

What does a real estate hoarding need to do?

A hoarding must get noticed, get remembered and get acted on, in a few seconds. Outdoor advertising guides commonly put the viewing window at roughly three to five seconds for a driver (Blip billboard guidelines). In that time a good board does three jobs:

  1. Gets noticed: one strong image or bold line that stands out from other boards on the same road.
  2. Gets remembered: the project name and one reason to care, such as location, price band or a standout feature.
  3. Gets acted on: one easy next step, like a WhatsApp number or short web address.

The board starts the conversation; the page, the real estate brochure and the WhatsApp reply finish it.

What are the types of real estate hoarding?

The main types are roadside billboards, unipoles, site hoardings, directional boards, wall branding and transit or mall panels. Each suits a different stage and budget.

What should you write on a real estate hoarding?

Write one short headline, then add the image, name, one call to action and the RERA details, in that order of size.

  1. One headline of about seven words or fewer, the limit outdoor guides commonly recommend for a main message (Blip). "2 & 3 BHK on Shaheed Path" beats a paragraph. For lines, see our real estate slogans.
  2. One image: the best render or a single lifestyle shot, not a collage.
  3. The project name and logo, large enough to read from the road.
  4. One call to action: a WhatsApp number written large, or a short web address.
  5. The RERA registration number and authority website, legible, plus a QR code where the state requires one.

What are the design rules for a property hoarding?

Design for distance, speed and glare: big type, high contrast, one focal point and plenty of empty space.

  • Size the type for distance. Print the design small, step back until it is hard to read, and see what survives. The headline and number must.
  • High contrast, few colours. Dark on light or the reverse; two brand colours plus white. No text over busy parts of a render.
  • Bold, simple fonts. No thin scripts. Keep the font consistent with the brochure and page; see real estate branding.
  • One focal point. The eye should land on image or headline first, then name, then number.
  • Leave empty space. Crowded edges are the most common flaw on property boards.
  • Plan for night. Check the design under the actual lighting.
  • Use the language of the road. In Lucknow, Kanpur and much of UP, a short Hindi or Hinglish headline can be read faster by many passers-by.

12 real estate hoarding ideas you can adapt

These are formats, not finished ads. For digital versions, see our real estate ad examples.

  1. The location line: "10 minutes from [landmark]" with the project name.
  2. The configuration and price band: "3 BHK from ₹X lakh*", with the asterisk explained on the page. Only a price you can honour on visit day.
  3. The possession promise: "Ready to move" or "Possession [month year]", only if it matches the RERA filing.
  4. The single standout amenity, shown big.
  5. The sequence board: three boards on one route, each line building on the last, ending at the site.
  6. The countdown: "Launching soon" then "Now open for site visits", only after RERA registration.
  7. The WhatsApp-first board: the number in very large type with "WhatsApp 'Hi' for price and floor plans".
  8. The progress board: update the site hoarding with construction milestones.
  9. The directional series: arrows and distances from the main road to the gate.
  10. The local language headline for the catchment area.
  11. The question: "Still paying rent?" or "Home before Diwali?".
  12. The QR panel at a waiting spot that opens the project page with the price sheet.

Is the RERA number mandatory on a hoarding?

Yes. A hoarding is an advertisement, so the RERA Act requires the project's registration number and the authority's website on it, prominently. Section 11(2) of the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016 says every advertisement by a promoter must prominently mention the authority's website and include the registration number. Section 3 bars any advertising before registration. States add more:

  • Uttar Pradesh: since July 2024 UP RERA requires each project's unique QR code in brochures and advertisements (PTI via The Week).
  • Maharashtra: MahaRERA's April 2025 directions require the registration number and a scannable QR code on all ads, including hoardings, with the QR in the top right corner and the registration details in a font at least as large as the contact details; violations can attract penalties of up to ₹50,000 (The Realty Today).

Also check before printing:

  • Agent numbers: if a broker or CP puts up the board, add their RERA agent number too.
  • Claims match the filing: possession dates, areas and amenities must match what is registered.
  • Local permission: outdoor ads usually need permission from the municipal corporation or development authority under its advertising rules. Media owners who rent sites normally handle it, but confirm it, including for boards on your own boundary wall.

How do you track which hoarding brings leads?

Give every board its own response route, send it to a matching page, and reply in seconds.

  1. One response route per location: a separate WhatsApp number or pre-filled message ("Hi, I saw your board on Kanpur Road"), and a tagged short link or QR code.
  2. A matching page, not the homepage: same image and headline, then price band, floor plans, RERA number and a site-visit button; see our project landing page blueprint.
  3. An instant reply: someone who messages at 8 PM should get the brochure and a visit slot straight away; see WhatsApp automation for real estate leads.
  4. Compare after a month: enquiries and site visits per board, then move budget to routes that work.

What are common real estate hoarding mistakes?

  • Too many words.
  • An unreadable RERA number, or a missing QR code where required.
  • Three phone numbers instead of one.
  • A render that doesn't match the site.
  • No tracking per board.
  • Old launch boards left up after the offer ended.

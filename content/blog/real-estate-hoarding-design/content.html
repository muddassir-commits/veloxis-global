A real estate hoarding has a few seconds to do one job: make a person in a moving car or auto remember a project name and know what to do next. Most property hoardings in Indian cities fail at it. They try to say everything, the RERA number is unreadable, and nothing on the board tells the viewer where to go. This guide covers real estate hoarding design that works: the types of hoarding and when to use each, what to put on the board and what to leave off, design rules for distance and speed, 12 hoarding ideas you can adapt, the RERA and permission rules, and how to connect every board to a page and a WhatsApp number so you know which ones bring site visits.

What a real estate hoarding has to do

A hoarding is not a brochure on a pole. A driver sees it for a few seconds, often at an angle, often through traffic. In that time a good board does three things:

  1. Gets noticed. One strong image or one bold line that stands out from the other boards on the same road.
  2. Gets remembered. The project name and one reason to care: location, price or a single standout feature.
  3. Gets acted on. One short, easy next step: a WhatsApp number, a short web address or a QR code for people stopped at a signal.

Everything else, such as the amenity list, the floor plans and the payment plan, belongs on the project page and the real estate brochure, not on the hoarding. The board's job is to start the conversation; the page and the WhatsApp reply finish it.

What to put on a real estate hoarding (and what to leave off)

Keep the board to five elements, in this order of size:

  1. One headline of about six or seven words. A common rule of thumb for outdoor ads is that drivers can take in roughly seven words. “2 & 3 BHK on Shaheed Path” beats a paragraph about lifestyle.
  2. One image: the best render of the building or a single lifestyle shot. Not a collage.
  3. The project name and logo, large enough to read from the road.
  4. One call to action: a WhatsApp number written large, or a short web address. A QR code only where people stop.
  5. The RERA registration number and authority website, legible, as the law requires (more on this below).

Leave off: the full amenity list, multiple phone numbers, the price of every configuration, legal small print nobody can read, and the developer's entire project history. If the board needs more than five elements, you are trying to make it do the landing page's job.

Real estate hoarding design rules that work

These rules come from how outdoor ads are viewed: from a distance, at speed, often in glare or at night.

  • Size the type for distance. Test it: print the design small, step back to where it is unreadable, and see what survives. The headline and phone number must survive.
  • High contrast, few colours. Dark text on a light background or the reverse. Two brand colours plus white is plenty. Avoid text over busy parts of a render.
  • Bold, simple fonts. No thin scripts or condensed fonts for anything that must be read. Keep the font consistent with the brochure and page so buyers recognise the project everywhere.
  • One focal point. The eye should land on the image or the headline first, then the name, then the number. If everything shouts, nothing is read.
  • Leave empty space. Space around the headline makes it readable faster. Crowding the edges is the most common mistake on Indian property boards.
  • Plan for night. If the board is lit, check the design under the actual lighting. Dark colours can disappear at night.
  • Write in the language of the road. In Lucknow, Kanpur and much of UP, a Hindi headline or a Hindi-English mix can be read faster than formal English by many passing buyers.

12 real estate hoarding ideas you can adapt

These are formats, not finished ads. Each one can be adapted to your project, city and budget. For digital versions of the same ideas, see our real estate ad examples.

  1. The location line. “10 minutes from [landmark]” with the project name. Distance to a known place sells faster than adjectives.
  2. The configuration and price band. “3 BHK from ₹X lakh*” with the asterisk explained on the page. Only use a price you can honour on the day of the visit.
  3. The possession promise. “Ready to move” or “Possession [month year]”, only if it matches the RERA filing.
  4. The single standout amenity. One thing, shown big: the clubhouse, the park view, the school inside the township.
  5. The sequence board. Three boards on the same route, each with one line that builds on the last, ending at the site entrance.
  6. The countdown. “Launching in 30 days” followed by a swap to “Now open for site visits”. Works well with an EOI campaign online.
  7. The WhatsApp-first board. The number in very large type with “WhatsApp ‘Hi’ for price and floor plans”. Easy to remember and easy to track.
  8. The site hoarding as a progress board. Update the boundary hoarding with construction milestones. It shows the project is real and moving.
  9. The directional series. Simple arrows and distances from the main road to the site gate, so site visitors never get lost.
  10. The local language headline. A short Hindi line for the catchment area, with the project name in the brand font.
  11. The question. “Still paying rent?” or “Home before Diwali?”, followed by the project name and number. Questions get a second look.
  12. The QR panel at a waiting spot. At bus shelters, metro stations and mall panels, a QR code that opens the project page with the price sheet.

RERA rules and permissions for hoardings

Under Section 11(2) of the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016, every advertisement by a promoter must prominently mention the website address of the RERA authority and include the project's registration number. A hoarding is an advertisement, so the number has to be on it and readable, not hidden in tiny type at the bottom. Some state authorities have added further requirements, such as a QR code linking to the project's registration page; Maharashtra is the best-known example. Our guide on the RERA number check explains the formats and where the number must appear.

A few other points to check before printing:

  • Agent numbers too. If a channel partner or broker puts up the board, their own RERA agent registration number should appear with the project number.
  • Claims must match the filing. Possession dates, areas and amenities on the board should match what is registered. “Approved” claims must be true.
  • Local permission. Outdoor advertising usually needs permission from the local body, such as the municipal corporation or development authority, under its advertising rules. Media owners who rent hoarding sites normally handle this, but check it is in place, including for hoardings on your own boundary wall.

Connect every hoarding to a page and WhatsApp

The biggest weakness of hoardings is that nobody knows which ones work. You can fix that with three simple steps:

  1. Give each location its own way to respond. A separate WhatsApp number or a pre-filled WhatsApp message per board (“Hi, I saw your board on Kanpur Road”) and a short URL or QR code that points to the project page with a tag for that board.
  2. Send people to a page, not the homepage. The board's promise should continue on the screen: same image, same headline, then the price band, floor plans, RERA number and a site-visit button. That page is a real estate landing page built for one project, and the landing page conversion hacks guide covers what makes it work.
  3. Reply in seconds. A person who messages after seeing a board at 8 PM should get the brochure and a visit slot straight away, not the next afternoon. WhatsApp automation for real estate leads makes that automatic.

After a month you can compare boards by the enquiries and site visits each one produced, and move budget to the routes that work. Our project landing page blueprint shows how the page behind a board is laid out.

Common real estate hoarding mistakes

  • Too many words. If it takes more than a glance to read, it is not read.
  • An unreadable RERA number. It is a legal requirement, and buyers look for it.
  • Three phone numbers. Pick one, ideally a WhatsApp number, and make it big.
  • A render that does not match the site. Buyers who visit and see something different stop trusting everything else.
  • Boards with no tracking. Without a separate number or link per board, you can't tell a good location from a bad one.
  • Leaving an old board up. A launch board with a sold-out offer, still up months later, tells buyers the project is not managed well.

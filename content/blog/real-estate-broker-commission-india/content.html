There is no national, government-fixed real estate broker commission rate in India. In resale, rental and land deals the fee is whatever the parties agree in writing; in new projects the developer pays the channel partner. RERA sets no rate. Haryana is the exception: its rules allow 1% of the agreed price, half from each side.

This guide explains broker commission rates in India by deal type, who pays, land broker commission, how developers pay channel partners, the RERA, GST and TDS rules, and how to put the deal in writing so nobody argues later.

How much commission do real estate brokers charge in India?

It depends on the deal type and is set by agreement, not by a national law. Market practice and published rules look like this:

Deal typeHow the fee is usually setWho usually pays
Resale flat or houseA percentage of the sale price, negotiatedBuyer, seller or both
New project (primary sale)A percentage set in the developer's CP agreementThe developer
Residential rentalLinked to the monthly rent, agreed before the visitTenant, owner or both
Commercial leasingLinked to the rent, agreed in writingOwner, tenant or both

Because rates are negotiated, they vary by city, ticket size and how much work the broker does. Treat any "standard rate" you are quoted as a starting point for negotiation, not a legal figure.

Are broker commission rates fixed state-wise?

Only Haryana has a clear published cap that we could verify; elsewhere the rate is contractual. Rule 10 of the Haryana Regulation of Property Dealers and Consultants Rules, 2009, made under the Haryana Regulation of Property Dealers and Consultants Act, 2008, allows commission of 1% of the agreed consideration value, paid half by the seller and half by the buyer. In October 2020 HRERA said it would issue restraint orders to promoters and brokers in registered projects so they do not charge more than this (The Tribune).

For Uttar Pradesh, Delhi, Maharashtra and other states we found no state rule fixing the brokerage percentage as of 5 November 2026. In those states, RERA regulates who may act as an agent and how they behave, but the fee is agreed between the parties. If you deal in Gurugram or Faridabad, check the current HRERA position before agreeing a fee.

In resale the parties pay the broker; in new projects the developer pays the CP.

For new projects, commission is usually a percentage of the agreement value or basic sale price, sometimes with higher slabs for more bookings or for selling during a launch window. Compare offers line by line, not by the headline percentage.

Who pays the broker: buyer, seller or developer?

The developer pays on new projects; whoever signed up with the broker pays on resale and rentals.

New project: the developer. When you visit with a channel partner (CP), the developer pays that CP. If a CP asks you for brokerage on a new-project booking, ask why in writing.

Resale: often both sides pay, because the broker has worked for both: finding the property or the buyer, checking documents, negotiating and helping with registration. Agree the percentage, who pays and when it is due before the first visit.

Rental: the tenant, the owner or both, as agreed in advance.

How much is land broker commission?

Land broker commission is a negotiated percentage of the sale price, usually paid on registration of the sale deed, and there is no national rate. Land deals differ from flat sales in ways that affect the fee:

  • More legwork. The broker often traces title, checks land records and mutation, confirms land use and helps with measurement and boundaries. Agree what work is included.
  • Who pays. Buyer, seller or both, by agreement. In Haryana, the 1% rule above is written for property deals generally, so check with HRERA or a lawyer how it applies to your land deal.
  • RERA may not apply. A single agricultural or private plot sale is usually outside RERA, but plots in a registered plotted development are inside it, and the agent must then be RERA-registered.
  • Payment in stages. For large land deals, commission is often split: part at agreement to sell, the rest at registry. Write the stages down.

For sellers, brokerage paid on a sale is an expense incurred in connection with the transfer, which the capital gains rules generally allow to be deducted when supported by records; keep the invoice and bank proof and confirm the treatment with your chartered accountant. How do developers pay channel partners?

Through a written CP agreement that sets the rate, slabs, payout milestone, lead rules and cancellation terms.

  1. Base commission: a percentage of the agreement value or basic price for each booking.
  2. Slabs or incentives: a higher rate after a set number of bookings, or for launch-period bookings.
  3. Payout milestones: for example after the buyer pays a set share of the price or after the agreement for sale is registered.
  4. Lead registration rules: how a buyer is credited to a CP and how disputes are settled.
  5. Cancellation terms: what happens to commission if the buyer cancels.

Payout timing matters as much as the rate: a high percentage paid only at possession can be worth less to a small broker than a lower rate paid at registration.

What do RERA rules say about broker commission?

The RERA Act doesn't fix commission; it requires agents to register and sets their duties and penalties. Under the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016:

  • Section 9: an agent must register with the state RERA authority before facilitating the sale or purchase of units in a project that has to be registered. See RERA registration for agents.
  • Section 10: a registered agent must not facilitate sales in unregistered projects, must keep books and records, must not use unfair trade practices such as false statements, and must help buyers get the documents they are entitled to.
  • Section 62: an agent who breaks Section 9 or 10 can be fined up to ₹10,000 for every day of default, up to 5% of the cost of the unit.

A RERA-compliant broker is one who is registered in the state, quotes the registration number on ads and invoices, and only markets registered projects. Developers check this before signing a CP agreement, and an unregistered broker is in a weak position if a payout is disputed.

Is GST and TDS charged on brokerage?

Yes: brokerage is a taxable service at 18% GST, and TDS applies to commission at 2% above ₹20,000 a year.

GST. A broker must register once yearly turnover crosses the threshold for services (₹20 lakh in most states, ₹10 lakh in special category states) and then adds GST to the invoice. Check current rates on the CBIC GST portal.

TDS. From 1 April 2026 the Income-tax Act, 2025 replaced the 1961 Act, and the rule that was Section 194H now sits in Section 393(1). As published for FY 2026-27, the rate is 2% once commission paid to one person crosses ₹20,000 in the year, and 20% without a PAN. The rate was cut from 5% to 2% from 1 October 2024, and the threshold rose from ₹15,000 to ₹20,000 from 1 April 2025. Confirm current figures on the Income Tax Department portal.

What is a 70/30 commission split?

It is how a brokerage firm and its agent share commission: the agent keeps 70% and the firm keeps 30%. The firm's share pays for office, leads, software and support. The term comes mainly from the US; in India, firm-agent arrangements range from salary plus incentive to a share of each deal. Whatever the model, write it down.

Should developers rely on brokers or their own ads?

Most developers use both and compare cost per booking. Channel partners bring buyers who already trust them; your own ads bring buyers and data that only you hold. To compare fairly, add up CP commission for a quarter, and separately ad spend plus agency and tools for the same quarter, then divide each by the bookings it produced. How do you agree commission without disputes?

Put the rate, payer, due date and scope in writing before the first visit.

  1. Check the broker's RERA agent registration and quote it in the agreement.
  2. Write the percentage or fixed fee, who pays it and the event that makes it due (agreement to sell, registry, rent agreement).
  3. List the work included: visits, document checks, negotiation, registry support.
  4. Pay by bank transfer against a proper invoice, with GST if the broker is registered. Avoid cash.
  5. For developers: deduct TDS, keep records and write the lead-registration and cancellation rules into the CP agreement.

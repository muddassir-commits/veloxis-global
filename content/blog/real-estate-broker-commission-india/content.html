Who pays the broker: buyer, seller, or developer?

One of the most frequent points of confusion for first-time homebuyers is who actually cuts the cheque for brokerage. The answer comes down to whether the inventory is fresh or pre-owned.

In new project launches, buyers should never pay brokerage. Builders engage channel partners as an extended sales force. When you visit a project site office accompanied by an agent, or register your details through an agent’s link, that agent is compensated entirely by the developer. If an agent asks a homebuyer for a commission fee on an under-construction builder project, that is non-standard practice, and buyers should decline.

In secondary resale transactions, both parties usually pay. Because the broker sources the property from the owner, verifies physical documents, negotiates price differentials, coordinates society NOCs, and assists with stamp duty registration, both buyer and seller are benefiting from an independent intermediary service.

RERA rules on broker commission and agent registration

While RERA does not cap commission percentages, it establishes strict operational boundaries:

  • Mandatory Agent Registration (Section 9): Under Section 9 of the RERA Act, no real estate agent or channel partner can legally facilitate the sale of any plot, apartment, or commercial building in a registered project without obtaining an official RERA agent registration number from the state authority. Our guide on RERA registration for agents outlines the exact fee structures across UP RERA, MahaRERA, and HRERA.
  • Advertising Compliance (Section 10): Agents cannot market or publish brochures, newspaper ads, or social media promotions for any project that does not prominently display the project’s valid RERA registration number and the state RERA website portal.
  • Unfair Trade Practices: Under Section 10(c), an agent who falsely promises amenities, guarantees unrealistic rental returns, or misrepresents possession timelines can have their license revoked and face financial penalties of up to ₹10,000 per day of ongoing default.
  • Right to Commission: Only a RERA-registered broker has legal standing to enforce commission recovery before RERA tribunals or civil courts in the event of a builder non-payment dispute. Unregistered agents operating on informal handshake agreements have zero protection under the law.

Taxes on brokerage:GST and SectionH TDS

Real estate brokerage in India is a taxable service. Both individual consultants and corporate brokerage agencies must account for two mandatory direct and indirect tax provisions:

1. If a broker is not GST-registered, developers operating under corporate compliance will often handle payouts under reverse charge or require formal registration.

2. Tax Deducted at Source (TDS under Section 194H): When a developer or company pays brokerage exceeding ₹15,000 in a financial year to a resident agent, they must deduct 5% TDS at the time of credit or payment under Section 194H of the Income Tax Act. (This was temporarily reduced to 3.75% during pandemic relief, but returned to the standard 5% rate). The broker receives 95% of the payment directly and claims the 5% TDS credit in their annual Form 26AS when filing income tax returns.

How to negotiate brokerage rates and protect your margins

Whether you are a homebuyer purchasing an apartment, a builder engaging a broker network, or an agent pitching a developer, negotiation hinges on clarity of terms before paperwork is signed:

For Builders:

  1. Insist on RERA Numbers on Invoices: Never issue a payment without verifying the agent’s active RERA registration certificate. Non-compliant payouts expose your company to regulatory scrutiny.
  2. Include Cancellation Clawback Clauses: If a buyer defaults or withdraws under RERA before construction begins, your agency agreement must state that the unearned commission is adjusted against future bookings.

For Homebuyers:

  1. Never Pay Brokerage on New Projects: If visiting a builder site, ensure you register directly at the reception or clarify in writing that the accompanying consultant is compensated by the builder.
  2. Get Invoices with GST: Always pay brokerage via RTGS/NEFT against a formal tax invoice. Cash brokerage payments leave you with zero proof if title disputes or document retrieval issues arise later.

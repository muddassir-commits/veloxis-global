When someone types “3 BHK in Sector 150 Noida”, “ready to move flats in Gomti Nagar” or your project’s name into Google, they have already decided what they want. Google Ads lets you appear at that moment with the right price, location and a way to enquire. Searches for Google Ads for real estate have almost doubled in India over the past year, yet most property campaigns still waste much of their budget on renters, job-seekers and portal browsers. This guide explains how to set up Google Ads for real estate in India so the budget goes to buyers.

Why Google Ads works for real estate

  • Intent. People searching for a locality, configuration or project name are further along than someone scrolling Instagram.
  • Exclusive leads. Unlike portal enquiries, a lead from your own ad goes only to you.
  • Control. You choose the searches you appear for, the areas you target and what you pay for each lead.

The limit is demand: Google can only show your ad when people search. For a brand-new launch with an unknown name, Meta usually builds interest first — see Meta ads for real estate in India and our Google vs Meta comparison.

Keyword groups that bring buyers

Organise campaigns by what the buyer is searching for, with separate ad groups so each ad matches the search:

  • Project name: your project, with and without the builder’s name. Usually the cheapest, highest-converting searches once the project is known.
  • Locality + configuration: “2 BHK flats in [locality]”, “3 BHK in [sector]”, “flats near [metro station]”.
  • Property type and stage: “ready to move flats in [city]”, “new launch projects in [area]”, “plots in [locality]”.
  • Builder brand: people searching for the developer’s name and other projects.

Some advertisers also bid on nearby competing projects’ names. Check your legal position first and never use another company’s trademark in your ad text.

Negative keywords save the most money

Property searches attract a lot of clicks from people who will never buy a new flat. Add negative keywords before launch and review the search terms report every week. Common ones:

  • Renting: rent, rental, PG, hostel, lease, roommate
  • Jobs and study: job, jobs, salary, vacancy, course, internship
  • Wrong stage: resale (for new launches), old, second hand, auction
  • Information seekers: news, map, meaning, pdf, free
  • Portals if you don’t want to pay for people looking for a portal by name

Location settings most accounts get wrong

Google’s default location option includes people who are merely interested in your area, not only those in it. For most projects, choose “Presence: people in or regularly in your included locations” and target the city and surrounding areas your buyers come from.

Use “presence or interest” deliberately when you want buyers from elsewhere — for example NRI buyers searching for property in your city, in a separate campaign with its own budget and ads.

Writing search ads for property

  • Match the search: the project ad group’s ad names the project; the locality ad group’s ad names the locality.
  • Price band and configuration in the headline: “2 & 3 BHK from ₹X lakh*”.
  • Trust: “RERA registered”, possession date, the developer’s name.
  • Assets: sitelinks (floor plans, price sheet, location, site visit), a call asset for mobile, and a lead form asset if you want enquiries without a page visit.

Every ad for a registered project should be consistent with the RERA registration, and the landing page should show the registration number. More copy formulas are in our real estate ad examples.

Send clicks to a project landing page

A search ad that lands on the homepage or a portal listing wastes the intent you paid for. The landing page should repeat what the ad promised — project, locality, price band, RERA details — with a short form and a WhatsApp button, and load fast on a phone. Our landing page checklist covers what to include.

Tracking and bidding on real outcomes

  1. Track every enquiry: form submissions, calls from ads, WhatsApp clicks and lead form assets, each as a conversion.
  2. Set up enhanced conversions for leads or offline conversion import, so qualified leads and site visits from your CRM are reported back to Google.
  3. Start with Maximise conversions, then move to a target cost per acquisition once there is enough conversion data.
  4. Optimise on the deeper goal: once enough site visits are reported, make “site visit” or “qualified lead” the conversion Google bids for, rather than every form fill.

Budget and what to expect

Cost per click and cost per lead vary a lot by city, locality and ticket size, so treat any number you read online with caution. What matters more is giving each campaign enough budget to collect data — a campaign that only gets a handful of clicks a day can’t learn — and judging it on cost per site visit, not clicks or leads.

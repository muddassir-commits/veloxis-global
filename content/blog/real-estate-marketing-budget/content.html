The most reliable way to set a real estate marketing budget is to work backwards from your sales target and your own cost per booking: bookings needed × cost per booking = budget. Industry "percentage of revenue" figures get quoted a lot, but we haven't found a published, sourced benchmark for Indian projects, and costs vary widely by city, segment and channel. If you have no history yet, run a small, measured test first and let your own numbers set the budget.

This guide shows how to calculate cost per lead, cost per site visit and cost per booking, how to build a budget from them, how to split it across channels, what to spend while testing, and the mistakes that waste most of it.

How much should a builder spend on marketing?

Enough to reach the bookings you need, at a cost per booking your margins can carry. The calculation:

  1. Bookings needed in the period (for example 30 in the next six months).
  2. Your cost per booking by channel, from past campaigns (see below).
  3. Budget = bookings × cost per booking, plus creative, landing pages, tools and agency fees.
  4. Check it against the margin: what you can spend to sell one unit and still make your target profit. If the budget is higher than the project can carry, change the plan (price, channel mix, follow-up) rather than hoping.

A builder with no past data can't do step 2 yet. That's what a test budget is for.

How to calculate cost per lead, per site visit and per booking

MetricFormulaWhat it tells you
Cost per lead (CPL)Spend ÷ leadsHow cheaply a channel gets enquiries. Easy to measure, easy to mislead.
Cost per qualified leadSpend ÷ leads whose budget, configuration and timeline fitWhether the enquiries are real buyers
Cost per site visitSpend ÷ site visits doneThe most useful number for comparing channels
Cost per booking (customer acquisition cost)Spend ÷ bookingsWhat it actually costs to sell a unit

Worked example (illustration, not a real campaign): ₹1,00,000 of Meta ads brings 250 leads (CPL ₹400), of which 60 qualify, 20 visit and 2 book. Cost per visit is ₹5,000 and cost per booking is ₹50,000. A second campaign with a higher CPL but more visits can be the better buy. Always compare channels on cost per visit and per booking, not CPL.

To do this you need the source of every lead recorded through to the booking. See real estate lead management.

What is a reasonable cost per lead in Indian real estate?

There isn't one honest number. Cost per lead depends on the city, the price segment, the platform, the ad and landing page, the form type (instant forms are cheaper but lower quality) and the season. A cheap CPL with no visits is worse than an expensive one that brings buyers. Set your own baseline from the first month and judge improvements against that, not against figures quoted online without a source.

How to split a real estate marketing budget across channels

Rather than a fixed ratio, split by job and then by results:

  • Capture demand that already exists: Google search ads on the project name, builder name and locality searches. These are usually the warmest leads. Google ads guide
  • Create demand: Meta ads (Facebook and Instagram) to reach likely buyers who aren't searching yet. Meta ads guide
  • Local awareness: hoardings and print around the project for launches. Newspaper vs digital ads
  • Partners and portals: channel partner commission and portal packages, which you pay per booking or per listing. Broker commission
  • Assets that keep working: landing page, photography and video, WhatsApp automation, CRM.

For a launch with little awareness, our own planning assumption is to start with more on Meta than Google, and to start with more on Google for a known project. We explain why in Google Ads vs Meta Ads. Then, every month, move money from the channel with the highest cost per visit to the lowest.

How much to spend while testing

A test needs enough spend to produce enough leads to learn from: enough to see which ad, audience and landing page brings qualified leads and visits. We recommend at least ₹15,000 a month per project on ads as a starting point for a single project, more for a launch or a competitive market like Noida. Plan the test for four to six weeks, change one thing at a time, and decide the full budget from what it shows.

Remember the money that isn't ad spend: a project landing page, creative (photos, video, ad designs), a CRM or WhatsApp tool, and any agency fee. With Meta and Google, the ad spend is paid directly to the platform.

Budget mistakes that waste the most money

  • Judging by CPL. The cheapest leads are often the least likely to visit.
  • Slow follow-up. Paying for leads that nobody calls for a day. A Harvard Business Review study found firms that contacted online leads within an hour were nearly seven times as likely to qualify them as firms that waited longer.
  • No tracking. In our own check of 749 real estate websites in October 2026, 56% had no ad or analytics tracking of any kind (see the research). Without it you can't calculate cost per visit.
  • Sending ads to a homepage instead of a project landing page with one clear action.
  • Spreading too thin: six channels with a little money each, none long enough to learn.
  • Spending everything at launch and nothing on retargeting the people who enquired but didn't book.

A monthly budget report for the owner

One table per month, one row per channel: spend, leads, qualified leads, site visits, bookings, cost per visit, cost per booking. Add one line of decision: what gets more money next month and what gets less. That's all a builder needs to see.

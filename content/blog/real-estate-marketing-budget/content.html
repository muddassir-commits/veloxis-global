The most reliable way to set a real estate marketing budget is to work backwards from your sales target and your own cost per booking: bookings needed × cost per booking = media budget, plus creative, tools and fees. Check it against the margin per unit. If you have no history yet, run a small measured test first and let your own numbers set the budget.

This guide is for developers, builders and brokers planning a real estate development marketing budget for a project, or a yearly budget for an agency or brokerage. It covers how much to spend, what goes into a development marketing budget phase by phase, how to calculate cost per lead, site visit and booking, how to split the budget across channels, what to spend while testing, and the mistakes that waste most of it.

How much should a real estate developer spend on marketing?

Enough to reach the bookings you need, at a cost per booking your margin can carry. The calculation:

  1. Bookings needed in the period (for example 30 in the next six months).
  2. Your cost per booking by channel, from past campaigns (see the formulas below).
  3. Media budget = bookings × cost per booking.
  4. Add the non-media costs: creative, landing pages, site branding, tools, events and agency fees.
  5. Check it against the margin: what you can spend to sell one unit and still make your target profit. If the budget is more than the project can carry, change the plan (price, channel mix, follow-up) rather than hoping.
  6. Phase it across pre-launch, launch, sustenance and possession (see the next section).

A builder with no past data can't do step 2 yet. That is what a test budget is for.

Is there a standard percentage of revenue for a real estate marketing budget?

Not one we can source for India. "X% of project cost" or "X% of sales" figures get quoted a lot, but we have not found a published, methodology-backed benchmark for Indian residential projects, and the figures we did find use different bases (project cost, gross development value or sales revenue), so they can't be compared. Costs also vary widely by city, segment and channel.

If you want an outside reference point, listed Indian developers publish their annual reports, and many show advertisement and sales promotion expenses as a separate line. Dividing that line by revenue from operations for the same year gives you one company's ratio, which you can compare with your own. Treat it as context, not a target: a large brand with many projects spends very differently from a single-project builder.

What goes into a real estate development marketing budget?

A development marketing budget covers more than ads. Plan it by phase, because spending peaks at launch and should not stop after it:

PhaseTypical line itemsMain goal
Pre-launchBrand and project name, RERA-compliant brochure, renders and walkthrough, landing page, CRM and WhatsApp setup, EOI campaignBuild a list of interested buyers before launch
LaunchMeta and Google ads, hoardings near the site, print on launch day, channel partner meet, site event, sample flatTurn awareness into site visits and bookings
SustenanceAlways-on search ads, retargeting, construction progress videos, portal listings, referral programmeSteady visits at a stable cost per booking
Possession / last unitsReady-to-move search ads, offers on remaining inventory, resale and referral pushesClose out inventory

How do you calculate cost per lead, site visit and booking?

MetricFormulaWhat it tells you
Cost per lead (CPL)Spend ÷ leadsHow cheaply a channel gets enquiries. Easy to measure, easy to mislead.
Cost per qualified leadSpend ÷ leads whose budget, configuration and timeline fitWhether the enquiries are real buyers
Cost per site visitSpend ÷ site visits doneThe most useful number for comparing channels
Cost per booking (customer acquisition cost)Spend ÷ bookingsWhat it actually costs to sell a unit

Worked example (an illustration, not a real campaign): ₹1,00,000 of Meta ads brings 250 leads (CPL ₹400), of which 60 qualify, 20 visit and 2 book. Cost per visit is ₹5,000 and cost per booking is ₹50,000. A second campaign with a higher CPL but more visits can be the better buy. Always compare channels on cost per visit and per booking, not CPL.

To do this you need the source of every lead recorded through to the booking. See real estate lead management and our real estate CRM guide.

What is a reasonable cost per lead in Indian real estate?

There isn't one honest number. Cost per lead depends on the city, the price segment, the platform, the ad and landing page, the form type (instant forms make enquiring easier, which usually means cheaper but less qualified leads) and the season. We have not found a public, sourced CPL benchmark for Indian real estate. The best-known published benchmark is from the US: LocaliQ's 2026 real estate search benchmarks report a median cost per lead of $102.51 across 894 US search campaigns, which says little about a project in Noida or Lucknow. Set your own baseline in the first month and judge improvements against that.

How should a real estate marketing budget be split across channels?

Split by job first, then by results:

  • Capture demand that already exists: Google search ads on the project name, builder name and locality searches, usually the warmest leads. See the Google Ads guide.
  • Create demand: Meta ads (Facebook and Instagram) to reach likely buyers who aren't searching yet. See the Meta ads guide.
  • Local awareness: hoardings and print around the project for launches. See newspaper vs digital ads.
  • Partners and portals: channel partner commission and portal packages, paid per booking or per listing. See broker commission in India.
  • Retargeting: a small fixed share for people who visited or enquired but didn't book. See retargeting ads.
  • Assets that keep working: landing page, photography and video, WhatsApp automation, CRM.

For a launch with little awareness, start with more on Meta than Google; for a known project, start with more on Google. We explain why in Google Ads vs Meta ads. Then, every month, move money from the channel with the highest cost per visit to the lowest.

How much should you spend while testing?

Enough to produce enough leads and visits to learn from. Work it out rather than guessing:

  1. Decide how many leads you need to judge a test, for example enough for your team to see which ad and audience bring qualified leads and visits.
  2. Use an early estimate of cost per lead from the first week of spend.
  3. Test budget = leads needed × early cost per lead.
  4. Run it for four to six weeks, change one thing at a time, and decide the full budget from cost per site visit.

A launch or a competitive market needs more than a quiet locality. Remember the money that isn't ad spend: a project landing page, creative, a CRM or WhatsApp tool, and any agency fee. With Meta and Google, the ad spend is paid directly to the platform.

Which budget mistakes waste the most money?

  • Judging by CPL. The cheapest leads are often the least likely to visit.
  • Slow follow-up. A Harvard Business Review study found firms that contacted online leads within an hour were nearly seven times as likely to qualify them as firms that waited longer.
  • No tracking. In our own check of 749 real estate websites in October 2026, 56% had no ad or analytics tracking of any kind (see the research). Without it you can't calculate cost per visit.
  • Sending ads to a homepage instead of a project landing page with one clear action.
  • Spreading too thin: six channels with a little money each, none long enough to learn.
  • Spending everything at launch and nothing on sustenance and retargeting.
  • Forgetting compliance costs: every ad must carry the RERA number and state website (Section 11(2), RERA Act, 2016); reprinting non-compliant creatives wastes money.

What should a monthly marketing budget report show?

One table per month, one row per channel: spend, leads, qualified leads, site visits, bookings, cost per visit, cost per booking. Add one line of decision: what gets more money next month and what gets less. That's all a builder needs to see.

Generative engine optimization (GEO) is the work of getting your business found, quoted and named in AI answers from ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Claude, Grok, Copilot and Google's AI Overviews. In practice it means letting AI crawlers read your site, getting indexed in both Google and Bing, writing answer-first pages with specific facts, and being described the same way on other trusted websites.

This guide is for Indian business owners and marketers, with a real estate section at the end. It explains how each AI engine finds its sources (with links to each company's own documentation), the robots.txt lines to check, what llms.txt is and isn't, how to write pages that get quoted, and how to measure AI visibility for free.

What is generative engine optimization (GEO)?

GEO means making your content easy for AI tools to find, trust and cite when they write an answer. The term comes from a research paper, "GEO: Generative Engine Optimization" by researchers including teams from Princeton University and IIT Delhi, accepted at the KDD 2024 conference. They tested content changes on a benchmark of queries and reported that the best methods (such as adding citations, quotations and statistics) raised a source's visibility in generative answers by up to 40%, with results varying by subject.

Related names you will see:

TermWhat it usually means

AEO (answer engine optimization)Writing each answer so search features and assistants can lift it as it is

The short version: SEO gets you into the index, AEO makes your answer easy to lift, and GEO makes the AI trust you enough to name you. For the classic SEO side, start with our guide to real estate SEO in India.

Is GEO different from SEO?

Mostly no: GEO is SEO done thoroughly, plus a few things that matter more when an AI writes the answer. Google's own guide to optimizing for generative AI features (published May 2026) says these features rely on the same core Search systems, that original, non-commodity content matters more than any other tactic, and that you don't need to split pages into chunks or rewrite them in a special "AI style".

What GEO adds is attention to engines beyond Google (several rely on Bing or their own crawlers), to how consistently other websites describe your business, and to measuring mentions in AI answers rather than only rankings.

How do ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Claude, Grok and Copilot find sources?

Each AI engine answers current or local questions by running a live web search and then summarising what it finds, but each uses a different index or crawler. That is the part you can influence. Answers from training data alone change slowly and are hard to affect.

Google also notes that AI Overviews and AI Mode may use "query fan-out": several related searches across subtopics to build one answer (Google). So a page that answers the follow-up questions around a topic has more chances to be picked up than a page that answers only the headline question.

Step 1: How do I allow AI crawlers in robots.txt?

Open yourdomain.com/robots.txt and make sure the search crawlers of each AI engine are not blocked. Many sites block them by accident through a copied robots.txt, a security plugin or a firewall setting. The key distinction is between bots that power search answers (which you want) and bots that collect training data (your choice).

User agentCompanyUsed forBlock it?

OpenAI says sites that opt out of OAI-SearchBot "will not be shown in ChatGPT search answers" and that robots.txt changes take about 24 hours to apply (OpenAI). Perplexity gives the same 24-hour estimate (Perplexity). Google-Extended has no effect on inclusion or ranking in Google Search (Google).

Three more technical checks:

  1. Your text must be in the HTML. Right-click a page, choose View page source and search for a sentence from it. If it isn't there, the content loads only through JavaScript and some crawlers may see an empty page. Ask your developer about server-side rendering.
  2. Don't block snippets. Google lists nosnippet, data-nosnippet, max-snippet and noindex as the controls that limit what appears in AI features (Google). Check that none is set by mistake.
  3. Keep key facts as text, not only inside images or PDF brochures.

Step 2: Why do Bing Webmaster Tools and IndexNow matter for GEO?

Because Copilot runs on Bing and ChatGPT search draws on Bing's index, a site that Bing hasn't indexed is at a disadvantage in two major AI engines. Many Indian businesses set up Google Search Console and never open Bing Webmaster Tools.

  1. Sign in at Bing Webmaster Tools and add your site; you can import it from Google Search Console.
  2. Submit your XML sitemap.
  3. Turn on IndexNow if your platform supports it. It lets your site notify participating search engines (Bing, Naver, Seznam, Yandex and Yep) the moment a page is added, updated or deleted.
  4. Open the AI Performance report. Since February 2026 it shows how often your pages are cited in Copilot and Bing's AI summaries (Bing Webmaster Blog), and in June 2026 Bing added intent, topic and citation-share views (Bing).

Step 3: How do I make my business one clear entity?

Describe your business in exactly the same words, with the same name, phone number and website, everywhere it appears online. AI tools connect facts to entities (a specific business, person or place). If your details differ between profiles, the AI can't be sure they are the same business and may leave you out or mix you up with another one.

  • Write one definition sentence and reuse it: "[Name] is a [what] in [city] that [does what] for [whom]." Put it on your About page, Google Business Profile, LinkedIn, Instagram, Facebook and directories.
  • Keep name, phone, email and URL identical. One old number on one directory creates doubt.
  • Add Organization or LocalBusiness schema with your logo, contact details, area served and a sameAs list of your official profiles.
  • Complete your Google Business Profile. Google's AI guide says Business Profiles can help your services appear in AI responses as well as normal results (Google). Our guide to Google Business Profile for real estate agents walks through it.

Step 4: How do I write content that AI tools quote?

Put a direct, specific answer right under a heading phrased as the question, then back it with sources and original facts. AI answers quote passages, so each passage should make sense on its own.

  1. Use the question as the heading: "Is this project RERA registered?" rather than "Approvals".
  2. Answer in the first 40–60 words, plainly, then explain.
  3. Be specific: places, dates, laws and official sources.
  4. Cite your sources with links. Adding citations and quotations was among the strongest methods in the GEO study.
  5. Publish something original: first-hand experience, a checklist from real work, or a study of public data. Google's guide puts original, non-commodity content first (Google).
  6. Use tables and lists for comparisons and steps.
  7. Show the author and updated date, and end with a short FAQ of real follow-up questions.

What not to do: mass-produce thin AI-written pages for every keyword. Google's spam policies cover scaled content made mainly to rank, and such pages add nothing an AI would want to cite.

Step 5: How do I get mentioned on other websites?

Earn genuine mentions on the sites AI engines already cite for your topic: directories, review platforms, community answers, industry media and "best of" lists. One website praising itself is weak evidence; several independent sources saying the same thing is strong.

  • Directories and listings in your industry and city, with identical details.
  • Reviews on Google and industry platforms from real customers. Never buy or fake them.
  • Reddit and Quora: helpful answers under your real name, without link spam. Grok also searches X posts directly (xAI), so active X accounts matter for that engine.
  • LinkedIn articles, YouTube answers and local or trade media, easiest to reach with original data.

Keep it real. Google's guide warns that artificial efforts to collect mentions "aren't as useful as they seem" (Google).

Step 6: Do schema and llms.txt help GEO?

Schema helps machines read your pages but is not required for AI features; llms.txt is an optional proposal, not a standard, and Google Search does not use it. Google says you don't need new machine-readable files, AI text files or special markup to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode (Google), though structured data can still make you eligible for rich results.

The useful types for most businesses are Organization or LocalBusiness (site-wide), Article on guides, FAQPage where a FAQ is visible, Service on service pages and BreadcrumbList on inner pages. Markup must match what is visible on the page. llms.txt is a proposal by Jeremy Howard (September 2024) for a plain-text file that helps AI agents use a website. Google's AI guide lists it among things you don't need for Search, adding that it may be useful for other services (Google). Add one if it's easy; don't expect it to move rankings.

Step 7: How do I measure AI visibility for free?

Track three things monthly: whether you appear for a fixed set of customer prompts, AI citations in Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools, and visits from AI tools in Google Analytics 4.

  1. Write 20 prompts your customers would ask, from broad ("best real estate marketing agency in Lucknow") to specific ("who can set up WhatsApp auto-replies for a builder in Noida").
  2. Ask them monthly in ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Claude, Grok and Copilot in a private window. Note whether you are named, linked, and who is cited instead.
  3. Google Search Console: Google points site owners to a Generative AI performance report for AI features (Google); AI feature traffic is also counted in the normal Performance report under "Web" (Google).
  4. Bing Webmaster Tools: the AI Performance report shows Copilot citations by page and grounding query.
  5. GA4: in Reports → Acquisition → Traffic acquisition, filter session source for chatgpt.com, perplexity.ai, gemini.google.com, claude.ai and copilot.microsoft.com.
  6. Study the sources cited instead of you. They are your to-do list: get listed there, or publish a better answer.

Be careful with paid "AI rank" tools that promise insider data. Google says no third-party tool has access to its internal systems (Google).

How does GEO work for real estate in India?

For a developer, broker or channel partner, GEO means making every project fact (RERA number, location, configurations, possession date) readable as text and consistent across your site, portals and profiles. Buyers increasingly ask AI tools questions like "is this project RERA approved" or "ready-to-move 3 BHK near a metro in Noida Extension". Imagine a channel partner in Noida working through the steps:

  • Crawlable: robots.txt allows the search bots, the site is server-rendered, and it's verified in Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools.
  • Entity: one definition ("[Name] is a RERA-registered channel partner in Noida helping families buy flats in Noida Extension") on the site, Google Business Profile, LinkedIn and Instagram, with the same WhatsApp number.
  • Answer-first pages: one page per project with the RERA number, carpet areas, price band, possession date and metro distance as text, not only in a brochure PDF; guides that answer buyer questions; and a landing page for each launch.
  • Mentions: the same details on property portals and directories, real buyer reviews, helpful Quora and Reddit answers, and walkthrough videos.

Keep RERA rules in mind: every public claim must match the registered project. For the wider picture, see AI for real estate in India and what the best property websites include.

GEO checklist

  1. robots.txt allows OAI-SearchBot, PerplexityBot, Claude-SearchBot and Bingbot.
  2. Training bots (GPTBot, ClaudeBot, Google-Extended) set by choice, not by accident.
  3. Main text visible in page source without JavaScript; no accidental nosnippet.
  4. Site verified in Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools; sitemap submitted; IndexNow on.
  5. One definition sentence, and the same name, phone, email and URL everywhere.
  6. Organization schema with logo and sameAs links.
  7. Question headings with 40–60 word direct answers and source links.
  8. At least one original piece of data or first-hand guidance.
  9. Real reviews and listings on the sites AI engines already cite.
  10. 20 prompts checked monthly; AI referrals tracked in GA4 and Bing.

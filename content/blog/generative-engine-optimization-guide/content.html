More and more people now get answers from ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Copilot and Google’s AI Overviews instead of clicking through ten blue links. When someone asks “best real estate agency in Lucknow” or “is this project RERA approved?”, the AI writes one answer and names a handful of sources. If your business isn’t one of them, you’re invisible for that question. Generative engine optimization (GEO) is the work of becoming one of those sources. This guide explains what GEO and AEO are, how AI tools choose what to quote, the six steps that actually move the needle, how to measure it for free, and a worked example from Indian real estate. No tricks: most of it is good SEO done thoroughly, plus a few things that matter more for AI than they did for Google alone.

What is generative engine optimization (GEO)?

Generative engine optimization (GEO) means making your content easy for AI tools to find, trust and quote when they write an answer. The “generative engines” are ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Microsoft Copilot, Claude and Google’s AI Overviews and AI Mode.

You will see several names for the same family of work:

TermWhat it usually means
GEO (generative engine optimization)Getting cited or mentioned in AI-written answers
AEO (answer engine optimization)Writing pages that answer one question directly, so search features and assistants can lift the answer as it is
AI SEO / LLM SEOUmbrella terms for both, used loosely
SEO (search engine optimization)Ranking in normal search results; still the foundation for all of the above

The short version: SEO gets you into the index, AEO makes your answer easy to lift, and GEO makes the AI trust you enough to name you. For the classic SEO side, start with our guide to real estate SEO in India.

Why it matters now

Three things have changed:

  • The answer comes before the links. Google shows AI Overviews above the normal results for many questions, and AI Mode answers in a chat. Fewer people scroll to the tenth result, and many never click at all.
  • People ask longer, more specific questions. Instead of “flats Noida”, they type or say “3 BHK under 80 lakh near a metro station in Noida Extension, ready to move”. Those long questions are where small businesses can win, because big portals rarely have a page that answers them exactly.
  • Visitors from AI answers arrive warmer. Someone who clicks your link inside an AI answer has usually already read a summary that mentions you. They are further along in deciding.

Normal search still sends most traffic to most websites, and it still matters. GEO is not a replacement. It’s the part of search that is growing.

How AI tools choose what to quote

AI assistants answer in two ways. Some answers come from what the model learned during training. Answers about current, local or commercial topics usually come from a live web search that the tool runs in the background, then summarises. That second path is the one you can influence quickly.

AI toolWhere its live answers come fromWhat that means for you
Google AI Overviews and AI ModeGoogle’s normal search indexNormal Google SEO and indexing come first
GeminiGoogle SearchSame as above
ChatGPT searchIts own crawler (OAI-SearchBot) plus search partners, including BingAllow OAI-SearchBot and get indexed in Bing
Microsoft CopilotBingBing Webmaster Tools matters
PerplexityIts own crawler (PerplexityBot) and indexAllow PerplexityBot

Across all of them, the pages that get quoted tend to share the same traits: the answer is stated clearly near the top, the facts are specific (numbers, places, dates), the source looks trustworthy, and other websites say the same thing about the business. AI tools also like to cite “best of” lists, review sites, directories, forums and YouTube, so what other people publish about you counts as much as your own site.

Step 1: Let AI crawlers in and make your text readable

Nothing else matters if the AI can’t read your pages.

  1. Check robots.txt. Open yourdomain.com/robots.txt. Make sure it does not block OAI-SearchBot, GPTBot, PerplexityBot, ClaudeBot or Bingbot. Some security plugins and firewalls block AI bots by default; check those settings too.
  2. Make sure your text is in the HTML. Most AI crawlers do not run JavaScript. If your page is built so that text appears only after scripts load, the crawler may see an almost empty page. Right-click → View page source and search for a sentence from your page; if it’s not there, ask your developer about server-side rendering.
  3. Get indexed in Bing as well as Google. Add your site to Bing Webmaster Tools (you can import it from Google Search Console in a few clicks) and submit your sitemap. Turn on IndexNow if your platform supports it, so Bing hears about new pages within minutes.
  4. Don’t block snippets. A nosnippet or max-snippet:0 robots tag stops Google from quoting the page in AI Overviews.

Step 2: Make your business one clear “entity”

AI tools think in entities: a specific business, person or place, with facts attached. If your business name, description, phone number and website differ from one profile to the next, the AI can’t be sure they are the same business, so it leaves you out or mixes you up with someone else.

  • Write one definition sentence and use it everywhere: website About page, Google Business Profile, LinkedIn, Instagram, Facebook, directories. For example: “[Name] is a [what] in [where] that [does what] for [whom].”
  • Keep name, phone, email and website identical on every profile. One old phone number on one directory is enough to create doubt.
  • Add Organization schema (structured data) to your site with your name, logo, URL, contact details, area served and a sameAs list of all your official profiles.
  • Claim and complete your Google Business Profile if you serve customers locally. Our guide to local SEO for real estate in Delhi NCR walks through it.
  • If your name is shared with another company, say clearly what you are and what you are not, on your About page and in your schema. AI tools confuse similarly named businesses more often than you’d expect.

Step 3: Write answer-first pages

This is the AEO part. AI tools quote passages, not whole pages. Make each passage easy to lift:

  1. Use the question as the heading. “How much does a project landing page cost?” rather than “Pricing”.
  2. Answer in the first 40–60 words under it, in plain language, then explain. Don’t make the reader (or the AI) wade through an introduction.
  3. Be specific. Numbers, places, dates, names of laws and tools. “Most clients see results quickly” is useless to an AI; “site visits usually start in the second or third week of a campaign” is quotable.
  4. Use tables and lists for comparisons, steps and costs. They are easy to extract.
  5. Publish something original. A small survey, a study of public data, a price comparison you collected yourself. Original facts get cited because nobody else has them. For example, we checked 749 real estate websites in 26 cities and found that 56% had no tracking and 60% had no WhatsApp link; numbers like that are what AI answers like to quote.
  6. Show who wrote it and when. An author with a real profile and a visible “updated” date both signal that the page can be trusted.
  7. Add a short FAQ at the end with the follow-up questions people ask, each answered in two or three sentences.

What not to do: mass-produce thin AI-written pages for every keyword. Google treats scaled, low-value content as spam, and AI answers ignore pages that say nothing new.

Step 4: Get mentioned on other websites

When an AI tool decides whether to recommend you, it looks at what the rest of the web says. One website praising itself is weak evidence; ten independent sources agreeing is strong. Places that tend to be cited:

  • Directories and listings in your industry and city, with identical details.
  • “Best X in [city]” articles. Find the ones that AI answers already cite for your main questions, and ask the authors to consider you, with a real reason why.
  • Reviews on Google and industry platforms. Ask every happy customer; never buy or fake them.
  • Local news and industry publications. Original data (Step 3) is the easiest way in.
  • Q&A sites, forums and communities such as Quora and Reddit, where you answer questions helpfully under your own name, without spamming links.
  • YouTube. Video answers to common questions are frequently shown and cited.
  • LinkedIn articles and posts by the founder, which build the person entity behind the business.

Step 5: Add structured data

Structured data (schema) is code that tells machines what a page is about. Google says there is no special markup needed for AI Overviews, and that normal SEO best practice applies. Schema still helps every system read your pages correctly, and it’s cheap to add. The useful types for most businesses:

Schema typeWhereWhy
Organization or LocalBusinessEvery page (once, site-wide)Defines your business entity, logo and profiles
Article or BlogPostingGuides and blog postsAuthor, dates and cover image
FAQPagePages with a visible FAQMarks up question-and-answer pairs
ServiceService pagesWhat you offer and where
BreadcrumbListEvery inner pageShows where the page sits on the site

The markup must match what is visible on the page. Test it with Google’s Rich Results Test before you publish.

What about llms.txt? It’s a proposed plain-text summary of a site for AI tools. It is quick to add and does no harm, but Google has said it does not use it, and there is little evidence other tools rely on it yet. Treat it as optional.

Step 6: Measure your AI visibility for free

There is no “AI Search Console” yet, but you can track progress without paid tools:

  1. Write 20 prompts your customers would actually ask, from broad (“best digital marketing agency in Kanpur”) to specific (“who can set up WhatsApp auto-replies for a builder in Lucknow”).
  2. Ask them once a month in ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Copilot, in a logged-out or private window. Note whether you are mentioned, whether you are linked, and which sources are cited instead.
  3. Check AI referrals in Google Analytics 4. In Reports → Acquisition → Traffic acquisition, look at session source for chatgpt.com, perplexity.ai, gemini.google.com and copilot.microsoft.com. ChatGPT also adds utm_source=chatgpt.com to many of its links.
  4. Watch Google Search Console. Clicks and impressions from AI Overviews and AI Mode are counted inside the normal Performance report. Rising impressions on long, question-style queries are a good sign.
  5. Study the cited sources. The pages AI tools cite instead of you are your to-do list: get listed there, or publish something better on the same question.

A worked example: a real estate business in Noida

Imagine a channel partner in Noida who wants to appear when buyers ask AI tools about flats in Noida Extension. Here is what the six steps look like in practice:

  • Crawlable: robots.txt allows the AI bots, the site is server-rendered, and it’s verified in both Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools.
  • Entity: one definition (“[Name] is a RERA-registered real estate channel partner in Noida that helps families buy ready-to-move and under-construction flats in Noida Extension”) on the website, Google Business Profile, LinkedIn and Instagram, all with the same WhatsApp number.
  • Answer-first pages: one page per project with the RERA number, carpet areas, price band, possession date and distance to the metro as plain text, not only inside a PDF brochure; plus guides such as “carpet area vs super built-up area” and a real estate landing page for each launch.
  • Mentions: listed on property portals and local directories with the same details, reviews from real buyers on Google, a few helpful answers on Quora, and walkthrough videos on YouTube.
  • Schema: Organization site-wide, FAQPage on project pages, BlogPosting on guides.
  • Measure: 20 buyer prompts checked monthly, AI referrals tracked in GA4.

None of this needs a big budget. It needs the facts to be written down clearly, in the same way, in many places. For more on where AI already helps property businesses day to day, see AI for real estate in India.

Common GEO mistakes

  • Blocking AI crawlers by accident through a firewall, plugin or copied robots.txt.
  • Key facts only in images or PDFs. AI tools read text. Prices, sizes, addresses and RERA numbers must be on the page as text.
  • Inconsistent details across profiles: old phone numbers, different business names, outdated descriptions.
  • Publishing hundreds of thin AI-written pages. It risks a spam penalty and doesn’t get cited.
  • Fake reviews or made-up statistics. They can be checked, and AI tools increasingly cross-check sources.
  • Only checking Google. ChatGPT and Copilot lean on Bing; ignoring Bing leaves those answers to your competitors.
  • Expecting results in a week. Indexing, mentions and trust build over months. Measure monthly, not daily.

GEO checklist

  1. robots.txt allows OAI-SearchBot, GPTBot, PerplexityBot, ClaudeBot and Bingbot.
  2. Main text is visible in the page source without JavaScript.
  3. Site verified and sitemap submitted in Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools.
  4. One definition sentence used on the site and every profile.
  5. Same name, phone, email and URL everywhere.
  6. Organization schema with logo and sameAs links.
  7. Question headings with 40–60 word direct answers.
  8. At least one piece of original data or research.
  9. Author name and updated date on every guide.
  10. FAQ with FAQPage schema on key pages.
  11. Listed in relevant directories, with real reviews.
  12. 20 customer prompts checked monthly; AI referrals tracked in GA4.

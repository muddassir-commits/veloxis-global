The best real estate content ideas for builders and brokers in India are the ones that answer a buyer's real worries: construction progress, locality life, carpet area and floor plans, RERA checks, payment plans and possession. Post five times a week around these themes, film once a week, and move every interested viewer to WhatsApp.

This guide is for developers, channel partners and property dealers who post on Instagram, YouTube, Facebook and WhatsApp and feel they are out of ideas, or posting a lot with little to show for it. Most lists that rank for this topic are written for US agents: open houses, mortgage rates, "just sold" posts. Here you get ideas grouped by the question each one answers for an Indian buyer, a weekly calendar you can copy, the RERA and advertising rules that apply to social posts, and what to measure instead of likes.

What should a builder or broker post on social media?

Post content that removes one doubt a buyer has before a site visit. A buyer who is weighing a flat worth years of income wants proof that the project is real, the location works, the numbers add up and you will answer when they call. Each post should do one of those jobs.

A useful test before you post: if a buyer saved this post and showed it to their family, would it help the conversation? A rendered tower with "Luxury living awaits" will not. A 40-second clip of the Tower B slab, filmed this week with the date in the caption, will.

Instagram says its ranking looks at signals such as how likely you are to save, share or spend time on a post, not only likes (Instagram: ranking explained). Useful, specific content earns those signals; generic "dream home" posts rarely do.

What are 30 real estate content ideas that work in India?

Here are 30 ideas you can film or design with what you already have on site and in the sales office. Each one is specific to Indian buyers and projects.

Construction and project proof

  1. Monthly site walk: same route, same angles, dated in the caption.
  2. "Floor 12 of 24 done": a simple progress counter for each tower.
  3. Site engineer explains one stage, such as waterproofing or plastering, in 60 seconds.
  4. Quality check clip: concrete cube test or a plumbing pressure test, explained simply.
  5. Sample flat walkthrough with a tape measure, showing real room sizes.
  6. Monsoon post: how the site handles rain, drainage and safety.

Locality and lifestyle

  1. Peak-hour drive from the project to the nearest business district, filmed in real time.
  2. Nearest metro or railway station, walked or driven from the gate.
  3. Schools, hospitals and markets within a short drive, with names and a map.
  4. "Sunday in this locality": parks, food, temples, malls.
  5. Approved upcoming infrastructure, with a link to the official notice.
  6. Locality comparison: two areas your buyers usually choose between, with honest pros and cons.

Buyer education

  1. How to check any project's RERA number on the state website (see our RERA number check guide).
  2. Carpet area vs built-up vs super built-up, drawn on one floor plan.
  3. What is in a price sheet: base price, PLC, parking, maintenance deposit, GST, stamp duty.
  4. Construction-linked payment plan explained stage by stage.
  5. Documents a buyer needs for a home loan, as a checklist carousel.
  6. Ready-to-move vs under-construction: who each suits.
  7. What happens at possession: snag list, handover documents, society formation.
  8. NRI buying basics: power of attorney, video site visits, payment in rupees (link our NRI marketing guide for the team side).

Trust, people and process

  1. "What happens when you send us a WhatsApp": the real reply, the visit booking, the follow-up.
  2. A day in the sales office or experience centre.
  3. Answering the most common comment or DM question of the week on camera.
  4. Meet the architect or landscape designer: why the layout looks the way it does.
  5. Handover moments and housewarmings, only with written permission from the buyers shown.
  6. Myth vs fact: one common buying myth per post.

Offers, events and seasons

  1. Site visit weekend announcement with time slots and a WhatsApp link.
  2. Festival-season visit schedule (Navratri, Diwali, Gudi Padwa, Akshaya Tritiya, Onam), without pressure tactics.
  3. Launch countdown in Stories, with the RERA number on every frame.
  4. Year-in-review of construction: twelve monthly photos side by side.

How often should a real estate business post, and what does a weekly plan look like?

A steady rhythm of about five feed posts a week, plus Stories on most days, is enough for most builders and brokers if every post is useful. Consistency matters more than volume. Use this as a starting plan and adjust after a month of data.

To produce this without a full-time team:

  1. Pick one shoot day a week. Film the site walk, one locality clip and one talking-head answer.
  2. Keep a running list of questions from calls, WhatsApp and site visits. Each question is a post.
  3. Write captions in plain English, with the key line repeated in Hindi or the local language your buyers use.
  4. Check every post against the rules below before it goes live.
  5. Schedule the week in one sitting, then spend a few minutes each day replying to comments and DMs.

Which platform suits which kind of real estate content?

Use Instagram and YouTube Shorts for discovery, YouTube for long walkthroughs, Facebook for older and tier-2 audiences, and WhatsApp for people who already know you. Film once in vertical format, then cut versions for each platform.

  • Instagram Reels can now run up to three minutes (Social Media Today), long enough for a full sample flat walkthrough. Short clips still suit progress updates. Our Instagram Reels guide covers hooks and editing.
  • YouTube Shorts also allow up to three minutes (YouTube Help), while longer regular YouTube videos suit full project and locality tours that buyers watch before a visit.
  • WhatsApp Channels are one-way updates: you post text, photos, videos and polls, followers react but cannot reply in the channel, and followers' phone numbers are not shown to you or to each other (WhatsApp blog). They suit construction updates for existing buyers and warm leads. Personal follow-ups belong in one-to-one chats; see our WhatsApp message templates.
  • Email carries the long version: monthly construction reports with photos, which buyers keep and forward (see real estate email marketing).

Make what you post your own. In April 2026 Instagram extended a rule it already applied to Reels: accounts that mostly repost content made by others are no longer recommended to new people, and small edits such as adding a watermark do not make a repost original (TechCrunch). Your own site footage is original by nature, which is one more reason to film it.

What rules apply to real estate posts on social media in India?

A social post that promotes a registered project is an advertisement, so RERA and advertising rules apply to it like they do to a hoarding. Check these before posting.

  • RERA registration number and website. The RERA Act requires a promoter's advertisements to show the project's registration number and the authority's website address, and gives buyers a remedy if an ad's statements turn out to be false (RERA Act, India Code). Put both in the caption and on the image or first frame of the video.
  • State QR codes. MahaRERA has required a QR code in project advertisements since 1 August 2023 (Housing.com News), and UP RERA has issued QR codes for registered projects too (PTI via The Week). Check your state authority's latest orders. Our RERA advertisement rules guide lists what to show.
  • Influencer and paid partner posts. ASCI's guidelines for influencer advertising say a material connection must be disclosed upfront and prominently, using labels such as "Ad", "Sponsored", "Collaboration" or "Partnership", and that a disclosure only in a bio or at the end of a video is likely to be missed (ASCI guidelines). This also applies to channel partners paid to post about a project.
  • People in your content. Get written permission before you show buyers, residents or their homes. India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act and the DPDP Rules notified in November 2025 centre on consent for a clear purpose (PIB: DPDP Rules, 2025).

This is general information, not legal advice. Rules were checked on 8 November 2026; confirm current orders with your state RERA authority.

Can you post AI renders and walkthroughs of a project that is not built yet?

Yes, but label them clearly. ASCI's guidelines on synthetically generated content in advertising list "a 3D model of an unbuilt housing complex" as an example where a label is mandatory, and suggest wording such as "Video created using AI" (ASCI guidelines). Routine edits like colour correction do not need a label.

In practice, add "Artist's impression" or "AI-generated visual" on the image itself, not only in the caption. Then pair renders with real footage: a render of the clubhouse next to this month's photo of its foundations builds more trust than renders alone. If an amenity or view in a render is not in the approved plans, do not show it.

How do you know if your real estate content is working?

Measure what leads to site visits, not likes. Track saves, shares, profile visits, WhatsApp clicks, DMs that ask about price or visits, and site visits you can trace back to a post or campaign.

Should real estate content also go on your website?

Yes. Your best social topics should also become pages on your own site, where they can rank in Google and be quoted by AI answer tools. Social posts fade within days; a locality guide or a "how to check RERA" page keeps working. Google advises writing content that is helpful and made for people first, with clear signs of first-hand experience (Google Search Central). Site footage, real distances you have driven and your own floor plans are exactly that kind of experience.

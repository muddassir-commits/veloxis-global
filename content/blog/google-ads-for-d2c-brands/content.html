To run Google Shopping ads in India, a D2C brand creates a Google Merchant Center account, verifies its website, adds shipping and return settings, uploads a clean product feed in INR, links Merchant Center to Google Ads, sets up purchase tracking with values, and then launches a Standard Shopping or Performance Max campaign.

This guide is for founders and marketers of Indian direct-to-consumer brands who sell on their own Shopify, WooCommerce or custom store and want product ads on Google. Most guides that rank for "Google Shopping ads India" still talk about Smart Shopping (which Google retired), quote cost figures with no source, or skip the India-only details. This one walks through the current setup, step by step, with every rule linked to Google's own help pages.

General information, not advice on your specific account. Google's rules and product names change often; everything below was checked against Google's help pages on 11 November 2026.

What are Google Shopping ads, and how do they work in India?

Google Shopping ads are product ads that show a photo, title, price and your store name, built from a product feed you upload to Google Merchant Center rather than from keywords you write. When someone searches for a product, Google matches the search to products in your feed and decides whether to show your listing.

That last point is what makes Shopping different from normal search ads. You don't pick keywords. Google reads your product titles, descriptions, categories and other attributes, so the quality of your feed decides which searches you can appear for. A badly written feed limits your reach no matter how much budget you set.

There are three ways your products can appear on Google today:

Google's help page on free listings says new Merchant Center accounts are opted in by default, and that products can appear at no cost on Google Search, Google Maps, Gemini and YouTube. Free listings are not guaranteed placements, but they cost nothing, so there is no reason to leave them off.

Should a D2C brand start with Standard Shopping or Performance Max?

Start with Standard Shopping if you want to see which searches and products work before handing control to automation; start with Performance Max if your purchase tracking is solid and you have images and videos ready. Many D2C brands run both: Standard Shopping for control over core products, Performance Max for reach.

Two changes make older advice unreliable. First, Smart Shopping no longer exists. Google automatically upgraded Smart Shopping campaigns to Performance Max, and new Smart Shopping campaigns can't be created. Any guide still recommending Smart Shopping is out of date.

Second, Performance Max no longer wins automatically when both campaign types advertise the same products. Google told advertisers in October 2024 that "normal auction dynamics will now apply", meaning the campaign with the higher Ad Rank serves (Search Engine Roundtable). So running both side by side is now a practical setup, not a waste of budget.

How do you set up Google Merchant Center for an Indian store?

Create the account, add your business details, verify and claim your website, set shipping and return policies, then upload your products. Google's Performance Max setup guide lists business information, a verified and claimed website, shipping settings and product information as the parts that must be ready before a campaign can use your products.

  1. Create a Merchant Center account with the Google account your business will keep long term. Use a shared business login, not a freelancer's personal account, so you always own it.
  2. Add business information: legal business name, address and customer service contact. Keep it consistent with the details on your website.
  3. Verify and claim your website. On Shopify, the Google & YouTube app handles this and then syncs your products to Merchant Center automatically. On WooCommerce or a custom store, verify with Google Search Console, a meta tag or Google Analytics.
  4. Set shipping costs. Google's product data specification lists India among the countries where shipping costs are required for Shopping ads and free listings. Set them account-wide in Merchant Center, including free shipping thresholds if you have them.
  5. Add your return policy. In Merchant Center, go to the shipping and returns settings and add a return policy (Google's steps). Make it say the same thing as the return page on your site; mismatches are a common reason for warnings.
  6. Upload your products through the Shopify app, a WooCommerce plugin, a Google Sheet or a scheduled file. Pick one method and stick to it, so two sources don't overwrite each other.
  7. Check the diagnostics page and fix every disapproved product before you spend on ads.
  8. Link Merchant Center to Google Ads. The Merchant Center account can't be changed on a Performance Max campaign once it's created, so link the right one the first time.

Before any of this, your store itself needs the basics in place: visible contact details, clear policies and working checkout. Our D2C brand launch checklist covers what Indian law and Google expect your site to show.

What makes a good product feed for Shopping ads?

A good feed has accurate, specific titles, prices that match your site, clean images, correct product identifiers and labels you can use to group products by margin or season. Because Shopping has no keywords, the feed is your targeting.

AttributeGoogle's ruleWhat to do as a D2C brand
titleUp to 150 charactersLead with what the product is and the words buyers search: brand, product type, key feature, size or colour. Avoid promotional text like "best price".
descriptionUp to 5,000 charactersDescribe material, use and fit in plain words. Reuse the strongest lines from your product page.
image_linkAt least 500 x 500 pixels for all product images, enforced from 31 January 2027Use a clean main photo on a plain background, no watermarks or promotional overlays.
priceNumber plus currency code, for example 15.00 INR in Google's own sampleMust match the price on the landing page and at checkout. Use sale_price for discounts.
maximum_retail_priceIndia only, optional, used in free listingsSubmit your MRP if you show it on the product page; it must match the site.
brandUp to 70 charactersYour brand name, spelled the same way everywhere.
gtin / mpnGTIN required where the manufacturer assigned one; MPN if there is no GTINIf you make your own products and have no barcode, set identifier_exists to "no" rather than inventing a number.
google_product_category and product_typeOne Google category; your own type path up to 750 charactersUse product_type to mirror your store's collections, so you can split campaigns by it.
custom_label_0 to custom_label_4Up to 5 labels, 100 characters each, never shown to shoppersLabel margin band, bestseller status, season or launch batch. These become your campaign splits later.

The limits above come from Google's product data specification, and the MRP attribute from its separate help page on maximum retail price, which says "This attribute is only available in India."

A practical title formula for most D2C categories is: Brand + product type + key attribute + variant. For example, a title like "[Brand] cotton kurta for women, straight fit, navy, size M" tells Google far more than "Navy Classic". Write titles for how Indian shoppers search, including common Hinglish product words where your buyers use them, but keep them accurate.

How do you track Shopping sales correctly?

Track purchases as a Google Ads conversion with the real order value, add enhanced conversions, and test with a real order before launch. Smart Bidding and Performance Max optimise toward whatever you tell them is a conversion, so wrong tracking means Google learns the wrong thing.

  • Use one purchase conversion as the primary goal. Add-to-cart and checkout-start can be tracked as secondary actions, but if they count as primary, Google will chase cheap clicks that never buy.
  • Pass the order value and currency. Google's help page on Target ROAS bidding says you need to set values for the conversions you track before you can use it.
  • Turn on enhanced conversions. Google says enhanced conversions supplement your tags by sending hashed first-party data, such as an email address hashed with SHA256, to improve measurement (Google Ads Help).
  • Decide how to count cash on delivery. The tag fires when the order is placed, not when COD is paid. If many COD orders are refused, Google sees sales that never happened. Review refused orders in your reporting and consider uploading adjustments if your setup allows it.
  • Avoid double counting. If both the Shopify app and a manually added tag send purchases, keep only one as primary.

Tracking also needs consent handling that matches your privacy notice. Our conversion optimisation work starts with exactly this check, because a store that converts well but tracks badly still loses money on ads.

How do you launch your first Shopping or Performance Max campaign?

In Google Ads, choose the Sales objective, confirm the purchase goal, pick Shopping or Performance Max, select your Merchant Center account and country, then set budget, bidding and the products to include. The steps below follow Google's own flow.

  1. Click Create, then Campaign, and choose Sales as the objective.
  2. Confirm conversion goals. Remove anything that isn't a purchase from the campaign's goals.
  3. Pick the campaign type: Shopping for Standard Shopping, or Performance Max.
  4. Select the Merchant Center account and the country (India) or feed label where you sell.
  5. Choose what to advertise. Start with your proven sellers or a clear category rather than the whole catalogue, using product_type or a custom label.
  6. Set bidding. With little data, Maximise clicks or manual CPC (Standard Shopping) or Maximise conversion value without a target (Performance Max) is a sensible start. Add a ROAS target only once you have enough purchases (see the next section).
  7. Set a daily budget you can hold steady for a few weeks. Frequent large changes reset what the system has learned.
  8. For Performance Max, build the asset group: product images, lifestyle images, your logo, short videos if you have them, headlines and descriptions. Add audience signals such as past buyers or site visitors to guide it.
  9. Add brand exclusions and negatives before launch (next section), then publish.

How do you separate branded and generic searches?

Keep searches for your own brand name out of Performance Max with brand exclusions, and cover them with a separate, low-cost branded campaign; use negative keywords to keep generic campaigns focused on new customers. Otherwise your reports look better than they are, because people who already know you would often have bought anyway.

Google's help page on brand settings in Search and Performance Max says brand exclusions let you choose which brands a campaign should not match, and they also catch misspellings and brand searches in other languages. Add your own brand to the exclusion list on your generic Performance Max campaign.

For Standard Shopping, add your brand name as a negative keyword on the generic campaign and run a second campaign, limited to brand searches, at a lower bid. Then you can see two honest numbers: what new-customer searches cost you, and what branded searches return.

Also add negatives for searches you don't want: competitor brand names if you prefer not to pay for them, "free", "second hand", "how to make", job searches and products you don't sell. Google now supports campaign-level negative keywords and negative keyword lists in Performance Max (Google Ads Help), so the same hygiene applies to both campaign types.

How do you improve Shopping results after launch?

Read the search terms every week, improve titles for products that get impressions but few clicks, split products by margin with custom labels, and only add a ROAS target once you have enough conversion data. Most gains come from the feed and structure, not from bid tweaks.

  • Search terms: add irrelevant terms as negatives; when a good term appears, check that the matching product title uses those words.
  • Titles and images: for products with many impressions and few clicks, test a clearer title or a better main image first. Price and photo are what shoppers compare.
  • Margin splits: put high-margin and low-margin products in different campaigns or listing groups using a custom label, so you can accept a lower return where margin is high and demand more where it is thin.
  • Target ROAS: Google's guide to Target ROAS for Shopping campaigns says Shopping campaigns must have at least 15 conversions per Merchant Center ID in the last 30 days. Set the first target close to what the campaign already achieves, then move it in small steps.
  • Out of stock: keep availability accurate so you don't pay for clicks on products you can't ship. Shopify and most plugins sync this automatically; check that it does.
  • Sale seasons: for Diwali and other sale periods, update prices and sale_price in the feed before the ads change, and raise budgets in steps rather than all at once.
  • Profit, not just ROAS: ROAS ignores product cost, shipping, payment fees and returns. Judge campaigns on contribution margin after those costs, which you calculate from your own numbers.

Shopping ads bring the click; the product page has to close the sale. If people click but don't buy, the fix is often on the page itself: price clarity, delivery dates, COD terms and reviews. Strong e-commerce marketing treats the ad, the feed and the page as one system.

What mistakes do D2C brands make with Google Shopping ads?

The most common mistakes are poor product titles, price mismatches between the feed and the site, missing shipping or return settings, counting add-to-carts as conversions, and mixing branded and new-customer results in one campaign.

Vague titles like "Classic Tee"Google can't match the product to real searchesUse brand + product type + attribute + variant

Instagram Reels are the cheapest way left to put your business in front of people who don’t follow you yet. A good Reel can reach thousands of local buyers without a rupee of ad spend, and a bad one disappears in an hour. The difference is rarely the camera or the editing. It’s the first two seconds, the idea behind the video, and what happens after someone watches it. This guide covers what Reels are, how to set up your account, 15 Reel ideas you can shoot this week, how to film and edit on a phone, when and how to post, and how to turn views into enquiries. The steps work for any business. The examples come from real estate, because that’s the industry we work in, and property is one of the hardest things to sell in a short video.

What are Instagram Reels?

Reels are Instagram’s short vertical videos. They play full-screen, loop automatically and appear in four places: the Reels tab, the main feed, the Explore page and your profile grid. Instagram now allows Reels of up to three minutes, but for property a short, single-idea Reel is usually easier to watch to the end, and Instagram says what people do with a Reel, including watching it, is among the main signals it uses to recommend it (Instagram ranking explained).

The important part is who sees them. A normal post is shown mostly to your followers. A Reel is also shown to people who have never heard of you, based on what they watch and where they are. That makes Reels the organic format on Instagram where a new account with few followers can still reach people well beyond them.

Instagram decides how far a Reel goes mainly from how people react in the first hours: whether they watch to the end, watch again, share it in a DM, save it or follow you. Likes matter less than most people think. Shares and full watches matter most.

Why Reels work for business, and where they don’t

Reels do three jobs well:

  • Reach. They put you in front of local people who aren’t following you yet.
  • Proof. Before anyone sends an enquiry, they look you up. A profile full of real videos of real places, with your face and voice in them, tells them you exist and know your work.
  • Warm audiences for ads. Everyone who watches your Reels can be shown ads later. Organic Reels make your paid campaigns cheaper and better targeted.

What Reels don’t do on their own is produce a steady, predictable flow of enquiries. Views go up and down, and many viewers are curious rather than ready to buy. So treat Reels as the top of your funnel and pair them with paid ads and a fast follow-up. Our guide to real estate social media covers the wider posting plan; this guide goes deep on video.

Set up your account before you post

A viewer who likes your Reel taps your name next. You have about three seconds to convince them to follow or message you. Check these first:

  1. Switch to a professional account (Settings → Account type and tools → Switch to professional account → Business or Creator). This unlocks insights, contact buttons and the option to boost posts.
  2. Name field with a keyword. The name field is searchable. “Rahul Verma | Noida Property Advisor” is found by people searching “Noida property”; “Rahul Verma” is not.
  3. A bio that says who you help, where, and what to do next. For example: “2 & 3 BHK flats in Noida Extension · RERA-registered projects only · WhatsApp for prices and site visits”.
  4. A WhatsApp button. Edit profile → Add action buttons → WhatsApp. In India most buyers would rather message than call.
  5. One link that works on a phone: your website, a project page or a short link page with your main options.
  6. Story highlights that answer the first questions: Projects, Site visits, Possession, Reviews, FAQ.

If you sell property, the account also needs to follow the RERA rules in the “Rules” section below before you post a single project video.

15 Reel ideas you can shoot this week

The best Reels answer a question your buyers already have or show something they can’t see from a brochure. Pick the ideas that fit your business and repeat the ones that work.

Show the real thing

  1. The 20-second walkthrough. Walk from the door to the balcony in one take. Real light, real sounds, the view at the end. Buyers trust unedited walkthroughs more than renders.
  2. “What ₹X gets you in [locality]”. Show the flat or plot and say the size and price band on screen, with the RERA number in the caption.
  3. Construction progress. The same spot filmed every month. Over time this becomes the most convincing proof that the project is real and moving.
  4. The view from the 14th floor at sunset. One shot, no talking. These are often shared the most.
  5. Before and after. An empty plot to the finished building, a bare flat to the furnished sample flat.

Answer questions

  1. “3 questions to ask before booking a flat.” Talk to the camera, one point per line of on-screen text.
  2. Explain a confusing term. Carpet area vs super built-up area, what EOI means, how to check a RERA number. Link the full answer in your caption, for example our guide on how to check a RERA number.
  3. Reply to a comment with a Reel. Instagram lets you answer a comment with a video, and the original comment appears on screen.
  4. The total cost breakdown. Base price, parking, club, registry, GST: what a buyer will actually pay. Use clear on-screen text.
  5. Myth vs fact. “Ready-to-move is always more expensive” and similar beliefs in your market.

Show the people and the place

  1. A day at the site office or a day of site visits, cut down to 20 seconds.
  2. The locality tour. Metro station, school, hospital and market, each with the drive time on screen.
  3. Meet the team. The person who will answer the buyer’s WhatsApp, on camera, introducing themselves.
  4. A happy handover, only with the buyer’s written permission, and never with their personal details on screen.
  5. Trends with a twist. Use a trending audio only when you can tie it to your product in a clever way. Forced trends look like ads and get scrolled past.

How to film Reels on a phone

You don’t need a camera crew. A mid-range phone from the last three years, a ₹500 tripod and a clip-on microphone are enough.

  • Vertical, 9:16, 1080×1920. Shoot in 1080p or 4K at 30 frames per second. Clean the lens first; it is the most common reason phone video looks soft.
  • Light from the front. Face a window or shoot in the morning or late afternoon. For property, open every curtain and switch on every light.
  • Sound matters more than picture. People forgive slightly shaky video, not bad audio. Use a wired or wireless clip-on mic when you talk to the camera.
  • Move slowly. Walk at half your normal speed and hold the phone at chest height with both hands, or use a gimbal. Fast pans make viewers dizzy.
  • Shoot short clips. Three to five seconds each, with a second of stillness at the start and end so they cut cleanly.
  • Keep the edges clear. Instagram puts the caption at the bottom and buttons on the right. Keep faces and text in the centre of the frame.

Editing, hooks, captions and audio

Most viewers decide in the first one or two seconds whether to keep watching. That opening moment is the hook, and it decides more than anything else you do.

Hooks that work:

  • Say the result first. “This is a 3 BHK in Noida Extension for under ₹80 lakh.”
  • Ask the question your buyer is asking. “Is it smart to buy under-construction in 2026?”
  • Start in the middle of the action: the door opening, the view appearing, the keys being handed over.
  • Put the hook in on-screen text too, because many people watch with the sound off.

For editing, Instagram’s own editor or the free Edits and CapCut apps are enough. Cut every pause, add captions to every spoken word, and end on a clear next step: “WhatsApp ‘FLAT’ for the price list” works better than “link in bio”.

Write the caption for search, not only for style. Instagram search now reads captions, so say what and where in plain words (“2 BHK ready-to-move flat in Gomti Nagar, Lucknow”) and use three to five specific hashtags rather than thirty generic ones. Trending audio can help reach, but business accounts can only use sounds licensed for commercial use, so check before you post. A slogan or line from your branding can work well as the closing text; our list of real estate slogans and taglines has ideas.

When and how often to post

Consistency beats volume. Three Reels a week, every week, will do more than ten in one week and nothing for a month. A simple rhythm for a small team:

DayReel typeExample
MondayShow the real thingWalkthrough of a 2 BHK sample flat
WednesdayAnswer a questionCarpet area vs super built-up area in 30 seconds
SaturdayPeople and placeLocality tour: metro, school, market

Post when your audience is on their phones. There is no single best time: check when your followers are most active in Insights after your first few weeks, and post just before those hours. Use Trial Reels (an option in the share screen for professional accounts) to show a Reel to non-followers first and see how it performs before your followers see it.

Batch your work: shoot six to nine Reels in one morning at a site, edit them over the week, and schedule them from Meta Business Suite.

Rules for property Reels: RERA, music and privacy

Every Reel that promotes a specific project is an advertisement, and RERA rules apply to it just like a newspaper ad or a hoarding:

  • Show the RERA registration number of the project, and of the agent if you are a broker, in the caption or on screen. Some states, such as Maharashtra, also require a QR code that links to the registration.
  • No promises you can’t prove: no “guaranteed returns”, no possession date that differs from the RERA filing, no amenities that aren’t in the approved plan.
  • Label renders. If a shot is a 3D render or an artist’s impression, say so on screen.
  • Music licences. Use Instagram’s commercial sound library or royalty-free music. A copyrighted film song can get your Reel muted or removed.
  • People’s privacy. Get written permission from anyone who appears clearly on camera, and never show a buyer’s name, flat number or documents.

Turn views into enquiries

Views are not the goal. Conversations are. Most businesses lose the lead not in the Reel but in the step after it:

  1. Give one clear action in every Reel. “Comment PRICE” or “WhatsApp FLAT” in the last two seconds and in the caption.
  2. Answer every comment and DM fast. A buyer who comments “price?” and waits a day has already asked three other sellers. An instant automatic reply on Instagram and WhatsApp, followed by a person, keeps them. Our guide to WhatsApp automation for real estate leads shows how to set that up.
  3. Move the conversation to WhatsApp. That is where site visits get booked in India. Ask for the buyer’s preferred time, not just “interested?”.
  4. Boost the Reels that already work. When a Reel gets strong watch time and shares organically, put a small budget behind it as an ad aimed at your area. It’s usually cheaper than starting from a new ad.
  5. Retarget viewers. Build an audience of people who watched your Reels and show them your project ad with a lead form or a click-to-WhatsApp button.

Done this way, Reels stop being a vanity number and become the first step of real estate lead generation that you can measure. For the full picture of where property leads come from, read how to generate real estate leads in India.

What to measure

Open Insights on each Reel after 48 hours and after a week. Track these in a simple sheet:

  • Accounts reached, and the share that are non-followers. Shows whether Instagram is pushing the Reel to new people.
  • Average watch time and skip rate. If most people leave in the first three seconds, fix the hook.
  • Shares and saves. The best signal that the content is useful.
  • Profile visits, follows and link or WhatsApp taps. These connect views to business.
  • Enquiries and site visits that mention the Reel. Ask every new lead where they found you and write it down.

After a month, sort your Reels by enquiries, not views. Make more of the top three formats and drop the bottom three.

Common mistakes

  • Posting ads as Reels. A rendered tower with a “Launching soon” banner and a phone number gets scrolled past. Show, explain or help instead.
  • A slow start. Logos, drone intros and “Hi everyone, welcome to my channel” lose most viewers before the point.
  • No captions. Many people watch with the sound off.
  • Copyrighted music on a business account.
  • Missing RERA details on project Reels.
  • Slow replies to comments and DMs, which wastes the attention the Reel earned.
  • Giving up after two weeks. Most accounts need two to three months of steady posting before reach becomes consistent.

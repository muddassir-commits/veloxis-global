Newspaper ads are good at fast, wide local awareness on one day, such as a launch weekend, and at reaching older buyers and family decision-makers who still read the paper. Digital ads (Meta and Google) are better at targeting, testing, measurement and follow-up. For most projects the answer is both, each with a clear job, and print measured as carefully as digital.

This guide is for developers and marketing heads deciding where a launch or sales budget should go. It compares newspaper and digital ads for real estate, explains the main newspaper formats, what a print ad must include under RERA, how to track print enquiries, how to compare cost per site visit across channels, and how to combine both in a launch.

What is the difference between newspaper ads and digital ads?

Newspaper ads reach everyone who reads an edition on one day; digital ads reach the people you choose, for as long as you pay, and report results automatically.

Are newspaper ads still effective for real estate?

For some jobs, yes. Print still earns its place for:

  • Launches and big dates: a launch weekend, price release or site event, when you want many local people to hear about it on the same day.
  • Family decision-makers: older buyers and parents who influence a purchase even when a younger family member enquires online.
  • Credibility: some buyers see a large print ad as a sign of an established developer.
  • Local projects: a city or zonal edition can match a project's catchment.

Print is weaker for steady weekly lead flow, for testing messages, and for people who have recently moved to the city or read news mostly on their phones. Before booking, ask the publication for its current readership and circulation figures for your edition. Independent sources include the Indian Readership Survey published by the Media Research Users Council (MRUC) and the Audit Bureau of Circulations (ABC); compare editions on the same source and the same year.

When are digital ads the better choice?

  • Steady lead flow between launch events, week after week.
  • Buyers already searching for a locality or project name, via Google Ads.
  • Reaching people before they search, via Meta ads on Facebook and Instagram.
  • Retargeting everyone who visited your project page, including people who first saw your print ad (see real estate retargeting ads).
  • NRI buyers, who rarely see the local edition.
  • Testing which message, price point or configuration gets visits, before spending on print.

What must a real estate newspaper ad include?

Under Section 11(2) of the RERA Act, 2016, every advertisement for a registered project must prominently show the project's RERA registration number and the state RERA authority's website, and a project that must be registered can't be advertised before registration (Section 3). Some states add more: MahaRERA made a project QR code mandatory on ads from 1 August 2023 (Housing.com), and UP RERA asks for the QR code on ads and brochures (PTI, July 2024). Enforcement is real: ASCI's Half-Yearly Complaints Report 2024-25 named real estate the most violative sector, with 34% of cases investigated between April and September 2024.

General information, not legal advice. Rules checked on 5 Nov 2026; confirm your state RERA's current directions.

Beyond compliance, a good newspaper ad has:

  • One main message, readable in a few seconds.
  • One real picture of the project, sample flat or location.
  • The location with a landmark and a small map.
  • The price band if you share it, with the asterisk explained in readable type.
  • The RERA number, website and QR code large enough to read, not hidden in fine print.
  • One clear action: scan the QR code, WhatsApp a dedicated number, or visit this weekend.

How do you track enquiries from a newspaper ad?

Plan tracking before the ad goes to press:

  • A QR code that opens a dedicated landing page or WhatsApp chat with a tracking tag, built with Google's campaign URL parameters (for example ?utm_source=newspaper&utm_campaign=launch-oct).
  • A separate phone or WhatsApp number used only in that ad, so every call or message can be counted.
  • A short, separate web address such as yourproject.com/offer.
  • Ask every walk-in how they heard about the project, and record it on the site visit form.
  • Watch branded searches: a print ad often shows up as more Google searches for the project name in the following days. Compare dates in the Search Console Performance report.

How do you compare the cost of newspaper and digital ads?

Use the same three numbers for every channel: spend ÷ enquiries, spend ÷ site visits and spend ÷ bookings. Reach and impressions are not comparable between print and digital, but site visits and bookings are. Count print enquiries from the QR code, the dedicated number and walk-ins who name the paper; count digital ones from your CRM source tags. Then plan the next launch on cost per site visit, as explained in our marketing budget guide. A real estate CRM keeps every source on one sheet.

Which should a small builder or broker choose?

With a small budget, start with digital. It lets you spend a little each day, see which message brings site visits, and retarget the people who showed interest, which a single print insertion can't do. Keep print for a moment that needs many local people to hear the same news on the same day, such as a launch or a site event, and book it only once the landing page, WhatsApp replies and tracking are ready. Brokers selling resale or rental homes may still find classifieds and property supplements useful in their locality, as long as each ad carries a dedicated number so you can count what it brings.

How do you combine newspaper and digital ads in a launch?

  1. Before the print date: Meta ads and Google search running; landing page and instant WhatsApp replies ready and tested.
  2. Print day: the newspaper ad with a QR code and a dedicated number. Raise the Google search budget on the project name, because people who saw the ad will search for it.
  3. The following days: retarget everyone who visited the landing page with Meta ads showing the site, the sample flat and visit slots.
  4. The weekend: site visits, with every visitor's source recorded.
  5. After: compare cost per visit and per booking from print, Meta and Google, and plan the next launch on that basis.

What are common newspaper ad mistakes in real estate?

  • No QR code, dedicated number or separate URL, so results can't be measured.
  • The RERA number in tiny type at the bottom of a full page.
  • Too many messages, amenities and renders on one page.
  • Booking print with no landing page or WhatsApp reply ready for the people who respond.
  • Judging print by "the phones rang" instead of site visits and bookings.

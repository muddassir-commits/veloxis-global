Newspaper ads are good at fast, wide local awareness on one day, such as a launch weekend, and at reaching older buyers and families who still read the paper. Digital ads (Meta and Google) are better at targeting, measurement and repeat follow-up. For most real estate projects the question isn't one or the other, but what each is for, and how to measure print as carefully as digital. Without tracking, a newspaper ad's results are a guess.

This guide compares the two, explains the main newspaper ad formats, what a real estate newspaper ad must include under RERA, how to track print enquiries, and how to combine both in a launch.

When newspaper ads still work for real estate

  • Launches and big events: a launch weekend, a price-release date, a site event, where you want many local people to hear about it on the same day.
  • Buyers who read the paper: older buyers and family decision-makers, who may influence a purchase even when a younger family member enquires online.
  • Credibility: some buyers see a large print ad as a sign of an established developer.
  • Local projects: a city or zonal edition can match a project's catchment well.

Newspaper is weaker for steady weekly lead flow, for testing different messages, and for reaching people who've moved to the city recently or who mostly use their phone for news.

Real estate newspaper ad formats

  • Front-page jacket or full page: maximum impact for a launch; the most expensive.
  • Half page or quarter page: common for project ads in the property section.
  • Property supplements: weekend real estate pullouts where buyers are already looking.
  • Classifieds: small text ads, mostly for resale, rentals and brokers.
  • Inserts: printed leaflets placed inside newspapers delivered in chosen areas.

Rates vary by publication, edition, page, size, colour and day, so get a current rate card from the newspaper or a media agency for your city before planning.

What a real estate newspaper ad must include

Under Section 11(2) of the RERA Act, 2016, every advertisement for a registered project must prominently show the project's RERA registration number and the website of the state RERA authority. In Uttar Pradesh, UP RERA also requires the project's QR code on advertisements and brochures. Print the number large enough to read. A RERA number in tiny type at the bottom of a full page doesn't meet the spirit of "prominently". See RERA advertisement rules for the full checklist.

Beyond compliance, a good newspaper ad has one main message, one picture that shows the real project or location, the price band if you're sharing it (with the asterisk explained), the location with a landmark, and one clear action. How to track enquiries from newspaper ads

You can measure print properly if you plan it before the ad goes to press:

  • A QR code that opens a dedicated landing page or WhatsApp chat with a tracking tag (for example ?utm_source=newspaper&utm_campaign=launch-oct).
  • A separate phone or WhatsApp number used only in that ad, so every call or message can be counted.
  • A short, separate web address such as yourproject.com/offer.
  • Ask every walk-in how they heard about the project, and record it on the site visit form.
  • Watch branded searches: a print ad often shows up as more Google searches for the project name in the days after. Check Google Search Console.

Then calculate the same numbers you use for digital: spend ÷ enquiries, spend ÷ site visits and spend ÷ bookings. Compare channels on cost per visit and per booking, as explained in our marketing budget guide.

Combining newspaper and digital in a launch

  1. Before the print date: Meta ads and Google search running; landing page and WhatsApp replies ready.
  2. Print day: the newspaper ad with a QR code and a dedicated number. Increase Google search budget on the project name, because people who saw the ad will search it.
  3. The following days: retarget everyone who visited the landing page with Meta ads showing the site, the sample flat and visit slots.
  4. The weekend: site visits, with every visitor's source recorded.
  5. After: compare cost per visit from print, Meta and Google, and plan the next launch on that basis.

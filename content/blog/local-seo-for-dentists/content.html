Local SEO for dentists means making your clinic the obvious answer when someone nearby searches "dentist near me" or a treatment plus your area. Do it with a complete Google Business Profile, one website page per treatment, a steady flow of genuine reviews and consistent clinic details everywhere, all within dental advertising ethics.

This guide is for dental clinic owners and practice managers in Indian cities such as Noida, Lucknow, Kanpur, Pune or Bengaluru, though the Google rules apply anywhere. It is a checklist you can work through yourself, and it adds what they skip: Google's rules for practitioner profiles, review requests that follow Google's policy, and the Indian ethics rules on how dentists may publicise their practice.

How does a dental clinic rank in Google Maps?

Google ranks local results on relevance, distance and prominence, so a clinic ranks by matching the search, being near the searcher and being well known. Google explains these three factors in Tips to improve your local ranking on Google, and adds that complete, accurate profiles and more reviews with positive ratings can help.

FactorWhat it means for a clinicWhat you control
RelevanceDoes your profile and site match "root canal in Sector 62" or "kids dentist Gomti Nagar"?Category, services list, one page per treatment
DistanceHow close the clinic is to the searcher or the place namedOnly accuracy: correct pin, address and locality
ProminenceHow well known the clinic is online and offlineGenuine reviews, local mentions, directory listings, links

You cannot move your clinic closer to every patient, so the work goes into relevance and prominence. A clinic a little further away with a full profile, real photos and many recent reviews can be shown ahead of a closer clinic with an empty listing.

How should a dental clinic set up its Google Business Profile?

Create one profile for the clinic, named exactly as on your signboard, with "Dentist" or the most precise dental category as primary, and decide carefully whether each dentist also needs a profile. The general setup steps are in our Google Business Profile checklist. Clinics have one extra decision: practitioner profiles.

Google's guidelines for representing your business say an individual practitioner, such as a dentist, may have their own profile if they are public-facing and can be contacted directly at the verified location during stated hours. Support staff should not have profiles, and a practitioner should not create several profiles for different specialities.

Your situationWhat Google's guidelines say
Several dentists work at one clinicThe clinic gets its own profile; each public-facing dentist may have a separate profile named only with the practitioner's name
One dentist, branded clinicShare one profile, named "[Clinic brand]: [Dentist name]"
A dental department in a hospitalA public-facing department that operates as a distinct entity may have its own profile
A dentist with several specialitiesOne profile only, not one per speciality

Keep the name clean. "Smile Dental Clinic" is fine; "Smile Dental Clinic Best Implant Dentist Noida" breaks Google's naming rule and also reads as a claim of superiority, which Indian dental ethics rules discourage (see below). Add your treatments in the services section, set accurate hours including emergency hours if you offer them, and upload real photos of the entrance, reception, treatment rooms and sterilisation area.

Why does every treatment need its own page?

Because patients search by treatment and area, and a separate page per treatment is what lets Google match your clinic to each of those searches. A single "Services" page listing twenty treatments gives Google very little to work with. A page called "Root canal treatment in Indirapuram" that explains the procedure, who it is for and how to book does.

Each treatment page should cover:

  • What the treatment is, in plain language, and when a dentist recommends it.
  • What happens at each visit and how to prepare.
  • Who in the clinic performs it, with their registered qualifications.
  • Fees, if you choose to publish them. The Dental Council of India's code of ethics allows clinic websites to show treatment facilities and fees as long as the information is factual.
  • Short FAQs patients actually ask at the front desk.
  • The clinic's name, address and phone, written exactly as on Google, plus a map and a booking button.

Write each page from your own knowledge. Avoid "painless", "guaranteed" or "best" unless you can prove it: these are the kind of false promises the ethics code warns against. How should a dental clinic ask for Google reviews?

Ask every patient after their visit with a direct review link or QR code, offer nothing in return, and never reveal treatment details when you reply. Google's tips to get more reviews recommend sharing a review link or QR code, and Google's prohibited and restricted content policy bans three things clinics often do:

  • Incentives: discounts, free cleanings or any reward for a review.
  • Selective asking: Google lists "selectively solicit positive reviews from customers" as rating manipulation, so ask every patient, not only the happy ones.
  • Conflicts of interest: reviews from current or former staff, or from people the clinic has a business relationship with.

Replies need extra care in healthcare. The Dental Council of India's code of ethics says confidences entrusted by patients should never be revealed unless the law requires it. So a reply should never confirm that the reviewer is a patient, mention their treatment or quote their records. A safe reply to a complaint: "Thank you for your feedback. We take every concern seriously. Please call the clinic so the clinic manager can look into this personally."

What advertising rules apply to dentists in India?

Dentists in India may advertise, but only with factual, dignified information: no false promises, no boasting about cures, no claims of being the best and no disproportionately large signs. These rules are in Chapter 6, clauses 8.1 to 8.3, of the Revised Dentists (Code of Ethics) Regulations, 2014. The parts that matter most for local SEO:

  • Directories and the internet are allowed as public information (clause 8.2.8), but any claim to superiority or special skills over others is treated as unethical.
  • Clinic websites are allowed where all information is factual, and may show treatment facilities and fees (clause 8.2.9). Non-factual or misleading claims are not.
  • No boasting of cases, operations or cures, and no permitting publication of such reports (clause 8.2).
  • No "best" or "first" claims when announcing new equipment or services (clause 8.2.7).
  • Specialist titles only with a special qualification recognised by the Council (clause 8.3.3).
  • Signs should only show what is needed to find the clinic; flickering light signs and disproportionately large signboards are listed as unethical (clauses 8.1.5 and 8.3.2).

In October 2026 the Ethics and Dental Registration Board of the National Dental Commission issued an advisory to all registered dentists, reported by Medical Dialogues. It cited clauses 8.1 to 8.3 and flagged broadcasting consultations and procedures, patient testimonials, before-and-after photographs, misleading treatment claims and oversized signboards, warning of disciplinary action. For local SEO, that means your profile photos should show the clinic, not patients' mouths, and your site should not carry testimonial videos or before-and-after galleries.

If you also run Google Ads, two Google policies apply. The healthcare and medicines policy restricts promotion of prescription drugs and some treatments by country. The personalised advertising policy treats health as a sensitive interest category, so you cannot remarket to past visitors using your own data segments for health-related ads.

This is general information, not legal advice. Your State Dental Council may have its own rules, so check with it before you publish ads or signage. Rules checked in November 2026.

How do your website and listings support local rankings?

Your website and every directory should show the same clinic name, address and phone as your Google profile, and your site should tell Google in code that you are a dental clinic. Google's local business structured data guide says to use the most specific LocalBusiness type possible, and schema.org has a Dentist type for exactly this.

  • Add Dentist structured data with your name, address, phone, hours and map link on the home and contact pages.
  • List the clinic on the main Indian directories patients use, such as Justdial and Practo, with identical details. Factual directory listings are allowed under clause 8.2.8.
  • Earn local mentions honestly: a talk at a resident welfare association, a school oral-health session run through a registered charitable body (the ethics code restricts promotional camps by clinics), or a quote in a local news story.
  • Answer common questions clearly on your pages. AI assistants increasingly answer "which dentist near me" questions too; our GEO guide explains how they pick sources.

Clinics with several branches should create one profile and one location page per branch, each with its own address, phone and photos. Never create profiles for areas where you have no staffed clinic.

A 30-day local SEO plan for a dental clinic

Spend the first month fixing the foundations in this order, then keep a short monthly routine.

  1. Week 1: claim and verify the Google Business Profile with the clinic's own Google account. Fix the name, primary category, hours, phone and pin location. Decide which dentists, if any, get practitioner profiles.
  2. Week 1: upload real photos of the outside, entrance, reception, treatment rooms and sterilisation area. No patients, no before-and-after images.
  3. Week 2: list every treatment in the profile's services section, then write one website page for your top treatments, starting with the ones patients ask about most.
  4. Week 2: add Dentist structured data and put the same name, address and phone in the footer of every page.
  5. Week 3: print the review QR code for reception and send the review link on WhatsApp to every patient after their visit, with no incentive.
  6. Week 3: correct or create your listings on the main directories so every detail matches.
  7. Week 4: reply to every review in general terms, post one factual update (new hours, a new service without "best" claims) and review the profile's calls, direction requests and website clicks.
  8. Every month after: new real photos, one update, replies to all reviews, one more treatment page, and a check of your listings for edits.

Common local SEO mistakes dental clinics make

  • Adding "best dentist" or the city name to the profile name.
  • Choosing a broad category such as "Doctor" or "Medical clinic" instead of "Dentist".
  • Offering a free scaling or a discount for a five-star review.
  • Replying to reviews with treatment details, which breaks patient confidentiality.
  • Posting before-and-after photos and patient testimonial videos.
  • Claiming to be a "specialist" without a qualification the Council recognises.
  • Letting an agency or freelancer own the Google profile instead of the clinic.

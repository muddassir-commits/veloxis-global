Google Ads and Meta ads do different jobs in real estate: Google Search captures buyers already searching for a project, locality or configuration, while Meta (Facebook and Instagram) creates demand among people who aren't searching yet. Most Indian projects need both. Lead with Meta for a new launch, with Google for a known project, and compare them on cost per site visit, not cost per lead.

"Google or Meta?" is one of the first questions builders, brokers and channel partners ask when they plan a campaign. This guide compares Google Ads and Meta ads for real estate in India: how each finds buyers, what public cost-per-lead benchmarks exist (and why we don't quote Indian ones), which to lead with for different projects, how to split a budget, and how to measure both fairly.

Which is better for real estate: Google Ads or Meta ads?

Neither is better on its own; they reach buyers at different moments:

  • Google Search reaches people already searching for a project, locality or configuration. Fewer leads, usually closer to buying.
  • Meta (Facebook and Instagram) reaches people who aren't searching yet. More leads, more filtering needed, and essential for launches and retargeting.

Put simply, Google captures demand that already exists, and Meta creates demand. A project that only uses Google is limited by how many people happen to search. A project that only uses Meta pays to find buyers and then can lose them when they later search the project name and click a competitor's or portal's search ad.

How does Google Ads find real estate buyers?

By showing your ad at the moment someone searches. When someone types "3 BHK in Sector 150 Noida" or your project's name, they have already decided what they want, and a search ad lets you appear with the right price and location.

Google works best when there is existing demand: an established locality, a known project name, or ready-to-move inventory. For a brand-new project, project-name searches are close to zero at first, so early volume comes from locality and configuration keywords. Negative keywords (rent, PG, jobs, resale for a new launch) matter a lot, because property searches attract many irrelevant clicks. The strongest real estate Google accounts usually have three parts:

  • Project name: protects your name from competitors and portals bidding on it.
  • Locality and configuration: "2 BHK in [locality]", "ready to move flats near [metro]". Most new-project volume comes from here.
  • Remarketing and YouTube: bringing back people who visited the landing page, with walkthrough videos.

How do Meta ads find real estate buyers?

By putting your project in front of likely buyers while they scroll, before they search. That makes Meta the main channel for launches, EOI drives and building a retargeting audience.

The trade-off is intent. Instant forms make enquiring very easy, so you also get curious scrollers. Qualifying questions, fast follow-up and sending site-visit data back to Meta are what turn Meta leads into visits. Because targeting is broad, the creative is your targeting: a video that opens with the locality and price band attracts people who care about that locality and can afford it. Is there a real estate CPL benchmark for Google and Meta ads in India?

Not one we can honestly quote. We searched for a public India real estate cost-per-lead benchmark for Google or Meta with a stated sample and method, and did not find one as of 5 Nov 2026. Figures that circulate on agency blogs and social posts rarely say which city, segment, form type or period they come from, so they can't be compared with your campaigns.

The best-known published benchmark is from the US. LocaliQ's 2026 real estate search advertising benchmarks, based on 894 US search campaigns from April 2025 to March 2026, report a median cost per lead of $102.51, cost per click of $3.22, click-through rate of 7.61% and conversion rate of 3.70%. These are US dollar figures from a different market with different property prices and competition: use them to understand the shape of the funnel, not as a target for an Indian project.

How to calculate your own CPL benchmark

  1. Tag every lead with its source (Google Search, Meta form, Meta WhatsApp, retargeting) in your CRM.
  2. Run both channels for a few weeks with enough budget for each to collect real data.
  3. Calculate four numbers per channel: spend ÷ leads (CPL), spend ÷ qualified leads, spend ÷ site visits, spend ÷ bookings.
  4. Use the first month as your baseline and judge every change against it, not against figures from other markets.
  5. Recalculate monthly, because costs change with season, competition and creative.

When should a project lead with Google, and when with Meta?

  • New launch with little awareness: lead with Meta for volume and retargeting; run Google on locality and configuration searches.
  • Established project or known brand: Google on project-name and locality searches first; Meta for retargeting and offers.
  • Ready-to-move inventory: Google "ready to move" searches; Meta retargeting to past enquiries.
  • Plotted developments: Meta video to show the layout and surroundings; Google for "plots in [locality]" searches.
  • Luxury projects: smaller, carefully built Meta audiences and YouTube for reach; Google for project-name searches (see luxury real estate marketing).
  • NRI buyers: both, in separate campaigns, with the housing category declared on Meta where ads reach the US, Canada or Europe.
  • Channel partners with small budgets: Google on the specific projects you are mandated on, plus Meta retargeting of your page visitors.

How should you split the budget between Google and Meta?

Start with a sensible split and change it by results. For a launch with little awareness, start with more on Meta, because there isn't much search demand yet; our example launch plan uses roughly 60% Meta and 40% Google as a planning assumption, not a result. For a known project with steady searches, the balance often tips the other way. Then:

  1. Give each channel enough budget and time, usually a few weeks, before judging it.
  2. Compare channels on cost per site visit, not cost per lead.
  3. Move budget weekly, in small steps, towards the channel and campaigns that produce visits more cheaply.
  4. Keep a small fixed budget for retargeting on both; see real estate retargeting ads.

How do Google and Meta ads work together?

  • Meta creates searches. People who see a Meta video often search the project name later. Without a project-name search campaign, a competitor or portal may catch them.
  • Google visitors feed Meta retargeting. People who clicked a search ad but didn't enquire can see walkthrough videos and offers on Instagram.
  • One landing page, one follow-up. Both channels should send buyers to the same fast project landing page, and every lead should get the same instant WhatsApp reply.

This is also why judging each channel on its own dashboard misleads: a Meta campaign can look weak on its own numbers while producing many of the project-name searches that Google gets credit for.

How do you measure Google and Meta ads fairly?

On cost per site visit and cost per booking from your CRM, not the ad dashboards. Every lead needs a source tag, and every site visit must be recorded against the lead. Send those visits back to both platforms, through Meta's Conversions API for CRM and Google's enhanced conversions for leads, so their algorithms learn what a buyer looks like instead of what a form-filler looks like.

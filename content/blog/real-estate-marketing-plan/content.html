A real estate marketing plan is a written plan that says who you are selling to, what you'll tell them, which channels you'll use, how much you'll spend, how enquiries will be handled and how you'll measure results, usually for one project or one quarter. It fits on two or three pages. The value is not the document itself but the decisions it forces you to make before money is spent.

Below are the 7 steps to write one, a one-page template, an example for a mid-size project, and a version for individual agents and brokers.

How to write a real estate marketing plan: 7 steps

1. Set the goal in bookings, not leads

Start from the sales target: for example, "sell 60 units in Tower A in six months". Then work backwards using your own past numbers: how many site visits you usually need per booking, and how many leads per visit. If you have no history, set a first estimate, run the first month, and correct it with real numbers.

2. Define the buyer

Write down who is most likely to buy: configuration, budget, where they live now, why they'd move, and what worries them (possession date, loan, location). A project for first-time buyers in Noida Extension needs a different plan from one for NRI investors. If you sell to more than one group, plan each separately.

3. Write the positioning

One sentence on why this buyer should choose this project over the alternatives, with proof: location facts, the developer's delivery record, price band, specification. This sentence drives every ad, brochure and sales conversation. See real estate branding and slogans and taglines.

4. Choose the channels

Match each channel to a job:

  • Meta ads (Facebook, Instagram): reach people who aren't searching yet, and collect leads. Meta ads guide
  • Google search ads: catch people searching for the project, the builder or the locality. Google ads guide
  • Property portals: buyers comparing listings. 99acres vs MagicBricks
  • Channel partners: buyers who work through brokers. Channel partner guide
  • Hoardings and print: local awareness on the routes buyers travel. Newspaper vs digital ads
  • Organic: social media, Google Business Profile, SEO, WhatsApp updates to past enquiries.

A small budget is better spent on two channels done properly than on six done badly. The comparison of Google Ads vs Meta Ads helps decide where to start.

5. Set the budget

Give each channel a monthly amount and a test period, and keep some money back to move into whatever works. Our real estate marketing budget guide explains how to calculate it from your own cost per booking.

6. Plan the follow-up

Most plans stop at the ads. Write down who replies to enquiries, how fast, with what message, how leads are qualified and how site visits are booked. A plan that brings 500 leads to a team that calls back the next day will underperform a plan with half the leads and an instant reply. See lead management and how to book site visits.

7. Decide what to measure, and when to review

Pick a few numbers and review them weekly: leads, qualified leads, site visits and bookings by source, and cost per visit and per booking. Set a monthly review where budget moves from the weakest channel to the strongest.

Example: a plan for a mid-size launch

An illustration (not a real client) for a 200-unit project of 2 and 3 BHKs in a growing suburb:

  • Goal: 40 bookings in the first four months after launch.
  • Buyer: families renting within 10 km, buying their first or second home with a loan.
  • Positioning: "Ready-to-move-in-2028 homes 10 minutes from [metro], from a developer with three delivered projects in the city", with each claim matching the RERA filing.
  • Channels: Meta lead ads and Google search for the project and locality; two hoardings on the main commuter road; 15 briefed channel partners; a project landing page with WhatsApp.
  • Follow-up: instant WhatsApp reply with the brochure and two questions, a call within the hour during office time, weekend visit slots offered as two choices.
  • Review: every Monday: leads, visits and bookings by source. After month one, the weakest campaign's budget moves to the strongest.

A marketing plan for real estate agents and brokers

An agent's plan is smaller but follows the same steps:

  • Pick a patch: one or two localities and one type of property you know better than anyone.
  • Be findable: a complete Google Business Profile, RERA agent number on everything, a simple website or listing page (local SEO guide).
  • Post weekly: listings, locality updates and short videos (Instagram Reels guide).
  • Choose one paid source to test, a portal package or a small Meta campaign, and track cost per deal.
  • Follow up every enquiry the same day and ask every happy client for a review and a referral.

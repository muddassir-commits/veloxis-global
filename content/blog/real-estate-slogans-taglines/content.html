What is the difference between a slogan and a tagline?

A tagline belongs to the company and lasts for years; a slogan belongs to a project or campaign and changes with it. People use the words interchangeably (see slogan and tagline on Wikipedia), but the split is useful:

TaglineSlogan
Belongs toThe company or broker brandA project, phase or campaign
LifespanYearsMonths, or one launch
SaysWhat the business stands forWhy this project, now
Where it sitsUnder the logo, letterhead, website headerHoardings, ad headlines, brochure cover
Example"Homes built the way we'd build our own.""Ready to move. Ready for Diwali."

A developer usually needs one tagline plus a new slogan for each project. A broker or channel partner usually needs one tagline that says why to call them instead of the next agent.

What makes a good real estate slogan?

A good real estate slogan is short, specific, about the buyer, true and easy to say.

  • Short: three to seven words, so it fits a hoarding read from a moving car. Our real estate hoarding design guide explains why.
  • Specific: it mentions something only your project has: location, view, possession date, size or price band. "10 minutes from the airport" beats "Connected living".
  • About the buyer: "Your parents will love the park" works harder than "A landmark by XYZ".
  • True: every claim must match the RERA filing and the facts on site. A slogan promising possession earlier than the filing is a liability (see RERA advertisement rules).
  • Easy to say: if a buyer can repeat it to family, it spreads.

How do you write a real estate slogan?

List the facts, pick one idea, write many versions, cut hard, then test.

  1. List the facts. Location, distances, configurations, price band, possession date, standout amenity, who the buyer is.
  2. Pick one idea. The single thing that would make your buyer choose this project.
  3. Write 20 versions. Short, rhyming, a question, a promise, English, Hindi. Don't judge yet.
  4. Cut to five. Remove anything vague, anything another project could use, and anything you can't prove.
  5. Check it's free to use. Search the line online and in the IP India trade mark register so you don't copy someone else's mark.
  6. Test. Run two or three as ad headlines with the same image and budget, and keep the one that brings the most enquiries.

What slogan mistakes should you avoid?

  • Generic lines any project could use.
  • Unprovable claims such as "best", "No. 1" or guaranteed returns. The ASCI Code requires advertising claims to be truthful and substantiated, and RERA makes promoters answer for false ad claims.
  • Too many ideas in one line.
  • Copying a famous brand's slogan, which can raise trade mark problems.
  • Never testing which line actually brings enquiries.

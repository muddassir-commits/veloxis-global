When someone searches “property dealer near me” or “real estate agent in [locality]”, Google shows the map results first, above the websites. For brokers and channel partners, a well-kept Google Business Profile is the cheapest source of local enquiries you can build. It costs nothing, it works while you sleep, and most competitors leave theirs half finished. This guide covers local SEO for real estate step by step: setting up the profile correctly, the description, photos and posts, reviews, local citations, and the weekly routine that keeps you in the map results.

Why a Google Business Profile matters

Google ranks local results mainly on three things:

  • Relevance: how well your profile matches the search. Categories, services, description and reviews all feed this.
  • Distance: how far you are from the searcher, or from the place they named in the search.
  • Prominence: how well known you are. Google looks at reviews, links, mentions across the web and how complete and active your profile is.

You can’t change distance, but you can improve relevance and prominence, and that is what the rest of this guide is about.

The profile also matters beyond the map. People who hear your name from a builder or a past client often search for you before calling. A complete profile with real photos and genuine reviews is what they see first.

Who can have a profile, and is it free?

A Google Business Profile is free. There is no paid version that ranks better.

To be eligible, a business must make in-person contact with customers during its stated hours, either at its own premises or by travelling to customers. A broker who meets clients at the office or takes them on site visits qualifies. So does a developer’s staffed sales office. A purely online operation with no in-person contact doesn’t.

A few rules follow from this:

  • One profile per real location. Don’t create profiles for localities where you have no office, and don’t use a virtual office or a co-working address you don’t staff. These are common reasons for suspension.
  • A project site office can have a profile only if it is staffed during the hours you list, and it should be named as the developer’s sales office, not as a stand-alone “best flats” business.
  • Individual agents inside an agency can have their own profile if they deal with the public directly, but the agency and agent profiles must not duplicate each other.

Setting it up correctly

  • Name: your real business name, exactly as on your signboard and documents. Adding keywords, like “Sharma Realty – Best Flats in Noida”, breaks Google’s guidelines and can get the profile suspended.
  • Primary category: “Real estate agency” or “Real estate agent”. This is the single most important relevance setting. Add “Real estate consultant” as a secondary category if it fits, and “Real estate developer” only if you are one.
  • Phone and website: the same number and website you use everywhere else. A WhatsApp-enabled number is ideal, because many local enquiries arrive as chats.
  • Services: list what you actually do: new projects, resale, plots, commercial, rentals if you handle them, NRI assistance. Each service is another relevance signal.
  • Opening hours: real hours, including Sundays if you do weekend site visits, and special hours for holidays.
  • Verification: complete it. An unverified profile doesn’t show properly in search, and Google may ask for a video showing your signage, office and proof of business.

How to write a real estate business description

You get up to 750 characters, and the first two lines matter most because they show before “more”. A description that works has four parts:

  1. Who you are and where: “A RERA-registered real estate agency helping families buy new and resale homes in Noida Extension and Greater Noida West since 2018.”
  2. What you handle: the kinds of property, price bands and buyers you serve.
  3. Why choose you: something specific and true, like the developers you are empanelled with, your site-visit support, or help with home loans and registration.
  4. What to do next: “Message us on WhatsApp to book a free site visit.”

Include your RERA agent registration number. Don’t stuff in lists of localities or phrases like “best property dealer”. Google ignores that for ranking and buyers find it off-putting. Don’t put links or promotional prices in the description either; posts are the place for offers.

Office address or service area?

If clients visit your office, show the address. If you meet clients at sites and don’t receive visitors, set it up as a service-area business: hide the address and list the areas you cover, for example specific sectors or localities. Keep the service area realistic, roughly the distance you would actually drive for a client. A profile that claims to serve the whole of Delhi NCR is less relevant to each locality than one that honestly covers five sectors.

Photos, posts and updates

  • Real photos of your office, your team and the projects you are mandated on, not stock images. Add new ones every few weeks; profiles with fresh photos look active to buyers and to Google.
  • A short video of a site visit or a sample flat walkthrough, recorded on a phone, is often the most-viewed item on a real estate profile.
  • Regular posts for new launches, site-visit weekends, offers and useful updates such as new metro stations or road openings. Link each post to the relevant project page on your website.
  • Accurate opening hours, including holidays.
  • Common questions answered. If your profile still shows a Q&A section, add and answer the questions buyers really ask, such as “Do you charge brokerage on new projects?”. Otherwise answer them in your description and posts.

Getting reviews the right way

Ask every client for an honest review after a site visit, booking or registration, and make it easy with a direct review link sent on WhatsApp. Don’t offer incentives, don’t write reviews yourself, and don’t ask only happy clients. Google’s policies prohibit fake and selectively solicited reviews, and profiles that break them can lose reviews or be suspended.

Reply to every review, including negative ones, calmly and specifically. A good reply to a complaint thanks the person, addresses the actual issue, and offers to continue the conversation offline. Future clients read replies as closely as the reviews themselves.

The timing matters. The best moment to ask is right after something went well: a smooth site visit, a booking, a registration completed on time. A short, personal WhatsApp message with the link works far better than a generic request weeks later.

Citations: your business details across the web

Google cross-checks your business name, address and phone number (NAP) against other sites. When they match everywhere, it trusts your profile more. In India the listings that matter most for real estate are:

  • Your own website’s contact page and footer.
  • Property portals where you have an agent profile, such as 99acres, MagicBricks and Housing.com.
  • General directories like Justdial and Sulekha.
  • Your state RERA agent registration, which shows your registered name and address.
  • Your LinkedIn company page, Facebook page and Instagram profile.

Use exactly the same name, number and address format on all of them. If you have moved office or changed numbers, update the old listings; conflicting details are one of the most common reasons a profile struggles to rank.

Connect it to a page that converts

Link the profile to a page that explains what you do, the localities you cover and the projects you handle, with WhatsApp and call buttons. For project-specific posts, link to the project landing page rather than your homepage.

A local page on your website also supports the profile. A page about the localities you serve, with real information about each one (connectivity, price bands, the projects you handle there), gives Google another relevance signal and gives buyers a reason to contact you rather than a portal. Track what the profile brings in

The profile’s Performance view shows searches, calls, chats, direction requests and website clicks. Two small steps make it much more useful:

  • Tag the website link with UTM parameters, for example ?utm_source=google&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=gbp. Visits from the profile then show up separately in Google Analytics.
  • Ask every new enquiry how they found you, and note it in your CRM or sheet. Profile leads often call directly, and without this they go uncounted.

A 30-minute weekly routine

  1. Publish one post: a project update, site-visit weekend, or locality news.
  2. Add two or three new real photos.
  3. Reply to every new review.
  4. Send review links to the clients you helped that week.
  5. Check that the hours, phone and services are still correct.

Done every week for three months, this routine usually puts a profile well ahead of competitors who set theirs up once and forgot it.

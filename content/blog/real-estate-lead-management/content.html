Real estate lead management is the process of capturing every property enquiry in one place, replying within minutes, qualifying the buyer, following up until each lead books a site visit or says no, and measuring which sources bring bookings. Most builders and brokers don't lose deals because they have too few leads. They lose them in the gap between the enquiry and the first useful conversation.

This guide is for developers, brokers and channel partners who get enquiries from ads, portals and WhatsApp. It covers what a lead is, the types of leads, a step-by-step lead management process, how fast to reply, a stage system, follow-up rules, what a real estate CRM should do, automation, and the numbers to watch every week.

What is a real estate lead?

A real estate lead is a person who has shared their contact details and shown interest in buying, selling or renting property: by filling a form, sending a WhatsApp message, calling, enquiring on a portal or walking into a site office. A lead is not yet a buyer. Lead management covers everything between "enquiry received" and "booking done" or "closed, not interested".

What are the types of real estate leads?

Sort leads by where they came from, because each source behaves differently:

SourceWhat to expect
Meta lead forms (Facebook, Instagram)Easy to submit, so many are early-stage; qualification matters most
Google search adsPeople were actively searching for a project, builder or locality
Website or landing page formThe person has seen your page; usually warmer than an instant form
WhatsApp click-to-chatConversation starts instantly; reply while they're still on the phone
Property portalsOften sent to several sellers at once, so speed decides who gets the conversation (portals compared)
Channel partnersPre-qualified by the broker; follow the registration rules in the CP agreement
Referrals and walk-insUsually the warmest; make sure they're entered in the system too

Then sort by readiness: hot (budget and timeline fit, wants a visit), warm (interested, not ready yet), cold (researching, or no reply) and closed (booked, or not a fit). Our guide on how to generate real estate leads covers the sources in detail.

The real estate lead management process, step by step

A working lead management process has the same seven steps whatever tool you use:

  1. Capture: every enquiry from every source lands in one list (CRM or shared sheet), with the source recorded automatically.
  2. Acknowledge instantly: an automatic WhatsApp or SMS reply with the brochure, price band and one or two questions.
  3. Assign: each lead goes to one named salesperson, by project, locality or round-robin, so no lead belongs to "everyone".
  4. Call and qualify: the salesperson calls with context and confirms configuration, budget, timeline and purpose.
  5. Book the site visit: two specific time options, a location pin and reminders.
  6. Follow up: every lead has a next action and date until it books or says no.
  7. Measure and feed back: report by source weekly, and send qualified leads and site visits back to the ad platforms.

Steps 1, 2 and the reminders in step 5 can be automated. Steps 4 and 5 work best with a person.

How fast should you reply to a real estate lead?

Reply within minutes if you can, and within an hour at the latest. In a study published in Harvard Business Review (Oldroyd, McElheran and Elkington, March 2011), firms that contacted online leads within an hour were nearly seven times as likely to qualify the lead as firms that waited longer, and more than 60 times as likely as firms that waited 24 hours or more. The study looked at US companies, not Indian property, but the pattern is easy to test in your own data.

A buyer who enquires at 10 pm has usually enquired on other projects too. The practical answer is an instant WhatsApp reply (brochure, price band, two questions, a visit slot) followed by a call from a person as soon as the office opens. See WhatsApp automation for real estate leads.

What lead stages should a real estate team use?

Use a short set of stages where every lead sits in exactly one stage, with a next action and a date:

StageMeansNext action
NewEnquiry received, auto-reply sentFirst call today
ContactedA person has spoken or chatted with themFinish qualifying
QualifiedBudget, configuration, location and timeline fitOffer two visit slots
Visit bookedDate, time and location pin sentReminders; meet them
Visit doneThey came; notes on likes and objectionsAnswer objections; family visit
Negotiation / EOIUnit, price or EOI under discussionWritten offer or EOI form
Booked / ClosedBooked, or not interested with a reasonHandover, or record the reason

Four questions are usually enough to qualify: which configuration, what budget range, when they plan to buy, and whether it's for living or investment. Our guide to real estate lead qualification questions gives the wording and a simple scoring system.

Follow-up rules that stop leads going cold

  • No lead without a next date. If the next action isn't in the system, it won't happen.
  • Each follow-up adds something new: a floor plan, a walkthrough video, the payment plan, a construction update. "Just following up" gets ignored.
  • Offer two visit times instead of "when would you like to come?".
  • Stop when they say no, and record the reason. "Budget too low" and "wrong location" tell you how to fix your ads.
  • Respect consent and channel rules. On the WhatsApp Business Platform, messages outside the customer service window that opens when the buyer last wrote to you must use approved templates (WhatsApp pricing and messaging docs). India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 expects personal data to be used for the purpose it was given for.

What is a CRM in real estate, and what should it do?

A real estate CRM (customer relationship management system) is software that stores every lead with its source, stage, notes, calls, messages and next action, and reminds the team what to do each day. For a small broker, a shared Google Sheet with the stage columns above can work to start. Once leads come from several sources, or more than two or three people handle them, a CRM stops leads falling through the gaps. For a buying guide, see how to choose a real estate CRM in India.

How do you automate real estate lead management?

Automate the parts people do badly at 11 pm or on a busy Sunday: the first reply, the qualifying questions, the brochure, reminders and visit confirmations. Keep people on what automation does badly: understanding the buyer, answering hard questions and negotiating. A typical setup is ad → landing page or lead form → CRM → instant WhatsApp reply → salesperson alert → follow-up reminders → outcomes sent back to the ads. Common lead management mistakes

  • Leads in five places: some in a portal inbox, some on a salesperson's phone, some in a sheet. Nobody sees the whole picture.
  • No owner: leads shared in a group chat get called by everyone or by no one.
  • Judging sources by lead count: the cheapest leads are often the ones that never visit. Judge by cost per site visit and per booking.
  • No closed reasons: without them you can't fix targeting.
  • Over-messaging: repeated messages without new information push buyers to block you.

What should a real estate sales team measure every week?

  • Time to first reply, by source and by salesperson.
  • Leads with no contact after 24 hours, which should be zero.
  • Lead to qualified, qualified to visit, and visit to booking rates, by source.
  • Cost per site visit and cost per booking for each campaign.
  • Reasons for closing leads, to improve targeting.

WhatsApp automation for real estate means every new enquiry gets an instant WhatsApp reply with the brochure and one question, the buyer is qualified with tap-to-answer buttons, the hot lead is passed to a salesperson, and site visit confirmations, reminders and follow-ups go out on their own. It needs the WhatsApp Business Platform (API), approved templates and opted-in leads.

Property buyers enquire with several projects at once, often in the evening. The project that replies first, with something useful, usually gets the conversation. This guide explains why leads go cold, what you need to automate WhatsApp, a lead-response flow that turns enquiries into site visits, what Meta charges, the rules WhatsApp and Indian law set, and how to measure whether it is working.

Why do real estate leads go cold?

Real estate leads go cold mainly because of slow or single follow-up, not because the ads were bad. The usual causes:

  • Late replies. Leads that arrive after office hours wait until the next day, by which time the buyer has spoken to other projects.
  • One attempt. A salesperson calls once, gets no answer, and moves on.
  • Scattered sources. Portal emails, Meta forms, website forms and personal WhatsApp chats, with no single list of who was contacted.
  • Unknown numbers. Many buyers screen calls from numbers they don't recognise. A WhatsApp message from a named business is easier to answer.
  • Nothing useful in the first contact. "Sir, you enquired about our project?" gives the buyer no reason to reply. The brochure and a price band do.

None of these are ad problems, but they make ads look expensive. When builders say their leads are junk, a large part of the problem is often here, in the first hour after the enquiry. Our guide on how to generate real estate leads covers the other half: where good leads come from.

Can you automate the WhatsApp Business app, or do you need the API?

The free WhatsApp Business app offers only basic automation: greeting and away messages and quick replies. Full automation (instant replies to ad and website leads, chatbot questions, routing, reminders and CRM sync) needs the WhatsApp Business Platform, usually called the API. What do you need to automate WhatsApp for real estate?

  • Access to the WhatsApp Business Platform, directly through Meta's Cloud API or through one of Meta's solution providers. You need a Meta business portfolio and a phone number for the business.
  • Approved message templates for the first message, reminders and follow-ups. Templates are required for any message sent outside the 24-hour customer service window that opens when a buyer messages you (Meta pricing documentation).
  • A connection to your lead sources: Meta lead forms, your landing page, Google Ads lead forms and portal lead emails, so every enquiry triggers the same flow.
  • A CRM or a Google Sheet where every lead and conversation is recorded (see how to choose a real estate CRM).
  • Opt-in wording on your forms that names your business and says you will contact the buyer on WhatsApp.
  • A human owner. Automation answers instantly, but someone must pick up qualified leads the same day.

How to set up WhatsApp automation, step by step

  1. Map your lead sources and decide which ones trigger the flow (Meta forms, website, Google, portals).
  2. Choose a provider or the Cloud API, and verify your business in Meta Business Manager. Verification also matters for limits: new accounts start with a limit of 250 unique contacts messaged outside a service window per 24 hours, and verifying the business is one path to raise it to 2,000 (Meta messaging limits).
  3. Register the number and set the display name and profile (project or company name, website, address of the site office if any).
  4. Write and submit templates: welcome, visit confirmation, reminder, no-show, follow-up.
  5. Write the project fact sheet the bot answers from: price band, configurations, sizes, RERA number, possession date, location.
  6. Build the flow (next section) and the routing rules.
  7. Test end to end with your own numbers from every lead source, then train the sales team on the handover.

What should a real estate WhatsApp automation flow do?

A good flow does six things in order: reply, qualify, hand over, book, remind and follow up.

  1. Instant reply: "Hi [Name], thanks for your interest in [Project]. Here is the brochure. Are you looking for 2 BHK or 3 BHK?" Keep a light, phone-friendly edition of the file; our guide to the real estate brochure covers what to put in it.
  2. Qualify: budget band and buying timeline, with quick-reply buttons so the buyer taps instead of typing. Meta's WhatsApp Flows can also show a short in-chat form.
  3. Hand over: qualified buyers are assigned to a salesperson, who is notified immediately with the answers so far.
  4. Book the visit: the buyer picks one of two offered slots.
  5. Remind: the evening before and a couple of hours before the visit, with the location pin.
  6. Follow up: leads who go quiet get a short sequence: walkthrough video, construction update, visit invitation, then a polite close.

Keep the first message short. One useful thing (the brochure) and one easy question is enough. Buyers who answer the question are telling you they are real; that single reply separates genuine interest from accidental form fills better than any filter.

How much does WhatsApp automation cost?

There are two costs: Meta's message charges and your provider's or developer's fee. According to Meta's pricing page (checked 5 November 2026):

  • Meta charges per delivered message, by category (marketing, utility, authentication, service) and the recipient's country. Messages from buyers to you are free.
  • Every business phone number gets 1,000 free service messages a month. From 1 October 2026, service messages beyond that, and utility messages sent inside an open service window, are charged.
  • When a buyer messages you from a click-to-WhatsApp ad, a free entry point window opens for up to 7 days in which template and service messages are not charged.
  • For businesses billed in India, Meta is moving accounts to billing in rupees; eligible accounts must migrate by 31 December 2026.

Check Meta's current India rate card before budgeting, because rates change.

How should leads be routed to sales and channel partners?

Decide the rules before you automate: by project, by location, or in rotation. Record the time each lead arrived and was assigned. For developers working with channel partners, that timestamp is what settles most "whose lead is this?" disputes.

  • A lead that says it came through a CP is tagged to that CP, and the CP is notified.
  • A returning buyer goes back to the salesperson who handled them before, not into the rotation.
  • Leads that aren't picked up within a set time move to the next person automatically.

What are the rules for WhatsApp automation in India?

  • Only message people who opted in. Meta's opt-in rules say the person must have given you their number and agreed to receive messages, the opt-in must name your business, and you must follow applicable law. Bought databases don't qualify.
  • Make opting out easy. Include a "Stop" option in marketing messages and respect it.
  • Keep quality high. Meta only scales your messaging limits when message quality is high (messaging limits). Blocks and reports hurt it.
  • Handle personal data carefully. Under the DPDP Act, 2023 and the DPDP Rules notified on 14 November 2025, every business must give a clear consent notice explaining the specific purpose of data use, within an eighteen-month phased compliance period (PIB). Limit who can see lead data.
  • RERA details on promotions. Treat promotional broadcasts as advertisements: show the RERA registration number and website. See RERA advertisement rules.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Messaging people who didn't ask to be contacted.
  • Bots that trap buyers. Always offer "Talk to someone".
  • Long first messages. Send one useful thing and one question.
  • Asking for information the buyer already gave in the form.
  • Automating without a human owner. Someone must still call qualified leads quickly.
  • Broadcasting the same offer to everyone every week. Blocks and reports climb, and delivery suffers.

What should you measure after automating?

  • Time from enquiry to first reply, which should be seconds.
  • Share of leads who answer the first question.
  • Site visits booked and completed.
  • Leads with no human contact after 24 hours. Aim for zero.
  • Cost per site visit by lead source, once visits are recorded against the original ad.

Look at these weekly for the first month. The first question's reply rate tells you whether the message is right. The booked-to-completed visit rate tells you whether the reminders are working.

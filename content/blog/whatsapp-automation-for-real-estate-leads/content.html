Property buyers enquire with several projects at once, often in the evening. The project that replies first, with something useful, usually gets the conversation. WhatsApp automation makes sure that is you. This guide explains what you need to automate WhatsApp for real estate leads, a lead-response flow that turns enquiries into site visits, the rules WhatsApp and Indian law set, and how to measure whether it’s working.

Why real estate leads go cold

  • Late replies. Leads that arrive after office hours wait until the next day, by which time the buyer has spoken to two other projects.
  • One attempt. A salesperson calls once, gets no answer, and moves on.
  • Scattered sources. Portal emails, Meta forms, website forms and personal WhatsApp chats, with no single list of who was contacted.
  • Unknown numbers. Buyers screen calls from numbers they don’t recognise. A WhatsApp message from a named business is easier to answer.
  • Nothing useful in the first contact. “Sir, you enquired about our project?” gives the buyer no reason to reply. The brochure and a price band do.

None of these are ad problems, but they make ads look expensive. WhatsApp Business app vs WhatsApp Business API

Most brokers start with the free WhatsApp Business app, and it is fine for one person handling a few chats. It stops working once leads arrive from ads in volume.

If you already use the app with a number buyers know, that number can usually be moved to the API. It stops working in the phone app once it is migrated, so plan the switch.

What you need to automate WhatsApp

  • The WhatsApp Business API, set up directly on Meta’s Cloud API or through an official Business Solution Provider. You will need a verified Meta Business account and a phone number that isn’t already active on personal WhatsApp.
  • Approved message templates for the first message, reminders and follow-ups. WhatsApp requires templates for any message sent outside the 24-hour window that opens when a customer messages you. Templates are reviewed by Meta, usually quickly.
  • A connection to your lead sources: Meta lead forms, your landing page, Google Ads lead forms and portal lead emails or APIs, so every enquiry triggers the same flow.
  • A CRM or a Google Sheet where every lead and conversation is recorded.
  • A human owner. Automation answers instantly, but someone must pick up qualified leads the same day.

A simple lead-response flow

  1. Instant reply: “Hi [Name], thanks for your interest in [Project]. Here is the brochure. Are you looking for 2 BHK or 3 BHK?” Keep a light, phone-friendly edition of the file for this; our guide to the real estate brochure covers what to put in it.
  2. Qualify: budget band and buying timeline, with quick-reply buttons so the buyer taps instead of typing.
  3. Hand over: qualified buyers are assigned to a salesperson, who is notified immediately with the answers so far.
  4. Book the visit: the buyer picks a slot on WhatsApp.
  5. Remind: the day before and a couple of hours before the visit, with the location pin.
  6. Follow up: leads who go quiet get a short sequence: walkthrough video, construction update, visit invitation.

Keep the first message short. One useful thing (the brochure) and one easy question is enough. Buyers who answer the question are telling you they are real; that single reply separates genuine interest from accidental form fills better than any filter.

Message templates that get replies

Every automated message sent outside the 24-hour window must use an approved template. A small set covers most real estate needs:

Meta sorts templates into categories, and marketing messages cost more than utility messages such as confirmations and reminders. Name the project and the buyer, keep templates short, and avoid anything that reads like a mass promotion.

Routing leads to sales and CPs

Decide the rules before you automate: by project, by location, or in rotation. Record the time each lead arrived and was assigned. For developers working with channel partners, that timestamp is what settles most “whose lead is this?” disputes.

A few routing rules that prevent problems later:

  • A lead that says it came through a CP is tagged to that CP, and the CP is notified.
  • A returning buyer goes back to the salesperson who handled them before, not into the rotation.
  • Leads that aren’t picked up within a set time move to the next person automatically.

Rules to follow: consent, opt-out and data

  • Only message people who asked. WhatsApp’s policies require opt-in. A form that clearly says you will contact the buyer on WhatsApp covers enquiries; bought databases don’t.
  • Make opting out easy. Include a “Stop” option in marketing messages and respect it.
  • Keep the quality rating healthy. If many recipients block or report your number, WhatsApp limits how many people you can message, and eventually the number itself.
  • Handle personal data carefully. India’s Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 expects personal data to be used for the purpose it was given for, and protected. Limit who can see lead data, and don’t share it outside the people handling the enquiry.

This is general guidance, not legal advice.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Messaging people who didn’t ask to be contacted.
  • Bots that trap buyers. Always offer “Talk to someone”.
  • Long first messages. Send one useful thing and one question.
  • Asking for information the buyer already gave in the form.
  • Automating without a human owner. Someone must still call qualified leads quickly.
  • Broadcasting the same offer to everyone every week. Blocks and reports climb, and delivery suffers.

What to measure

  • Time from enquiry to first reply, which should be seconds.
  • Share of leads who answer the first question.
  • Site visits booked and completed.
  • Leads with no human contact after 24 hours. Aim for zero.
  • Cost per site visit by lead source, once visits are recorded against the original ad.

Look at these weekly for the first month. The first question’s reply rate tells you whether the message is right. The booked-to-completed visit rate tells you whether the reminders are working.

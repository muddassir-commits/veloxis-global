To sell plots online, put every plot on a map with its exact size, facing, road width and price band, show the approvals and RERA number up front, and answer every enquiry on WhatsApp the same hour with a location pin and a site-visit slot. Plot buyers ask different questions from flat buyers. They worry about title, approvals, access roads and what will be built around them. An online plan that answers those questions before the call sells plots faster than ads alone.

This guide covers who buys plots, what to put on the page, the photos and videos that work, where to advertise, and how to follow up so serious buyers book a site visit.

Who buys plots, and what they worry about

Plot buyers usually fall into three groups, and each needs a slightly different message:

  • End users who want to build their own house later. They care about the neighbourhood, schools, water, electricity and how soon others will build around them.
  • Investors who expect the land to appreciate. They care about upcoming infrastructure (expressways, airports, metro lines), the price per square yard and how easy it will be to resell.
  • NRIs and buyers from other cities who can't visit easily. They need video, documents and a trusted person on the ground. Our page on reaching NRI buyers covers this group.

All three share the same fears: unclear title, missing approvals, encroachment, and a plot that looks different in person from the brochure. Your online material should remove those doubts one by one.

What to show for every plot

A plot listing with only a price and a photo of empty land gets few serious enquiries. Show the facts a buyer would otherwise ask for on the phone:

  • Layout plan with plot numbers, sizes (in square yards or square metres, whichever buyers in your area use), facing, corner plots and road widths.
  • Availability: which plots are sold, booked or available. Update it every week; an old layout destroys trust.
  • Approvals: the authority that approved the layout, the RERA registration number where the project needs one, and the land-use status. Put the RERA number on the page and on every ad. Our guide to RERA advertisement rules explains what must appear.
  • Location: a Google Maps pin, travel time to the nearest highway, metro, school and hospital, and the infrastructure that is actually approved or under construction (not just rumoured).
  • Amenities that exist today: boundary wall, gate, roads, electricity poles, water line, drainage. Mark clearly what is planned and when.
  • Price band and payment plan: even a range helps filter out people who can't afford the plot.

On RERA: the RERA Act exempts very small projects (land up to 500 square metres, or up to eight units), but most plotted colonies need registration before they are advertised. Check with your state RERA authority, and see our guide on how buyers check a RERA number.

Photos, drone video and site walk-throughs

Empty land is hard to photograph, so show what makes the site real:

  • Drone video of the whole layout with plot boundaries marked, the approach road and nearby landmarks. It answers "where exactly is it?" in seconds.
  • Ground-level walk-through on a phone: entering the gate, driving the internal roads, standing on a sample plot.
  • Progress updates every few weeks: roads laid, wall built, poles installed. They give you a reason to message old leads again.

Drones in India are governed by the Drone Rules, 2021. Before flying, check the zone on the Digital Sky airspace map: green zones need no permission for small drones, while yellow and red zones (for example near airports such as Jewar) need permission or are off limits. Hire a pilot with a remote pilot certificate if you can.

Where to advertise plots online

  • Google Search catches people already searching, such as "plots near [expressway]" or "residential plots in [area]". Use exact locality names. See Google Ads for real estate.
  • Meta (Facebook and Instagram) reaches investors and families who aren't searching yet. Lead with location and approvals rather than "best investment" claims. See Meta ads for real estate.
  • Property portals such as 99acres and MagicBricks have a large plot section. Compare them in our portal comparison.
  • YouTube suits drone and walk-through videos, since plot buyers often watch long videos before they visit.
  • Your own landing page for the project, so every ad sends people to one place with the layout, approvals and WhatsApp button. See our landing page conversion guide.

Avoid promising returns ("double your money in 3 years"). It attracts the wrong buyers, and advertising assured returns can break RERA and consumer protection rules.

Follow up the same hour

A plot enquiry goes cold quickly, because the buyer is usually comparing several colonies at once. Reply within minutes on WhatsApp with:

  1. A short thank-you and the project name.
  2. The layout plan PDF with available plots marked.
  3. The location pin and the drone video link.
  4. Two questions: the plot size they want, and whether it is for building a home or for investment.
  5. Two site-visit slots, for example Saturday 11 am or Sunday 4 pm.

Then follow up on day 2 and day 5 with something new (a progress update, a plot that just sold), not just "any update?". Our WhatsApp message templates and WhatsApp automation guide show how to send this automatically.

Make the site visit easy

Most plot sales close on site. Share a pin that opens directly in Google Maps, send a reminder the evening before, and have someone meet the buyer at the gate. Carry printed copies of the approvals and the layout plan, and mark the plots they asked about with flags or pegs. Our guide on how to book more site visits covers reminders and no-shows.

Checklist before you start

  • Layout plan with plot numbers, sizes, facing and road widths
  • RERA number and approvals ready to share, and on every ad
  • Drone video, walk-through video and 10–15 real photos
  • One landing page with a WhatsApp button and an enquiry form
  • A WhatsApp reply ready with the layout PDF, location pin and visit slots
  • A sheet or CRM that records each lead's source, plot interest and visit date

To sell plots online, put every plot on a map with its exact size, facing, road width and price band, show the approvals and RERA number up front, add drone and walk-through videos, and answer every enquiry on WhatsApp the same hour with a location pin and a site-visit slot.

Plot buyers ask different questions from flat buyers: title, approvals, access roads and what will be built around them. An online plan that answers those questions before the call sells plots faster than ads alone.

This guide is for plot developers, colonisers and brokers in India. It covers who buys plots, what to show for every plot, RERA and land rules, photos and drone video, where to advertise, how to follow up, and how to make the site visit easy.

Who buys plots, and what do they worry about?

Plot buyers usually fall into three groups, and each needs a slightly different message:

  • End users who want to build their own house later. They care about the neighbourhood, schools, water, electricity and how soon others will build around them.
  • Investors who expect the land to appreciate. They care about approved infrastructure (expressways, airports, metro lines), the rate per square yard and how easy it will be to resell.
  • NRIs and buyers from other cities who can't visit easily. They need video, documents and a trusted person on the ground. Our guide on NRI real estate marketing covers this group.

All three share the same fears: unclear title, missing approvals, encroachment, and a plot that looks different in person from the brochure. Your online material should remove those doubts one by one.

What should a plot listing show?

A plot listing should show the facts a buyer would otherwise ask for on the phone. A listing with only a price and a photo of empty land gets few serious enquiries.

Show only infrastructure that is approved or under construction, with a link to the official source, not rumours. "Near the upcoming expressway" without a source is the kind of claim buyers stop trusting.

Do plotted projects need RERA registration?

Most plotted projects do. Section 3 of the RERA Act, 2016 requires a promoter to register a real estate project, including plotted development, before advertising or selling it. The Act exempts only small projects where the land proposed to be developed does not exceed 500 square metres or the number of apartments does not exceed eight, and states can lower that threshold. Section 11 requires the registration number and the authority's website on every advertisement. Check with your state RERA authority, and see our guides on RERA advertisement rules and how buyers check a RERA number.

Two more rules shape who you can sell to. NRIs cannot buy agricultural land, plantation property or farm houses under the RBI's FEMA Master Direction, so don't target NRIs with farmland or farmhouse plots. And avoid promising returns ("double your money"): advertising assured returns attracts the wrong buyers and can break RERA and consumer protection rules.

Photos, drone video and site walk-throughs

Empty land is hard to photograph, so show what makes the site real:

  • Drone video of the whole layout with plot boundaries marked, the approach road and nearby landmarks. It answers "where exactly is it?" in seconds.
  • Ground-level walk-through on a phone: entering the gate, driving the internal roads, standing on a sample plot.
  • Progress updates every few weeks: roads laid, wall built, poles installed. They give you a reason to message old leads again.
  • Real photos of the gate, roads and corners, taken in good light, with the date.

Drones in India are governed by the Drone Rules, 2021. According to the Ministry of Civil Aviation's announcement, no permission is needed to fly in green zones, while yellow zones (now within 12 km of an operational airport's perimeter) and red zones need permission or are off limits. Check the zone on the Digital Sky airspace map before every flight; sites near airports, such as the Jewar airport area, need extra care. A commercial shoot needs a registered drone and a certified pilot. Our real estate video marketing guide covers shot lists and how to reuse footage in ads, Reels and WhatsApp.

Where to advertise plots online

Advertise plots where buyers are already searching, and where investors scroll:

  • Google Search catches people searching "plots near [expressway]" or "residential plots in [area]". Use exact locality names. See Google Ads for real estate.

How to follow up a plot enquiry

Follow up a plot enquiry within minutes on WhatsApp, because the buyer is usually comparing several colonies at once. Send:

  1. A short thank-you and the project name.
  2. The layout plan PDF with available plots marked.
  3. The location pin and the drone video link.
  4. Two questions: the plot size they want, and whether it is for building a home or for investment.
  5. Two site-visit slots, for example Saturday morning or Sunday afternoon.

Then follow up a couple of days later and again later in the week with something new (a progress update, a plot that just sold), not just "any update?". Our WhatsApp message templates and WhatsApp automation guide show how to send this automatically.

Make the site visit easy

Most plot sales close on site. Share a pin that opens directly in Google Maps, send a reminder the evening before, and have someone meet the buyer at the gate. Carry printed copies of the approvals and the layout plan, and mark the plots they asked about with flags or pegs so they can stand on them. Our guide on how to book more site visits covers reminders and no-shows.

Common mistakes when selling plots online

  • A layout that isn't updated, so buyers ask for plots that sold weeks ago.
  • Hiding approvals until the site visit.
  • Rumoured infrastructure presented as fact.
  • Return promises in ads or WhatsApp messages.
  • Slow replies, which hand the buyer to the next colony.

Checklist before you start

  • Layout plan with plot numbers, sizes, facing and road widths
  • RERA number and approvals ready to share, and on every ad
  • Drone video, walk-through video and real, dated photos
  • One landing page with a WhatsApp button and an enquiry form
  • A WhatsApp reply ready with the layout PDF, location pin and visit slots
  • A sheet or CRM that records each lead's source, plot interest and visit date

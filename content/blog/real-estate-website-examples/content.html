The best real estate websites in India load fast on a phone, show the project's location, price band, RERA number and floor plans without making the buyer dig, and make it one tap to start a WhatsApp chat or book a site visit. Design matters less than most builders think: a plain page that answers a buyer's questions in the first scroll beats a beautiful one hidden behind animations.

This guide is for developers, brokers and channel partners planning a new site or fixing an old one. It covers the types of real estate websites, what public examples from large Indian developers show, a features table for each type, the sections every project page needs, mobile speed, search and AI visibility, tracking, and the most common mistakes.

What types of real estate websites are there?

There are four main types: a developer brand site, a project microsite or landing page, a broker or channel partner site, and portal listings. Most businesses need one or two of them.

  • Developer brand site: who the builder is, completed and ongoing projects, approvals and contact details. It builds trust when a buyer searches the builder's name.
  • Project microsite or landing page: one project, one page, built to turn ad clicks into enquiries. This is where most paid leads arrive. See our landing pages service.
  • Broker or channel partner site: the projects they sell, the localities they know and how to reach them. Our channel partner page explains what works for CPs.
  • Portal listing: not your own site, but often the first place buyers see you. Compare the big two in 99acres vs MagicBricks.

What do good real estate websites have in common?

They put the key facts first, show real progress, treat location as a feature, give floor plans with carpet area, repeat one clear call to action and stay fast on mobile.

  • Key facts first: project name, locality, configuration, starting price or band, possession date and RERA number, visible without scrolling far.
  • Real photos next to renders: dated construction photos show what exists today.
  • Location as a feature: a map with honest travel times to metro, schools and hospitals, not "prime location".
  • Floor plans with sizes: carpet area per unit, since apartments are sold on carpet area under RERA (see our real estate brochure guide).
  • One main action (enquire or book a site visit) repeated through the page, plus WhatsApp and phone.
  • Fast on mobile, because Google indexes the mobile version of sites (Google) and most ad clicks arrive on phones.

Many smaller sites miss these basics. In our own automated check of 749 real estate homepages in North India in October 2026, 60% had no WhatsApp link and 56% had no ad or analytics tracking; the full table is in our landing page best practices guide.

Do you need a full website or just a landing page?

If you are launching one project with ads, a fast project landing page is enough to start; add a brand site once you have several projects or buyers start searching your company name. A broker or CP usually needs a small site with locality pages and the projects they sell, plus a strong Google Business Profile.

  • One project, running ads: a landing page per project and configuration.
  • Several projects: a brand site with one page per project, and separate landing pages for each campaign.
  • Broker or CP: a site with locality pages and listings; see Google Business Profile for real estate agents.

What sections does every project page need?

A project page should follow the order of the buyer's questions, from "where and how much" to "how do I visit".

  1. Hero: project name, locality, configuration, price band, RERA number and one button.
  2. Highlights: four to six facts a buyer cares about (towers, open area, possession date, approvals).
  3. Floor plans and prices: carpet area per unit; offer the price sheet on WhatsApp if you don't publish it.
  4. Location: map pin and travel times.
  5. Amenities: photos where they exist, renders labelled as renders.
  6. Construction status: latest photos and the month taken.
  7. About the developer: past projects and completion record.
  8. FAQs: payment plan, approved bank loans, possession, maintenance.
  9. Enquiry form: name, phone and at most two quick choices, with WhatsApp.

Every page advertising a registered project should show its RERA number and the state RERA website. Check a number with our RERA number check guide.

How fast should a real estate website be on mobile?

Aim for Google's "good" Core Web Vitals: main content within 2.5 seconds, responses to taps within 200 milliseconds and almost no layout jumping, measured on real mobile visits (web.dev).

  • Compress images and serve WebP or AVIF.
  • Avoid autoplay video and heavy sliders at the top.
  • Keep the WhatsApp button fixed where a thumb can reach it.
  • Test with PageSpeed Insights and on a mid-range Android phone over mobile data.
  • Make buttons and text readable for everyone; the WCAG guidelines cover contrast and tap targets.

How do real estate websites get found on Google and AI search?

Give each project and locality its own page with the facts in plain text, add structured data, and keep business details consistent everywhere. Google uses structured data to understand page content (Google); schema.org even has a RealEstateListing type for listing pages. Facts locked inside PDF brochures or images are hard for search engines and AI assistants to read. More in real estate SEO in India and generative engine optimization.

How do you track leads from a real estate website?

Install Google Analytics 4 and your ad platform tags (after cookie consent where you use it), mark form submissions, WhatsApp clicks and calls as conversions, and save the UTM source with every lead. Then you can judge campaigns on cost per site visit instead of cost per click. UTM tagging is explained in Google Analytics Help, and our guide on real estate lead management shows how to keep it all in one sheet or CRM.

What are the most common real estate website mistakes?

  • Hiding the price completely. Even a range filters out buyers who can't afford it.
  • Long forms asking for budget, timeline, city and email before first contact.
  • Sending every ad to the homepage instead of the matching project page.
  • No RERA number on registered projects.
  • Stock photos of foreign skylines instead of the real site and locality.
  • Brochure-only details in a PDF nobody downloads on mobile.
  • No reply after the form. The best site is wasted if nobody answers within the hour. See WhatsApp automation for real estate leads.

The best real estate websites in India do three things well: they load fast on a phone, they show the project's location, price band, RERA number and floor plans without making the buyer dig, and they make it one tap to start a WhatsApp chat or book a site visit. Design matters less than most builders think. A plain page that answers a buyer's questions in the first scroll gets more enquiries than a beautiful one that hides them behind animations.

This guide walks through the types of real estate websites, what good examples have in common, the sections every project page needs, and the mistakes we see most often.

Types of real estate websites

Most real estate businesses need one of these, and many need two:

  • Developer brand site: who the builder is, completed and ongoing projects, approvals, and contact details. It builds trust when a buyer searches the builder's name.
  • Project microsite or landing page: one project, one page, built to turn ad clicks into enquiries. This is where most leads come from. See our landing pages service.
  • Broker or channel partner site: the projects they are mandated on, their areas of expertise, and how to reach them. Our channel partner page explains what works for CPs.
  • Portal listing: not your own site, but often the first place buyers see you. Compare the big two in our 99acres vs MagicBricks guide.

What good examples have in common

Look at the sites of large, established developers and you'll notice the same patterns:

  • The key facts come first. Project name, locality, configuration (2/3 BHK, plots), starting price or price band, possession date and RERA number are visible without scrolling far.
  • Real photos and honest renders. Construction progress photos sit next to renders, so buyers can see what exists today.
  • Location is treated as a feature. A map, travel times and nearby schools, hospitals and metro stations, not just "prime location".
  • Floor plans with sizes. Carpet area is shown clearly, as RERA requires it to be the basis of sale.
  • Clear calls to action. One main action (enquire or book a site visit) repeated through the page, plus WhatsApp and phone.
  • Fast on mobile. Most property browsing in India happens on phones, often on patchy connections, so heavy videos and sliders cost enquiries.

The sections every project page needs

  1. Hero: project name, locality, configuration, price band, RERA number and one button.
  2. Highlights: 4–6 short facts a buyer cares about (tower count, open area, possession date, approvals).
  3. Floor plans and prices: carpet area per unit type; let people request the price sheet on WhatsApp if you don't publish it.
  4. Location: map pin and travel times.
  5. Amenities: with photos where they exist.
  6. Construction status: latest photos and the month they were taken.
  7. About the developer: past projects and completion record.
  8. FAQs: payment plan, bank loans approved, possession, maintenance.
  9. Enquiry form: name, phone and one question at most, with a WhatsApp option.

Every page that advertises a registered project should show its RERA number and the state RERA website. Our guide to RERA advertisement rules lists what must appear.

Mobile and speed

A buyer who taps an Instagram ad expects the page in a couple of seconds. Google's Core Web Vitals give simple targets: the main content should appear within 2.5 seconds (Largest Contentful Paint), and the layout shouldn't jump while loading. To get there:

  • Compress images and serve them in modern formats (WebP or AVIF).
  • Avoid autoplay videos and heavy sliders at the top of the page.
  • Keep the form short and the WhatsApp button fixed where a thumb can reach it.
  • Test on a mid-range Android phone on mobile data, not just on your office Wi-Fi.

Tracking: know which ad brought the lead

A website that doesn't track leads can't tell you which ads work. At minimum, install Google Analytics 4 and the Meta Pixel (after cookie consent), mark form submissions and WhatsApp clicks as conversions, and save the ad source (UTM tags) with every lead. Then you can judge campaigns on cost per site visit instead of cost per click. Our guide on real estate lead management shows how to keep this in one sheet or CRM.

Common mistakes

  • Hiding the price completely. Even a range filters out buyers who can't afford it and saves your sales team time.
  • Long forms. Asking for budget, timeline, city and email before the first contact loses most visitors.
  • One page for every project. Ads for a specific project should land on that project's page, not on the homepage.
  • No RERA number. It is required for registered projects and buyers look for it.
  • Stock photos of foreign skylines. Buyers want to see the actual site and locality.

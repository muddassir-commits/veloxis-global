A real estate brochure should include the project name and RERA number, the location with distances you can prove, floor plans with carpet area, specifications, amenities, construction status and possession date, the payment plan (or price on request) and one clear way to book a site visit. Keep the PDF readable on a phone and light enough to send on WhatsApp.

A real estate brochure has one job: to move a buyer from “this looks interesting” to “let’s visit”. In India it is also the PDF your team sends on WhatsApp after an enquiry, the file a channel partner forwards, and the document a family reads together on a phone. This guide covers what to include, the order buyers read in, design tips, floor plans and area terms, print versus PDF, how to compress a brochure PDF for WhatsApp, Hindi and Gujarati brochures with a terminology table, and the mistakes that cost site visits.

What to include in a real estate brochure

Buyers open a brochure with a short list of questions. If the brochure answers them in the order they are asked, it works. These are the sections almost every project brochure needs:

SectionWhat the buyer wants to knowWhat to put there
CoverWhat is this and where is it?Project name, one-line positioning, locality, the RERA registration number and authority website
The project in one pageIs it for me?Configurations (2 BHK, 3 BHK, plots), land area, number of towers or units, possession timeline as declared to RERA
LocationHow far is it from what matters to us?A clear map with real travel distances to schools, hospitals, offices, metro and highway, not just arrows
Floor plansWill our furniture and family fit?Unit plans with carpet area, balcony and room dimensions, and the tower or site plan
AmenitiesWhat will daily life be like?The amenities that actually exist in the approved plan, with the clubhouse or landscape plan
SpecificationsWhat will we get inside the flat?Flooring, fittings, doors and windows, electrical, structure, in plain words
DeveloperCan we trust them?Company background, completed projects, and where to verify them
Next stepWhat do we do now?WhatsApp number, site office address, visit timings, a QR code to book a visit
DisclaimerWhat is binding?A clear statement that images are artistic impressions, and that the agreement for sale is the binding document

Pricing is often kept out of the main brochure and sent as a separate price sheet or cost sheet, because prices change more often than the brochure is reprinted. If you do that, make sure the price sheet carries the same project name and RERA number, and send it together with the brochure. Buyers who have to ask twice for the price often don’t.

The RERA line isn’t optional. Section 11(2) of the RERA Act, 2016 requires every advertisement or prospectus to carry the registration number and the authority’s website. Our guide to the RERA number check explains the rule and where else the number must appear.

How to structure it: the order buyers read in

Most brochures are ordered the way the developer thinks about the project: vision, architect, amenities, and finally, on page 14, the floor plans. Buyers read the other way round. They want to know whether it fits their budget, their family and their commute, and only then whether the lobby is beautiful.

A structure that works well for most residential projects:

  1. Cover with the name, the locality and a single promise.
  2. At a glance: configurations, sizes, possession, and three reasons to consider the project.
  3. Location with distances and the map.
  4. Floor plans, one unit type per page, with carpet area.
  5. Life at the project: amenities and open spaces.
  6. Specifications.
  7. About the developer.
  8. Visit us: a clear call to action.

If your brochure is long, add a one-page summary at the front. Many buyers will only ever read that page, and your sales team can send it as an image on WhatsApp when a full PDF is too much.

Real estate brochure design tips

Good brochure design is mostly about restraint. The best project brochures look calm, and every page has one message. A few principles:

  • One idea per spread. Location, a floor plan, amenities. Pages that try to say everything say nothing.
  • Real photos where you have them. Sample flat photos, the actual site, the neighbourhood. Renders are fine for what isn’t built yet, but label them as artistic impressions.
  • Readable type on a phone. Most people will read the PDF on a phone screen. Body text that looks elegant in print can be unreadable at phone size. Test it by opening the PDF on a mid-range phone before you sign it off.
  • Consistent brand colours and fonts across the brochure, the landing page, the ads and the hoardings, so a buyer recognises the project wherever they meet it. Our guide to real estate hoarding design covers the outdoor side.
  • Numbers big, adjectives small. “12 minutes to the metro station” is more persuasive than “excellent connectivity”.
  • Plain language. Write specifications the way a buyer would say them, not the way a contractor writes them.

Floor plans and areas: where trust is won or lost

Floor plans are the most-read pages of any brochure, and the pages where buyers most often feel misled. Get these right:

  • Lead with carpet area. The RERA Act defines carpet area in Section 2(k), apartments are sold on carpet area under RERA, and buyers increasingly compare projects on it. If you also mention built-up or super area, label each figure clearly.
  • Show room dimensions, not only the total area. Families want to know whether a bed and a wardrobe fit in the second bedroom.
  • Match the approved plans. The plans in the brochure should match the ones uploaded to the project’s RERA page. Buyers and CPs do check.
  • Mark furniture as indicative. Furniture drawn into a plan helps buyers imagine the space, but say that it is indicative and not included.
  • Include the key plan. Show where the unit sits in the tower and which way it faces. For many Indian buyers, orientation and Vastu matter as much as size.

Print brochure or digital brochure?

You need both, but they aren’t the same file.

Export the print file and the digital file separately, and make the digital one clickable: links to the Google Maps location, the project landing page and a WhatsApp chat. Its floor plan and amenity pages also make good carousels for real estate social media posts, as long as the facts match.

How do you compress a real estate brochure PDF for WhatsApp?

To compress a brochure PDF, export a separate screen version with images downsampled to screen resolution, fonts embedded and print marks removed, then check that it opens quickly on a mid-range phone over mobile data. Size limits are real: for automated sending through the WhatsApp Business Platform, Meta’s supported media documentation sets a maximum of 100 MB for a PDF document. A file well under that limit is still better, because buyers download it on mobile data.

  1. Export from the design file, not the print PDF. In InDesign, Canva or similar tools, choose a screen or “smallest file size” preset instead of “press quality”.
  2. Downsample images. Phone screens don’t need print resolution; replace full-bleed print photos with compressed JPGs.
  3. Flatten heavy effects such as transparencies and large vector maps that bloat the file.
  4. Run a PDF optimiser (Acrobat’s Reduce File Size or a trusted desktop tool). Avoid uploading unreleased plans and prices to unknown free websites.
  5. Check readability after compression: floor plan dimensions and the RERA number must stay sharp.
  6. Name the file clearly, for example “ProjectName-Brochure-v3-Nov2026.pdf”, so buyers can find it later in their downloads.

Keep three versions: the print file, a full screen PDF for email and download, and a short “WhatsApp edition” or one-page summary image for the first reply.

Sending the brochure: speed beats design

The most beautiful brochure loses to an average one that arrives first. Buyers often enquire with several projects at once, so make the brochure part of your lead flow, not a file someone remembers to send the next morning:

  • Instant delivery on WhatsApp when someone enquires from an ad or the landing page, day or night. See WhatsApp automation for real estate leads.
  • Ask one question with it: “Are you looking at 2 BHK or 3 BHK?” A reply starts the conversation.
  • Follow up a day later: ask whether they looked at the floor plans, and offer a visit slot.
  • Gated or open download? A common middle path is an open one-page summary and the full brochure after a short form.

Track brochure downloads as a conversion on your landing page, so you know which ads bring people who actually read.

Can ChatGPT or an online tool design a brochure?

AI tools and templates help with parts of the job: drafting section copy, simplifying specifications, suggesting headlines, a first Hindi or Gujarati draft for review, and layout ideas in tools like Canva. They are not safe for floor plans, areas, distances, possession dates, amenities or legal text, which must come from approved documents and be checked by a person, because a wrong number in a brochure is a promise. Gujarati and Hindi real estate brochures: samples and terminology

A Gujarati or Hindi brochure translates the story, benefits and location sections fully, and keeps standard property terms in the form buyers and sales teams actually use, written in the local script. In Gujarat, Maharashtra and the Hindi belt, a regional or two-language brochure can keep a family reading longer, especially parents who will help decide.

A typical two-language layout puts English and Gujarati (or Hindi) side by side on each page, or keeps the main brochure in one language with a one-page summary in the other. Either way, the floor plans, numbers and RERA details stay identical. Three rules keep a translated brochure safe. First, every number must match the English version exactly: areas, prices, the RERA number and the possession date. Second, the RERA registration number and the state authority’s website appear in both languages; for Gujarat that is GujRERA. Third, a native speaker proofreads the final file, because a machine translation of a legal line can change its meaning. Keep the English version too: channel partners and NRI buyers often forward it.

Common brochure mistakes that cost site visits

  • No RERA number, or the wrong one. It undermines trust immediately and breaks the advertising rules.
  • Only super area. Buyers who later find the carpet area feel misled.
  • Amenities that aren’t in the approved plan. These become disputes at possession.
  • A map with no distances, or “5 minutes from the airport” claims nobody can match.
  • No clear next step: no WhatsApp number, visit timings or QR code on the last page.
  • Different details from the ads and landing page. Price bands, possession dates and offers must match everywhere, or buyers assume the worst.
  • A file too heavy for WhatsApp. If it doesn’t open, it doesn’t exist.

Brochure checklist before you print or send

  1. RERA registration number and authority website on the cover and with the disclaimer.
  2. Carpet area on every floor plan, other area figures clearly labelled.
  3. Plans, amenities and possession date match the RERA page and approvals.
  4. Location map shows real travel distances.
  5. Renders labelled as artistic impressions, furniture as indicative.
  6. WhatsApp number, site address, visit timings and a QR code on the last page.
  7. Digital version opens quickly on a phone over mobile data.
  8. Details match the landing page, ads, price sheet and CP material.
  9. Version number and date in the footer of the digital file.

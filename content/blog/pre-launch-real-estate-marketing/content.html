Pre-launch real estate marketing is the early-access campaign a developer runs before a project's public price launch, usually collecting Expressions of Interest (EOIs) from first buyers. Under the RERA Act, 2016, a project that must be registered can't be advertised, marketed or booked until it is registered. So a lawful pre-launch runs from the day the RERA number arrives to the public launch, and everything before registration is preparation, not promotion.

This guide is for developers, marketing teams and channel partners planning a launch, and for buyers weighing a pre-launch offer. It covers what pre-launch means after RERA, what you can and can't do before registration, a step-by-step campaign plan with channels and messages, how to work with CPs, what to measure, and a buyer checklist.

What is pre-launch in real estate?

Pre-launch in real estate is the window before a project's public launch when the developer offers early buyers priority, often through an EOI, before full prices and inventory are released. Before RERA, the term usually meant selling flats in a project that wasn't approved yet, at a lower price, to raise early money. Buyers carried the risk that approvals, plans or timelines would change.

Section 3 of the RERA Act ended that model for projects that must be registered. Today the honest version of "pre-launch" is an early-access phase for a registered project, ahead of the public price release.

Is pre-launch marketing legal under RERA?

Pre-launch marketing is legal only after the project is registered. Section 3 bars a promoter from advertising, marketing, booking, selling, offering for sale or inviting people to buy any unit in a project that needs registration until it is registered. Section 59 sets a penalty of up to 10% of the estimated project cost for breaking this, with imprisonment of up to three years possible if the promoter keeps defying orders.

Regulators read "advertising" widely. In 2026 Bihar RERA treated "Coming Soon" posters for an unregistered project as advertising and imposed costs of ₹1 lakh on the developer (LiveLaw). Teasers, site banners and "register your interest" ads for an unregistered project carry the same risk.

After registration, Section 13 still applies: a promoter can't take more than 10% of a unit's cost as an advance or application fee without first signing and registering a written agreement for sale.

What can a developer do before RERA registration?

Before registration, a developer can prepare everything but publish nothing. The line is simple: if the public can see it or pay through it, wait for the number.

How does a pre-launch EOI phase work after registration?

After registration, a short EOI phase lets interested buyers reserve priority before public prices are released:

  1. Registration arrives. Add the RERA number, authority website and QR code where required to every creative. UP RERA, for example, requires its project QR code on brochures and ads (The Week).
  2. EOI window opens. Buyers fill an EOI form with their preferred configuration and pay the EOI amount, within the Section 13 limit. Our guide to EOI in real estate explains how it works.
  3. Prices and allotment. EOI holders choose units in the order set in the EOI terms (first come or by draw).
  4. Booking or refund. Buyers who choose a unit move to booking and the agreement for sale; others get refunds on the written terms.

How to plan a pre-launch campaign, step by step

A pre-launch campaign works best when everything is built before registration and switched on the same day the number arrives:

WhenWhat happens
Several weeks before registrationCreative, landing page, WhatsApp flow, CRM and call scripts built; campaigns built and paused; sales team trained
Registration dayRERA details added everywhere; page published; ads on; CPs signed and briefed
EOI windowAds drive enquiries; every enquiry gets a reply within minutes; site office open for visits
Launch dayAllotment for EOI holders; public prices released; ads move from "register interest" to "book a site visit"
After launchRetargeting of people who enquired but didn't book; construction updates

The weekly rhythm inside the EOI window: review enquiries, EOIs and site visits by source every Monday, move budget to what produces visits, and refresh creative that has stopped working.

Which channels work for pre-launch marketing?

The best pre-launch channels are the ones that reach people already looking in that location and price band, and that let you reply instantly:

  • Meta ads (Facebook and Instagram): awareness and lead forms in the project's catchment. Our Meta ads guide covers targeting and forms.
  • Google search ads: people searching the locality, the builder's name or the configuration. See Google Ads for real estate.
  • A project landing page: location, configurations, EOI terms, RERA details and a short form. Our landing page guide lists what to include.
  • WhatsApp: an instant reply with the brochure, price band and two qualifying questions (see WhatsApp automation for real estate leads).
  • Channel partners and past buyers: briefed with the same facts, so every message is consistent.
  • Hoardings and print near the site and in the catchment, carrying the RERA details.

What to say in pre-launch ads (and what not to)

Good pre-launch messages are specific and true:

  • The location and what's near it, with real distances.
  • The configurations and the price band once it's decided, with any asterisk explained.
  • How the EOI works: amount, what it gives you, and when it's refundable.
  • The developer's completed projects, with possession dates you can prove.

Avoid "guaranteed appreciation", "lowest price ever", invented scarcity and any amenity not in the approved plans. Section 12 of the RERA Act makes the promoter liable to compensate buyers who lose money because of false statements in an advertisement, and the ASCI code requires claims to be truthful. How should channel partners be used in a pre-launch?

Brief channel partners before the EOI window opens, with one fact sheet: project facts, EOI terms, commission terms and how to register a buyer so the booking is credited to them. Insist that every CP is RERA-registered and that their ads carry the project's registration details. CPs must not start marketing before the project is registered either. See channel partner in real estate and broker commission for how CP agreements usually work.

What should you measure in a pre-launch?

Measure the steps between an enquiry and a booking, by source (each ad campaign, each CP, walk-ins, portals):

  • Time to first reply, and enquiries with no contact after 24 hours.
  • Enquiries to qualified leads to site visits to EOIs to bookings.
  • Cost per site visit and cost per EOI for each campaign.
  • EOI refunds and their reasons.

What should buyers check before paying for a pre-launch offer?

  • Does the project have a RERA registration number? Look it up on the state RERA website (how to check a RERA number). If there's no number, the project shouldn't be taking money.
  • Are the EOI terms written down: amount, refund conditions, refund timeline and deductions?
  • Is payment going to the developer's account, by cheque or bank transfer, with a receipt?
  • Is the advance within 10% of the unit cost until an agreement for sale is signed and registered?
  • Does the possession date in the ads match the RERA filing?
  • Is the price compared with similar registered projects nearby? There's no rule that pre-launch prices are lower, and no guarantee of appreciation.

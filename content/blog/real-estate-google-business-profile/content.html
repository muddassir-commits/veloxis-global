A real estate agent or builder in India should create one Google Business Profile per staffed office, use the real business name with no project names or localities, choose "Real estate agency" or "Real estate developer" as the primary category, and show the RERA number in the description and project posts. Sample flats can't have their own.

This guide is for brokers, channel partners, agency owners and developers in India. It covers the questions a general checklist skips: whether each project can have a listing, whether every agent in a firm needs a profile, which categories fit, how RERA numbers fit into posts, and which review requests Google treats as fake. Which Google Business Profile setup fits an agent, broker firm or builder?

The right setup depends on where buyers actually meet you: at a staffed office, at a staffed site sales office, or at their own location. Google's eligibility guidelines say a business must make in-person contact with customers during its stated hours, and that there should be one profile per business location.

If you visit buyers at their homes and also receive them at an office, Google calls you a hybrid business: you can show the office address and add service areas, as its service area help page explains.

Can a builder create a separate profile for each project?

Only if the project has a staffed sales office that receives buyers during its listed hours. Google's eligibility rules list "rental or for-sale properties such as vacation homes, model homes, or vacant apartments" as ineligible, and then say that "sales or leasing offices, however, are eligible for verification."

In practice, for an Indian developer that means:

  • A sample flat on its own is a model home. It can't hold a profile, even if it is beautifully furnished.
  • A site office with a sales team present during its listed hours is a sales office. It can have its own profile at that address.
  • A project that is only a plot of land or a tower under construction, with no one there to meet buyers, isn't eligible. Promote it with posts and photos from your main profile instead.
  • Don't open a second profile for the same office to target another project or locality. Google's representation guidelines say not to create more than one page for each location of your business.

When a project is handed over, the sales office usually closes. Mark that profile as permanently closed rather than leaving old hours and a phone number nobody answers.

Should each agent in a brokerage have their own profile?

Usually not; a single agent who works under a branded firm should share the firm's profile. Google's guidelines name real estate agents as individual practitioners: public-facing professionals, typically with their own customer base. They also say that when a single practitioner works in a branded organisation, it is best for the practitioner to share the organisation's profile.

Where a firm has several well-known senior agents at one office, Google allows the office to have its own profile, separate from each practitioner's. Even then, one agent should not hold several profiles for different specialisations, such as one for plots and one for rentals. For most small Indian brokerages, one strong firm profile beats five thin ones that split the reviews.

How do you set up a real estate Google Business Profile, step by step?

Create the profile from the business's own Google account, then work through the steps below in order. Google's ownership rules say only business owners or authorised representatives may verify and manage a profile, so never let a portal, freelancer or channel partner own it.

  1. Business name: exactly as on your signboard, RERA registration and letterhead.
  2. Primary category: the one that completes "This business is a…" (see the next section).
  3. Address or service area: show the address only if buyers visit it. A service-area profile can list up to 20 areas, and Google says the overall area should be no more than about 2 hours of driving time from your base (Google).
  4. Phone: a number under the business's direct control that reaches this office, as Google's guidelines require. Not a portal's call-tracking number.
  5. Website: the page for that office or city, not a property portal listing.
  6. Hours: the hours someone is actually there to meet buyers, including site-visit days.
  7. Description: who you serve, which localities, what you handle (new launches, resale, plots, rentals, NRI buyers), and your RERA agent or promoter registration number.
  8. Services: one line each for what you really do.
  9. Verify, then add managers by email instead of sharing the password.

What name and category should a real estate business use?

Use your registered business name only, and the most specific category that describes the whole business. Google's guidelines say your name should reflect your real-world name, and that taglines, location details and other unnecessary information aren't permitted. They also ask you to use as few categories as possible.

How should real estate posts handle RERA numbers and phone numbers?

Treat every project post as an advertisement: show the project's RERA registration number and the RERA website, and use the Call button instead of typing a phone number. Section 11(2) of the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016 says a promoter's advertisement must prominently mention the Authority's website and the project's registration number. A Google post promoting a project is still promotion, so keep the same habit there.

  • Project posts: project name, RERA registration number, state RERA website, one real photo, and a link to the project page.
  • Phone numbers: Google's photos, videos and posts content policy says posts with unverified contact details, including phone numbers, may be removed. Add a "Call now" button instead; it uses your verified number.
  • Claims: no "guaranteed returns" or possession dates you can't back with the RERA record. Our guide to RERA advertisement rules lists what every ad must show, and how to check a RERA number helps buyers verify it.
  • Unregistered projects: don't post them at all until registration is in place.

General information, not legal advice. Rules were checked against the RERA Act and Google's help pages in November 2026; your state RERA may add its own advertising rules.

What photos should a broker or builder upload?

Upload real photos that help a buyer find you and trust you: the office entrance and signboard, the reception, the team, the sample flat, and genuine site progress. Avoid brochure renders presented as finished buildings, and stock photos of homes you don't sell.

  • Outside: the building and signboard from the road, so first-time visitors recognise it.
  • Inside: reception and meeting area.
  • Projects: dated construction progress, the sample flat, the approach road and nearby landmarks. Add these from the profile of the office that sells the project.
  • People: your own team only, with their consent.

How do real estate businesses get Google reviews without breaking the rules?

Ask every buyer, seller and tenant you have served, with your review link or QR code, and offer nothing in return. Google's tips to get more reviews say offering incentives, like free or discounted goods or services, in exchange for reviews is prohibited.

Real estate has some traps of its own. Google's Maps contribution policy bans:

  • Reviews from people with a conflict of interest, including current or former employees and anyone in a contractual relationship with you. That covers your channel partners, sub-brokers and contractors.
  • Selective asking: merchants may not "selectively solicit positive reviews". Ask every client, not only the happy ones.
  • Review quotas for staff and telling customers what to write.
  • Payment in kind, such as waiving part of the brokerage or a free modular kitchen for a five-star review.
  • Reviews on a competitor's listing meant to damage it.

The right moment to ask is after possession, registry or key handover. Reply to every review, and to negative ones politely, without sharing the buyer's unit number or payment details.

Can buyers message you from your Google profile?

Not through Google's own chat, which has ended; buyers can call you, or reach you on WhatsApp or text where Google offers that option. Google's page on changes to Business Profile chat says chat and call history stopped being available on 31 July 2024, and that eligible profiles can let customers contact them by text or WhatsApp.

Whichever channel you add, answer it fast. A buyer who taps your profile at night may be comparing several brokers. Our guide to WhatsApp automation for real estate leads shows how to send an instant first reply and route the lead to the right person.

Google has also been changing the questions-and-answers section on Maps. Don't rely on it to explain your fees or process. Put the answers buyers need, such as whether you charge brokerage on new launches, in your description, your posts and the FAQ on your website. A monthly routine for real estate profiles

  1. Post one project update or site-visit day, with the RERA number and a Call button.
  2. Add a few new real photos: site progress, the sample flat, a recent handover.
  3. Reply to every new review.
  4. Check for suggested edits to your name, category or hours, and reject wrong ones.
  5. Close profiles for sales offices that have shut.
  6. Check that the website link still opens the right page.

To book more real estate site visits from online enquiries: reply within minutes, ask two or three qualifying questions on WhatsApp, call with context, offer two specific visit times instead of asking "when can you come?", confirm with the location pin and host's number, remind twice, and follow up anyone who doesn't show. Most property decisions are made at the site, so every step after the enquiry should lead there.

This guide is for brokers, channel partners and developer sales teams. It covers what a site visit is, a step-by-step booking process, WhatsApp and call scripts, how to prepare and run the visit itself, a site visit form and report you can copy, how to reduce no-shows, follow-up after the visit, virtual visits for NRI buyers, and how to measure your visit rate.

What is a site visit in real estate?

A site visit is when a prospective buyer visits the project, usually the sample flat, site office or actual tower, with a salesperson or channel partner. For under-construction projects it is often the buyer's first real look at the location, construction quality and the people behind the project. Few buyers book a home they haven't seen, so for most builders and brokers the site visit is the most important step after the enquiry.

How to book a real estate site visit, step by step

  1. Reply within minutes. Send an instant WhatsApp message with the brochure or a short summary, the price band and a question. In a study published in Harvard Business Review, firms that contacted online leads within an hour were nearly seven times as likely to qualify them as firms that waited longer.
  2. Qualify in two or three questions. Which configuration? What budget range? Buying in the next three months or later? (See lead qualification questions.)
  3. Call with context. The salesperson calls knowing the answers and talks about the buyer's needs, not a speech about the project.
  4. Offer two specific times. "Saturday 11 am or Sunday 4 pm?" gets a decision more easily than an open question.
  5. Confirm in writing. Date, time, Google Maps pin, the name and number of the person meeting them, parking and what to bring.
  6. Remind. The evening before and on the morning of the visit, with an easy way to reschedule.
  7. Follow up after. Thank them the same day, answer what they asked, and agree the next step with a date.

Steps 1, 5 and 6 can be automated; steps 2 to 4 work best with a person. See WhatsApp automation for real estate leads.

What should you say to get a buyer to visit the site?

Refer to what the buyer told you, suggest the units that fit, explain why seeing them helps, and offer two times.

First WhatsApp reply:

Hi [Name], thanks for your interest in [Project], [locality]. Here's the brochure and price list. Quick question so I can share the right options: are you looking at 2 BHK or 3 BHK, and roughly what budget?

On the call:

"I saw you're looking at a 3 BHK under [budget]. We have two that fit, one on a higher floor with a park view. The best way to judge is to see the sample flat and the site. Would Saturday at 11 or Sunday at 4 work better for you?"

Hinglish version: "Aapne 3 BHK ke liye poocha tha. Do options aapke budget mein fit hote hain. Ek baar site dekh lijiye, size aur view ka sahi andaza wahin hota hai. Shanivaar 11 baje ya Ravivaar 4 baje, kya theek rahega?"

Confirmation:

Confirmed: Saturday 11 am at [Project] site office. Location: [Google Maps link]. [Salesperson name] will meet you, [phone]. Parking is available at the gate. Reply 1 to confirm or 2 to change the time.

How do you prepare for a site visit?

Prepare the visit around what this buyer told you, so the site shows them answers, not a standard tour:

Before the visitWhy
Re-read the call notes: configuration, budget, purpose, concernsYou show the two or three units that fit, not the whole inventory
Keep the cost sheet, payment plan and RERA details readyBuyers ask about total cost and possession on site
Check the sample flat and route: clean, lit, signpostedThe first five minutes set the tone
Know real distances and travel times to schools, offices and metroLocation is often the deciding question
Plan for family members, children and eldersDecision makers who feel looked after stay longer

During the visit, follow a simple order: welcome and recap what they told you, show the location and amenities, walk the sample flat or actual unit, sit down for the cost sheet and questions, then agree the next step before they leave.

Real estate site visit form (template)

Use a site visit form, on paper, a tablet or a Google Form, so every visit is recorded the same way:

SectionFields
VisitorName, mobile, email, current city or locality, number of people visiting
SourceHow they heard (Meta ad, Google, portal, CP name and RERA number, referral, hoarding, walk-in)
RequirementConfiguration, budget range, purpose, timeline, home loan needed
VisitDate, time, salesperson, units or sample flat shown
FeedbackWhat they liked, objections, other projects they're considering
ConsentPermission to contact them by phone and WhatsApp about this project
Next stepSecond visit, family visit, EOI, booking or closed (with reason), and the date

The source field matters most for marketing: it shows which ads and channel partners actually bring visitors. The consent field reflects India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, which expects personal data to be used for the purpose it was given for.

Site visit report example

A short report after each visit keeps the next conversation specific:

Visit: Sat 11 am. Rahul and Priya S., currently renting in Indirapuram. 3 BHK, budget up to ₹1.1 crore, buying within 3 months, home loan pre-approved. Saw Tower B sample flat and site. Liked: layout, park-facing units. Concern: possession date and distance to school. Also considering [competitor]. Next: send construction update video and school distances on Monday; invite parents for a second visit Sunday.

(Names and figures above are an illustration, not a real buyer.)

How do you reduce site visit no-shows?

  • Book only qualified leads. A visit booked with someone whose budget doesn't fit is likely to be skipped.
  • Get a specific time, not "this weekend".
  • Remind twice: the evening before and the morning of the visit, with the location pin.
  • Make rescheduling easy: "Reply 1 to confirm, 2 to change the time." A rescheduled visit beats a no-show.
  • Call no-shows the same day, without pressure, and offer the next two slots.
  • Help with travel where it fits: directions from the nearest metro station, or a pickup for families.

How should you follow up after a site visit?

Follow up the same day, and make every later message answer something the buyer raised at the site:

  1. Same evening: a thank-you with the cost sheet for the unit they liked.
  2. Next day: answer the main concern from the report (possession, school distance, loan).
  3. Within the week: invite the missing decision maker for a second visit.
  4. After that: useful updates only (construction progress, a new floor plan), until they book or say no.

If they say no, record the reason. It is the most useful data your marketing team will get.

Can a site visit be virtual?

Yes, for buyers who can't travel, such as NRIs or families in another city. A live video call from the site, walking the actual unit and the approach road, answers more than a recorded film. Book it like a physical visit, with a fixed time in their time zone, and follow with a written summary. For NRI buyers, see NRI real estate marketing. Many buyers still want someone they trust to see the site in person before booking, so treat the virtual visit as the step that earns that trip.

What is a good site visit conversion rate?

We haven't found a reliable public benchmark for India. Rates depend on lead source, price segment, location and how fast the team replies, so a figure quoted without its source tells you little. Measure your own instead:

  • Lead to visit booked: visits booked ÷ leads, by source.
  • Visit show rate: visits done ÷ visits booked.
  • Visit to booking: bookings ÷ visits done.
  • Cost per site visit: ad spend ÷ visits done, by campaign.

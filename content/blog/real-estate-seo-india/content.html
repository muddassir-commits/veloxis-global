Real estate SEO is how a builder, broker or channel partner gets found on Google without paying for every click. In India it is harder than it looks: property portals take most of the top results for broad searches such as “flats in Noida”, and buyers compare several projects before they ever fill a form. But the searches that matter most, a project's name, a builder's name, a locality plus a configuration, are winnable for a small business with the right pages. This guide explains what real estate SEO is, the four types of SEO, the keywords worth targeting, how to build project and locality pages that rank, what changes for agents and CPs, whether AI is replacing SEO, and how to measure it.

What is real estate SEO?

Real estate SEO (search engine optimisation) means setting up your website and online presence so that Google shows your pages when buyers search for the projects, localities and services you sell. Done well, it brings enquiries that cost nothing per click and keep coming for as long as the page ranks.

It is different from SEO for a shop or a software company in three ways:

  • Portals dominate broad searches. For “2 BHK in Lucknow”, the first page is usually property portals and aggregators. Competing with them head-on rarely pays.
  • Searches are very local. Buyers search by sector, corridor and project name, not by city. Each locality is its own small market.
  • Projects have a life cycle. Searches for a project rise around launch and possession and fade once it is sold out. Your pages need to follow that cycle.

So real estate SEO is less about one big keyword and more about owning many small, high-intent searches that portals don't serve well.

The four types of SEO, applied to real estate

SEO is usually split into four parts. All four matter for a property business, but not equally.

TypeWhat it coversFor a builder or broker, it means
On-page SEOThe content and structure of each pageOne page per project and locality, clear titles, price band, configurations, RERA number and FAQs written in plain text
Technical SEOHow easily Google can crawl, load and understand the siteFast mobile pages, no brochure locked inside a PDF, a sitemap, no broken links when a project page is retired
Local SEOShowing up for searches with a place in them, and on Google MapsA complete Google Business Profile, consistent name and phone number across directories, reviews
Off-page SEOSignals from other websites, mainly linksMentions and links from local news, directories, the developer's site (for CPs) and partner pages

For most small developers and brokers, on-page and local SEO bring the fastest results. Technical SEO is usually a one-time clean-up. Off-page SEO is slow, steady work.

Real estate keywords worth targeting

Pick keywords by how close the searcher is to booking a visit, not by how many people search for them.

  1. Project and builder names. Someone searching your project's name is already interested. If your own page doesn't rank first for it, a portal or another broker will take that buyer. This is the first thing to fix.
  2. Locality plus configuration. “3 BHK in Gomti Nagar Extension”, “plots on Sultanpur Road”, “ready to move flats in Sector 150”. Lower volume, high intent, and often winnable.
  3. Questions buyers ask before they buy. “What is EOI in real estate?”, “How to check a RERA number?”, “Is it safe to buy under-construction property?” Guides that answer these build trust and bring people into your site early.
  4. Agent and service searches. For brokers and CPs: “property dealer in Kalyanpur”, “real estate agent near me”. These are mostly won on Google Maps, through local SEO.

Avoid chasing broad searches such as “property in Noida” on a new site. The portals have years of authority there. Spend the same effort on ten locality and project pages instead.

Project and locality pages that rank and convert

The page that ranks for a project search should also be the page that turns the visitor into an enquiry. That is why SEO and the real estate landing page belong together. A good project page has:

  • The project name, locality and configuration in the title and main heading, for example “Project Name: 2 & 3 BHK Apartments on Shaheed Path, Lucknow”.
  • Real text, not only images. Google reads text. Price band, sizes, amenities, possession date and the RERA number should be written on the page, not hidden in a render or a PDF.
  • A short FAQ answering the questions your sales team hears every day: parking, loans, possession, maintenance.
  • Location detail: distances to landmarks, schools, metro stations and highways, and an embedded map.
  • A fast mobile load and a clear enquiry button and WhatsApp option on the first screen.

Locality pages work the same way: one page per area you sell in, describing the area honestly and listing the projects you have there. Our landing page conversion hacks cover the design side, and the project landing page blueprint shows a full layout.

One technical point that costs many builders their rankings: when a project sells out, don't delete the page. Keep it with a “sold out” note and links to your newer projects, or redirect it to the closest live page. A deleted page throws away everything it had earned.

Local SEO for builders, brokers and channel partners

For agents, CPs and small developers, the Google Business Profile is often the single biggest SEO win. It decides whether you appear in the map results for “real estate agent near me” and similar searches. Fill in every field, choose the right categories, add real photos, post updates and ask happy clients for reviews. Our local SEO guide for agents walks through each step.

Two more local signals matter:

  • Consistent business details. The same name, phone number and website on your profile, your site and every directory listing.
  • Local mentions and links. Directory listings, local news coverage of a launch, and, for CPs, a link from the developer's channel partner page.

Channel partners selling a developer's project should not copy the developer's project page word for word. Google tends to show only one version of duplicate content, and it will usually be the developer's. Write your own page with your own details, your RERA agent number and what you add for the buyer.

Is SEO being replaced by AI?

No, but it is changing. Google now shows AI-written summaries at the top of many searches, and buyers also ask ChatGPT and other assistants for advice. Those answers are built from web pages, and they often cite the pages they used. Clear, factual pages that answer a question directly are more likely to be quoted and linked.

In practice this rewards the same things good SEO already needed: real information in plain text, honest answers, FAQs, a consistent business identity across the web, and pages that are easy for machines to read. Simple definition searches may bring fewer clicks than before, but searches with intent to buy, such as a project name or a locality and budget, still send people to websites. How long SEO takes, and how to measure it

SEO is slow. A new page can take weeks to be indexed and months to settle into a stable position, and a new website takes longer than an established one. That is why most builders run ads for a launch and build SEO alongside it, so that organic enquiries take over a growing share of leads over time.

Measure it with free tools:

  • Google Search Console shows which searches your pages appear for, their position and their clicks. Watch project-name and locality searches first.
  • Google Business Profile insights show calls, website clicks and direction requests from Maps.
  • Your lead sheet or CRM should record “Google search” as a source, so you can count site visits and bookings from organic leads, not just traffic.

As with ads, the number that matters is site visits, not visitors.

Common real estate SEO mistakes

  • One page for every project. A single “Projects” page can't rank for five different project names.
  • Brochures only as PDFs or images. Google can't read the key details, and mobile visitors won't download them.
  • Deleting sold-out project pages instead of keeping or redirecting them.
  • Copying the developer's text onto a CP or broker site.
  • Buying cheap backlinks. They can do more harm than good. A few genuine local links are worth more.
  • Ignoring the Google Business Profile, which is often the fastest local win.
  • Expecting results in a month. Plan SEO alongside ads, not instead of them.

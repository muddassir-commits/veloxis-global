Real estate lead qualification means finding out, in the first one or two conversations, whether a buyer can buy, wants to buy, and when. Ask a short set of questions about purpose, budget, funding, configuration, location, timeline and who decides, in a natural order. Write the answers down, label the lead hot, warm, cold or not a fit, and you'll know who deserves a site visit this week.

This guide is for brokers, channel partners and developer sales teams in India. It gives the ten core questions with exact wording for calls and WhatsApp, how to ask about budget and home loans, extra questions for investors, plot buyers and NRIs, a simple scoring table, how to qualify before the call, and the questions never to ask.

Why does lead qualification matter?

Qualification matters because not every enquiry is a buyer, and serious buyers shouldn't wait while your team chases casual ones. Some people are curious, some are comparing prices for a relative, and some filled a form by mistake.

Qualifying is not a reason to delay the first call. In a study published in Harvard Business Review (Oldroyd, McElheran and Elkington, 2011), firms that contacted online leads within an hour were nearly seven times as likely to qualify the lead as firms that waited longer. Call quickly, and qualify during the call.

Good qualification also improves your ads. When you record why leads were not a fit (wrong budget, wrong location, just browsing), you can change the targeting, form or landing page so fewer of them arrive. Our real estate lead management guide shows how to track this.

What are the 10 core lead qualification questions?

These ten questions cover the classic BANT idea (budget, authority, need, timeline) in plain language for Indian real estate:

  1. Purpose: "Are you buying for your own family to live in, or as an investment?"
  2. Configuration: "What size are you looking for: 2 BHK, 3 BHK, a plot?"
  3. Location: "Which areas are you considering? What matters most: office, school, metro, family nearby?"
  4. Budget: "What budget range are you comfortable with, all-inclusive?"
  5. Funding: "Will you take a home loan? Have you checked your eligibility with a bank yet?"
  6. Timeline: "When are you planning to buy: this month, in the next three months, or later?"
  7. Possession need: "Do you need ready-to-move, or is under-construction fine?"
  8. Decision makers: "Who else will be part of the decision? Will they come for the visit too?"
  9. Current situation: "Are you renting now, or do you need to sell a property first?"
  10. What they've seen: "Have you visited other projects? What did you like or not like?"

You don't need all ten on the first call. Purpose, budget, timeline and decision makers tell you most of what you need; the rest can come over WhatsApp or at the site visit.

How do you ask about budget and home loans without putting buyers off?

Give a range and ask whether it fits, instead of asking "what is your budget?". Two things help.

Give ranges. "Most of our 3 BHK units are between {{price A}} and {{price B}}. Is that in your range, or should I show you the 2 BHK options too?" A range is easier to answer, and it shows you're trying to fit them, not push them.

Ask about the down payment, not only the loan. Banks don't lend the full price. The Reserve Bank of India's Master Circular on Housing Finance (1 April 2025) caps the loan-to-value ratio at 90% for loans up to ₹30 lakh, 80% for loans above ₹30 lakh and up to ₹75 lakh, and 75% above ₹75 lakh. The same circular tells banks not to count stamp duty and registration charges in the property cost for this calculation (except for homes costing up to ₹10 lakh). So the buyer pays the rest of the price, plus stamp duty and registration, from their own money.

A useful question: "For the down payment and registration, roughly how much are you planning to put in yourself?" A buyer who has thought about this is usually serious. One who hasn't may still be a good lead, just not for this month. Offering to connect them with a loan officer to check eligibility, with no obligation, is often the most helpful next step.

Which timeline and decision-maker questions work best?

Ask the timeline in a way that makes an honest answer easy:

"Some buyers want to book this month, some are just starting to look. Where are you right now?"

Then find out who decides. In many Indian families the decision includes a spouse, parents or a sibling helping with money. If the decision maker isn't at the visit, expect a second visit or a delay.

"Will {{your wife / your parents}} also be part of this decision? It's best if everyone sees the flat together, so I'll plan a time that works for all of you."

Finally ask what could stop them: "Is there anything that would stop you booking if you like the flat?" The answer (selling another property, waiting for a bonus, a job transfer) tells you exactly what to follow up on.

Extra questions for investors, plot buyers and NRIs

Different buyers need one or two extra questions on top of the core ten:

Buyer typeExtra questions
Investor"Are you looking for rental income or long-term growth?" "How long do you plan to hold it?" (Never promise returns.)
Plot buyer"Do you plan to build soon or hold the plot?" "What plot size and facing do you need?" "Do you need a corner or park-facing plot?"
NRI buyer"Which country are you in, and what time suits a video call?" "Will someone in India visit the site for you?" "Will you fund it from India or abroad?"
Upgrader (selling first)"Is your current home listed yet?" "Do you need the sale money before booking?"
First-time buyer"Have you spoken to a bank about eligibility?" "Would a ready-to-move home help you stop paying rent sooner?"

How should you score real estate leads?

Use four labels, the same in your CRM, sheet or WhatsApp Business labels:

LabelWhat it meansNext step
HotBudget and configuration fit, buying within about three months, funding clear, decision makers knownBook a site visit within days; senior salesperson handles it
WarmGood fit but later timeline, or loan or a sale still pendingUseful follow-ups; launch events or construction updates
ColdJust researching, budget far from your prices, or no reply after several triesOccasional updates only, if they agreed to receive them
Not a fitWrong location or product, or asked not to be contactedClose the lead and record the reason

Record the reason for every "not a fit". After a few weeks, those reasons tell you what to change. If most poor leads have a budget far below your prices, show the starting price range in the ad and on the landing page. How can you qualify leads before the first call?

You can collect some answers before anyone picks up the phone:

  • Lead forms: on Meta lead ads, add one or two multiple-choice questions, such as configuration and timeline. Meta's "Higher intent" form type adds a review step so people confirm their details before submitting (Meta Business Help: Instant Form types). Every extra question lowers the number of leads, so keep it short. Our Meta ads guide covers form setup.
  • Landing pages: a page that shows the price range, sizes and possession date answers the basics upfront, so those who enquire already know the project fits. See our landing page guide.
  • WhatsApp: the first reply can ask one qualifying question, such as "for yourself or as an investment?". Our real estate WhatsApp message templates include first replies that do this.

Which questions should you never ask a home buyer?

  • Religion, caste, community or diet. These have nothing to do with whether someone can buy a home. Asking is wrong, offends good buyers and damages your brand.
  • Exact salary or bank balance on the first call. Ask about the budget range and loan plan; the bank checks the details.
  • Pressure questions such as "Can you pay the booking amount today?" on a first call.
  • Leading questions about returns. Never ask "Do you want guaranteed returns?" or promise appreciation. Agents must not make false or misleading claims under Section 10 of the RERA Act, 2016.

Putting it together: a sample first call

Here is how the questions flow in a two-to-three-minute call:

"Hi {{name}}, this is {{your name}} from {{company}}. You asked about {{project}} a few minutes ago. Is this a good time for two minutes?
Great. Are you looking for your own family or as an investment?
And which size: 2 BHK or 3 BHK?
Our 3 BHKs are between {{price A}} and {{price B}}, all-inclusive. Does that work for you?
When are you planning to buy?
Who else will be part of the decision?
Then let's plan a visit when everyone can come. Would Saturday or Sunday be better?"

Write the answers down straight after the call, add the label, and send a WhatsApp message confirming what you agreed. Our real estate sales scripts continue into the site visit and follow-up, and how to book real estate site visits covers confirmations and no-shows.

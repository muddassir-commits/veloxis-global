An abandoned cart email sequence is a short automated series, usually two or three messages, sent to shoppers who left items in their cart or checkout. Send the first reminder within a few hours with no discount, a helpful follow-up about a day later, and an optional final nudge. Stop it the moment they order.

This guide is for e-commerce and D2C founders and in-house marketers, especially stores in India running Shopify with cash on delivery, prepaid and WhatsApp in the mix. Most pages that rank for "abandoned cart email sequence" are written by email tool makers. They share their own benchmark tables, push a discount in every email and ignore that many Indian shoppers check out with only a phone number. This guide covers what a sequence is, the difference between cart and checkout abandonment, how many emails to send and when, what each email should say, when to offer a discount, how to add WhatsApp and SMS without spamming, the consent rules that apply in India, and how to measure whether the sequence earns its keep.

Platform settings, policies and laws change. The Shopify, Klaviyo, Google, WhatsApp and Indian rules below were checked on 20 November 2026. This is general information, not legal advice.

What is an abandoned cart email sequence?

It is a set of automated emails triggered when a known shopper adds products to a cart or starts checkout and then leaves without paying. Each email links straight back to their cart so they can finish the order in a click or two.

The sequence only works for shoppers you can identify. That means someone who is logged in, has given you their email earlier (for example through a sign-up form), or typed their email into the first step of checkout. An anonymous visitor who never shared contact details cannot be emailed at all; for them, the tools are retargeting ads and a better store.

The problem it solves is large. The Baymard Institute calculates an average documented cart abandonment rate of 70.22%, based on 50 studies. In other words, most carts are never turned into orders. Some of those shoppers were never going to buy; some just got distracted. The sequence is for the second group.

What is the difference between abandoned cart, abandoned checkout and browse abandonment?

They are three different moments in the funnel, and each deserves its own message. The deeper the shopper got, the stronger their intent and the more direct your reminder can be.

Shopify's help page on marketing automations describes all three: product browse (a customer viewed at least one product but did not add it to a cart), abandoned cart (added items but did not start checkout) and abandoned checkout (started checkout but did not finish the order). Klaviyo's guide to creating an abandoned cart flow makes the same split between an "Added to Cart" trigger and a "Started Checkout" trigger, and notes that someone who starts checkout shows stronger purchase intent than someone who only added an item.

FlowWhat triggers itIntentTone of the first message
Browse abandonmentViewed a product, did not add to cartLowLight: "still thinking about this?" plus help choosing
Abandoned cartAdded to cart, did not start checkoutMediumReminder of the items, answers to common doubts
Abandoned checkoutEntered contact details at checkout, did not payHighDirect: "your order is waiting", one-click return link

Run them as separate flows and make sure one shopper does not get messages from two of them at once. Klaviyo's suggested way is to add a filter to the Added to Cart flow so that anyone who later starts checkout leaves it and only receives the checkout flow.

A note on Shopify's own definition: Shopify counts a checkout as abandoned once it has stayed incomplete for more than ten minutes after the customer gave an email address, according to its help page on recovering abandoned checkouts. That is when the clock starts for any delay you set.

How many emails should an abandoned cart sequence have, and when should they go out?

Two or three emails is the usual recommendation, starting a few hours after the shopper leaves. Klaviyo advises sending two to three abandoned cart messages, with the first two to four hours after someone starts a checkout and a second 20 to 48 hours later (Klaviyo Help Center).

Some articles recommend six or more emails over a week. More messages mean more chances to annoy people, more unsubscribes and more spam complaints, which hurt the delivery of every other email you send. Start with three, look at the numbers for each email, and add or remove one only if your own data says so.

Here is a starting schedule to test. The timings follow Klaviyo's guidance above; adjust them to your store.

MessageWhenJobDiscount?

Email 3: last nudge (optional)A few days after the shopper leftRemind once more, offer help choosing, or a modest incentive if your margins allowOptional, never automatic for everyone

Timing also depends on what you sell. A shopper weighing an expensive product may need more time than one buying a refill, so it can make sense to wait longer before email 2 for high-value carts. Test one change at a time.

What should each abandoned cart email say?

Each email should do one job: remind, reassure or nudge, with the cart contents visible and a single clear button back to checkout. Nothing else in the email should compete with that button.

Email 1: the reminder

  • Subject line: plain and specific, for example "You left something in your cart" or "Your [product name] is still in your cart".
  • Body: product image, name, variant (size, colour) and price as it was in the cart.
  • Button: "Return to your cart" or "Complete your order", linking to the saved cart or checkout.
  • Help line: a reply-to address someone reads and, if you offer it, a WhatsApp link for questions.

Email 2: the reassurance

This email answers the reasons people hesitate. Baymard's survey on reasons for abandonment during checkout lists extra costs (shipping, tax, fees) as the top reason at 40%, followed by slow delivery and not trusting the site with card details. So email 2 should state, in plain words:

  • The delivery charge (or that delivery is free, if it is) and the expected delivery time to the shopper's area.
  • Your return and exchange policy in one or two lines, linking to the full policy.
  • The payment options you offer, such as UPI, cards, net banking and cash on delivery if available.
  • Real customer reviews, only if they are genuine and you can show where they came from. Never invent them.

Email 3: the last nudge

Keep it short. Remind them once more, offer help ("Not sure about the size? Reply to this email"), or suggest a close alternative if the item is low in stock. Only mention stock or a deadline if it is true: India's Guidelines for Prevention and Regulation of Dark Patterns, 2023 list "false urgency" (falsely suggesting limited quantity or time to rush a purchase) as a dark pattern. "Only 2 left" must mean only two are left.

Should abandoned cart emails include a discount?

Not in the first email, and not automatically for everyone. If every abandoned cart gets a coupon, regular customers learn to leave their cart and wait for one, and you give away margin on orders that would have happened anyway.

A safer approach:

  1. Send emails 1 and 2 with no discount and see how many carts they recover on their own.
  2. If you add an incentive, put it only in the last email, and only for first-time buyers or carts above a value where your margin can absorb it.
  3. Make it a single-use code that expires, and say the real expiry date.
  4. Prefer non-price incentives where they suit you, such as free delivery or a free sample.
  5. Check the effect on profit, not only on recovered revenue. Our guide to why Meta and Shopify numbers differ explains why store data is the number to trust.

Whatever you offer, show the final price honestly. The same dark patterns guidelines also cover drip pricing (revealing charges late) and basket sneaking (adding items or charges to the cart without the shopper's consent).

What changes for Indian stores with COD and phone-number checkouts?

Many Indian shoppers give a phone number instead of an email at checkout, so an email-only sequence misses them. Shopify's help page says recovery emails are not sent when the customer entered a phone number instead of an email address (Shopify Help Center).

That has three practical consequences:

  • Collect both where you can. Ask for a phone number for delivery and offer an optional email for order updates. Explain why you need each one.
  • Add WhatsApp or SMS for phone-only checkouts, with the shopper's opt-in. Shopify's automations support email, SMS and WhatsApp for all three abandonment flows, though SMS and WhatsApp messages are billed separately (Shopify Help Center).
  • Check third-party checkouts. If you use a one-click checkout app instead of Shopify's own checkout, Shopify's built-in recovery may not fire. Shopify notes that abandoned checkout recovery is available only for the Online Store and Buy Button sales channels. Ask your checkout provider how abandonment events reach your email or WhatsApp tool.

Cash on delivery adds one more point. A recovered COD order is not money in the bank until it is delivered and paid. Track recovered orders through to delivery, and avoid incentives that attract shoppers who are likely to refuse the parcel. How do you add WhatsApp and SMS to an abandoned cart sequence?

Use WhatsApp or SMS for one message in the sequence, for shoppers who opted in, and let email carry the rest. Messaging channels feel personal, so one well-timed message works better than a stream of them.

The rules for WhatsApp are specific:

  • Opt-in first. The WhatsApp Business Messaging Policy says you may contact people only if they gave you their number and you received opt-in permission from them. You must also respect every request to stop messages, whether it is made on WhatsApp or elsewhere.
  • Templates outside the 24-hour window. Meta's WhatsApp pricing documentation explains that a business can send free-form messages only within 24 hours of the customer's last message. Otherwise it must use an approved template.
  • Cart reminders are marketing. Meta's template category guidelines give "You left items in your cart" as an example of a retargeting message, which falls under the marketing category, even when the user asked for it. Shopify also says its SMS and WhatsApp automations can only go to customers subscribed to marketing.

A sensible mixed sequence for an Indian store:

StepShopper gave emailShopper gave phone only (opted in to WhatsApp)
First reminderEmail 1One WhatsApp template with the cart link
ReassuranceEmail 2Reply to questions if they write back (inside the 24-hour window)
Last nudgeEmail 3 (optional)Usually nothing more; do not chase on WhatsApp

If someone replies on WhatsApp with a question, that is the real opportunity: answer quickly, or let a well-scoped bot answer common questions and hand over to a person. Our guide to WhatsApp AI chatbots for business covers how to set that up without losing the human touch. For SMS, commercial messages in India fall under the TRAI commercial communications regulations, so register as a sender through your SMS provider before you switch SMS on.

What consent and deliverability rules apply to cart emails?

Treat cart recovery as marketing: get clear consent, make unsubscribing easy and keep complaints low. That keeps you on the right side of Indian law and keeps your emails out of spam folders.

  • Shopify's default. Shopify's marketing automations send only to customers subscribed to marketing by default. For abandoned checkout emails you can change the audience to all customers, but you cannot for SMS or WhatsApp (Shopify Help Center). Decide this with the consent point below in mind.
  • India's DPDP Rules. The government notified the Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025 in November 2025, with a phased compliance period. Under the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, consent must be for a specified purpose and it must be as easy to withdraw as to give. In practice: say clearly at checkout that you may send cart and marketing reminders, and put a working unsubscribe link in every email.
  • Gmail's sender requirements. Google requires anyone sending 5,000 or more messages a day to Gmail accounts to set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC, support one-click unsubscribe for marketing messages and keep the spam rate reported in Postmaster Tools below 0.30%, ideally below 0.10% (Google Workspace Admin Help). Even smaller senders benefit from the same setup.

Other basics: send from your own domain, use a sender name shoppers recognise, and never buy email lists.

How do you set up an abandoned cart sequence on Shopify or Klaviyo?

Turn on one abandonment flow per stage in a single tool, set the delays, write the messages and add an exit rule so buyers stop receiving reminders. The steps below work for Shopify's built-in automations and for tools such as Klaviyo.

  1. Choose one tool for flows. If you move to a dedicated email tool, switch off Shopify's built-in abandoned checkout email so shoppers do not get two reminders.
  2. Check the trigger data. Make sure the tool receives "added to cart" and "started checkout" events from your store and from any third-party checkout.
  3. Build the checkout flow first. It has the highest intent. In Shopify, go to the marketing automations area and open the abandoned checkout automation; in Klaviyo, use the Started Checkout trigger.
  4. Set the delays, starting from the schedule in the table above.
  5. Add the exit rule. In Klaviyo the filter is "Placed Order zero times since starting this flow", which Klaviyo checks before each message, so anyone who buys is skipped (Klaviyo Help Center).
  6. Write and test the emails on a phone, with a real test order, and check the cart link restores the right items and variants.
  7. Add the cart and browse flows once the checkout flow works, with a filter so nobody gets two flows at once.
  8. Add WhatsApp or SMS for opted-in, phone-only shoppers last.

How do you measure whether your abandoned cart sequence works?

Measure recovered orders and profit per message, not just opens. Privacy features in some email apps distort open tracking, so clicks and orders tell you more.

MetricWhat it tells youWatch out for
Recovered orders per emailWhich message actually brings shoppers backOrders that would have happened anyway
Click rateWhether the subject, content and button workClicks without orders point to a checkout problem
Unsubscribe and spam complaint rateWhether you are sending too much or too soonRising complaints damage all your email
Discount costWhat you gave away to recover ordersCoupons used by people who would have paid full price
Delivered, paid orders (COD)Real recovered revenueRecovered COD orders that are refused at the door

To know how many orders the sequence truly adds, hold out a small random group of shoppers who receive no reminders and compare their purchase rate with the group that does. Many email tools support this kind of split test. Then improve one thing at a time: timing, subject line, the reassurance content or the incentive.

Look at the whole funnel too. If many shoppers abandon at the same step, the fix may be on the site, not in the inbox. Baymard's research points to extra costs, slow delivery and trust as leading reasons, and those are best fixed on the product and checkout pages.

What are the most common abandoned cart email mistakes?

The biggest mistakes are sending too many messages, discounting too early, faking urgency and forgetting to stop the flow after a purchase. Each one costs you money or trust.

  • Two tools sending reminders for the same cart.
  • No exit rule, so a customer who just ordered gets "you forgot something".
  • A cart link that opens an empty cart or the home page.
  • A coupon in email 1 that trains customers to abandon on purpose.
  • "Only a few left" or countdown timers that are not true.
  • Hiding delivery charges until the last step, then reminding people of a cart total they never saw.
  • Messaging phone-only shoppers on WhatsApp without opt-in.
  • Judging the flow on open rates instead of delivered orders.

An abandoned cart sequence works best on top of a store that already answers shoppers' doubts and a consent setup you can stand behind.

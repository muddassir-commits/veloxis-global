What social media actually does for real estate

Buyers rarely see one post and book a flat. Buying a home takes months, often with the whole family involved. Social media does three jobs along the way:

  • Proof. Before a buyer calls, they look you up. An active Instagram profile or Facebook page with real site photos, construction updates and faces of the team tells them you exist and the project is moving.
  • Memory. A buyer who enquired three months ago and went quiet keeps seeing your posts. When they are ready, you are the name they remember.
  • Warm audiences for ads. People who watch your videos or visit your profile can be retargeted with paid ads. Organic posts make your ads cheaper and better targeted.

What organic social media rarely does on its own is produce a steady flow of enquiries. Reach for business pages is limited, and most followers are neighbours, friends and other brokers. Set up your real estate Facebook page and Instagram profile

A buyer who finds your profile decides in a few seconds whether you look real. Get these basics right before you post anything:

  1. Name and handle. Use the brand or project name buyers will search for. Avoid “official_realty_9876” style handles.
  2. Profile photo. A clean logo for a company, a clear face for an individual broker.
  3. Bio. What you sell, where, and for whom, in one line. For example: “2 and 3 BHK homes in Sector 150, Noida. Site visits every day.”
  4. RERA details. Add the project or agent RERA registration number and the state authority’s website. Buyers check, and so do regulators.
  5. One clear action. A WhatsApp button or a link to a project landing page, not a list of ten links.
  6. Pinned posts or highlights. Location, floor plans, construction updates and FAQs, so a new visitor gets answers without scrolling.
  7. Business accounts. Switch Instagram to a professional account and connect it to your Facebook page in Meta Business Suite. That gives you insights, one inbox for both platforms and the option to run ads later.

How to make a real estate social media post that gets watched

Each real estate social media post has about one second to stop the scroll. A few rules make a big difference:

  • Lead with the answer. Open with the price band, configuration or locality on screen, not with the logo. “3 BHK, 1,450 sq ft carpet, 8 minutes from the metro” beats “Welcome to a new lifestyle.”
  • Film real places. Phone footage of the actual site, sample flat and road often gets more trust than a render, because buyers can tell it’s real. Use renders only where the building doesn’t exist yet, and label them as artistic impressions.
  • Vertical first. Shoot Reels and stories in 9:16, and carousels in 4:5. Most buyers will watch on a phone.
  • Add text on screen. Many people watch with the sound off. Put the key facts and a caption on the video itself.
  • Use carousels to explain. A 6 to 8 slide carousel is ideal for a floor plan walkthrough, a cost breakdown or a “5 questions to ask before booking” list. People save and share these.
  • End with one action. “Comment PLAN for the floor plans” or “Tap the WhatsApp button to book a Sunday visit.” One ask per post.

The 5-3-1 rule and a simple weekly calendar

People often ask what the 5-3-1 rule is. It is an informal rule of thumb for social media, not an official standard, and you will see different versions of it. The version that suits real estate works like this: out of every nine posts, five give value (education, location guides, answers to questions), three build connection (people, progress, behind the scenes) and one sells (a launch, an offer, a site visit weekend). The point is balance. An account that only sells gets ignored, and an account that never sells doesn’t get enquiries.

A realistic weekly calendar for a small team looks like this:

DayPostRule bucket
MondayCarousel: one buyer question answered (for example, carpet vs super area)Value

FridayReel: locality, drive time or amenity tourValue
SaturdayPost and stories: site visit weekend, with the RERA numberSell

Three good posts a week, every week, will do more than a burst of ten posts in launch month followed by silence. Batch-film on one day a month at the site, and schedule posts in Meta Business Suite so the calendar doesn’t depend on anyone remembering.

RERA rules apply to social media too

Under Section 11(2) of the RERA Act, any advertisement issued by a promoter must carry the project’s registration number and the website of the state authority. A Reel promoting a project, a Facebook post about a launch and a WhatsApp creative are all advertisements. Put the number on the creative itself, not only in the caption, because images and videos get forwarded without their captions. Some state authorities also ask for a QR code linking to the project page.

A few other rules keep you out of trouble:

  • Never promote a project that isn’t registered with RERA, unless it clearly falls under an exemption.
  • Mark renders and sample flat photos as artistic impressions.
  • Don’t promise returns, rental yields or appreciation you can’t back with a document.
  • Use buyer photos and names only with written permission.

This is general information, not legal advice. Check your state authority’s orders for any extra requirements.

Turn comments and DMs into site visits

This is where most accounts lose the leads they already have. Someone comments “price?” under a Reel and gets a reply the next afternoon, if at all. By then they have messaged three other projects.

  • Reply fast, everywhere. Comments, DMs, story replies and Facebook messages all land in the Meta Business Suite inbox. Assign one person to check it through the day.
  • Move the chat to WhatsApp. Reply publicly with a short answer, then send the price sheet and floor plans on WhatsApp, where you can follow up properly.
  • Ask two qualifying questions. Budget and timeline, or configuration and preferred visit day. Then offer a specific visit slot.
  • Log every lead. Record which post or Reel it came from, so you know what content actually brings buyers.

Speed matters more than anything else here. WhatsApp automation for real estate leads can send the brochure, price sheet and a visit slot within seconds, even at 11pm, and hand the conversation to a person when the buyer is ready.

What to measure

Likes and follower counts feel good, but they don’t tell you whether social media is helping sell homes. Track these instead, once a month:

  • Enquiries from social: DMs, comments asking for details, and WhatsApp chats that started from a post or profile.
  • Site visits from social: the number that actually matters.
  • Saves and shares: a sign that content is useful enough to send to a spouse or parent.
  • Profile visits and link clicks: whether posts make people want to know more.
  • Which posts led to enquiries: then make more of those.

Once you know which content brings enquiries, put paid budget behind it.
